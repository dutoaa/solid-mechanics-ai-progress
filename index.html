SSF-Net: A Spatial-Spectral Fusion Network for Accurate PDE Operator Learning

Siqi Zhao, Flora D. Salim

Machine learning methods for solving partial differential equations (PDEs) significantly improve computational efficiency over traditional numerical solvers such as finite element method (FEM). However, PDE dynamics inherently span both local and global dynamics: disturbances propagate and decay gradually through the domain, while global coupling effects can influence the whole system simultaneously. Capturing both types of dynamics with a low complexity model remains a key challenge. To address this, we propose the Spatial-Spectral Fusion Network (SSF-Net), built around a dual-branch Spatial-Spectral Fusion (SSF) block comprising a spatial branch and a spectral branch. The spatial branch employs a multi-directional Mamba, which mitigates its inherent causal, single-direction scanning bias and capture local interactions with reduced directional bias at linear complexity. The spectral branch leverages a Fourier transform with a block-diagonal MLP to efficiently capture global spectral features with reduced parameter count and complexity. The SSF block is embedded in a multiresolution convolutional encoder-decoder, which extracts multi-scale features. We evaluate SSF-Net on a comprehensive PDE benchmark spanning multiple task types and assess its long-horizon stability. Compared with attention-based and convolution-based baselines, SSF-Net achieves lower prediction error with fewer parameters while retaining near-linear asymptotic complexity in the number of spatial tokens.

Mathematical analysis and vectorized FEM implementation of logarithmic strain space framework for finite strains: a case study on elastoplasticity

Miroslav Frost, Martin Kružík, Alexej Moskovka, Jan Valdman

We investigate the mathematical and computational aspects of the logarithmic strain space framework for finite-strain models in nonlinear continuum mechanics, focusing on the classical von Mises elastoplastic model with linear kinematic hardening. To overcome the lack of coercivity and lower-semicontinuity in the quadratic Hencky energy, we introduce higher-gradient regularization and prove the existence of energetic solutions for rate-independent quasistatic evolution under time-dependent Dirichlet loading. Complementarily, we develop a vectorized MATLAB finite-element implementation of the framework leveraging page-wise matrix functions and operations, and our custom vectorization library. Computational benchmarks show that local tensor operations are significantly accelerated, the logarithmic-strain approach does not substantially increase computational costs, and the implementation robustly handles large rotations and deformations.

Finite-strain second-gradient elasticity with an energetic surface: existence of minimizers, incremental theory and elastocapillary beading

Michal Šmejkal, Martin Horák, Martin Kružík

We develop a finite-strain theory of second-gradient elasticity for solids with a material surface endowed with its own constitutive energy. The bulk second gradient plays two complementary roles. First, under suitable coercivity assumptions and convexity with respect to the second gradient, we prove the existence of minimizers for nonnegative, lower semicontinuous surface energy densities, without requiring convexity, polyconvexity, positive one-homogeneity, or an upper growth bound on the surface energy. Second, it introduces an intrinsic bulk length scale that affects the onset of instability and wavelength selection. To investigate these effects, we derive an incremental small-on-large formulation in the finitely deformed configuration and apply it to the elastocapillary beading of an axially stretched soft cylinder. For an infinite cylinder and the parameters considered, increasing the second-gradient modulus delays bifurcation and shifts the critical mode towards longer wavelengths. Above a finite threshold of this modulus, the critical stretch saturates in the long-wave limit. The model is implemented in the open-source finite element code OOFEM using C1-continuous isogeometric analysis. Finite-cylinder simulations yield onset stretches in close agreement with the analytical predictions. Selected post-bifurcation branches traced by arc-length continuation exhibit beaded states and supercritical bifurcation at small second-gradient moduli, whereas larger moduli are associated with subcritical bifurcation and the formation of distinct thick and thin segments.

PhyCAD: Physics-Aware Text-to-CAD Generation via Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning

Yuxin Cui, Weijie Zheng, Zhengyang Li, Han Wang, Wenjie Feng, Qiyu Kang

Language-driven computer-aided design (CAD) generation enables the creation of editable parametric models from text, yet geometric correctness alone does not ensure physical feasibility. We present PhyCAD, a framework for physics-aware Text-to-CAD generation that trains language models to produce executable CadQuery programs under geometric and physical constraints. To support this setting, we construct a Text-to-CAD dataset that links parametric CAD models with geometric specifications, loading conditions, and physical constraints. We further develop an automated CAD--CAE verifier that combines program execution, geometric checks, and finite element analysis to provide feedback for model training. A key learning challenge is to prioritize joint satisfaction of geometric and physical constraints while retaining informative feedback on partial progress. A flat aggregation of heterogeneous rewards can obscure this priority, allowing improvements in individual criteria to compensate for unmet design requirements. We therefore introduce a Hierarchical Balanced Advantage for Group Relative Policy Optimization, grouping candidates into levels according to their verification status and combining between-level progress with objective-wise peer ranking within each level. This formulation preserves cross-level priority in the training signal while providing fine-grained feedback for satisfying constraints and improving feasible designs. Experiments across multiple language models show that PhyCAD consistently improves joint geometric and physical constraint satisfaction. These results demonstrate the value of verifiable engineering feedback for advancing language-driven CAD generation beyond geometric reconstruction toward physics-aware design.

Self-Supervised Learning of Universal Latent Spaces for Operator Learning

Sepehr Mousavi, Levi Lingsch, Siddhartha Mishra, Laura De Lorenzis

Neural operators fit their representation of functions to the task they are trained on, so every new task rebuilds a reduced space from labeled data. Following the self-supervised pre-training that made representations shareable in natural language processing and computer vision, we decouple representation learning from operator learning. A functional autoencoder is pre-trained on analytic functions synthesized on the fly, with no solver and no labels, to represent functions on arbitrary point clouds, and the latent space it induces is frozen and reused across tasks. The representation is exactly equivariant to translation, dilation, and amplitude, and reconstructs solutions of partial differential equations (PDEs) and natural images on geometries it never saw. Being task-agnostic, the latent space can be interpreted in its own right. The whole space is used and decodes to valid fields, it preserves the ordering of distances between physical fields, its geodesics interpolate physical evolutions, and edit directions transfer across fields. With frozen representation maps, a dataset is encoded once and a neural operator is trained entirely on cached embeddings, which saves memory and training time. On 28 tasks in fluid dynamics, solid mechanics, acoustics, and the Helmholtz and Poisson equations, this paradigm can exceed the accuracy of state-of-the-art end-to-end architectures, with up to an order of magnitude higher throughput and memory savings that fit models nearly two orders of magnitude larger on consumer-grade GPUs. At inference, latent rollouts run up to an order of magnitude faster than regular rollouts, and chunked inference processes functions of over half a billion points on a single consumer-grade GPU.

A Tensor-Network Chebyshev-Galerkin Method for Boundary-Value Problems in Computational Mechanics

Juan José Rodríguez-Aldavero, Ángel de la Rosa Velasco, Juan José García-Ripoll

Quantum-inspired numerical methods for partial differential equations represent discretized fields and operators as quantics tensor trains (QTT), so that their cost depends on how compressible the problem is rather than on the number of unknowns. Global spectral formulations in this setting have so far relied on Fourier bases, restricted to periodic problems, or on collocation, which does not preserve the variational structure exploited by tensor-network solvers. We introduce a QTT Chebyshev-Galerkin method that assembles and solves global spectral systems directly in compressed form, without forming the underlying arrays. Starting from the weak formulation, the method handles coupled elliptic systems with variable coefficients, smoothly mapped geometries, and mixed Dirichlet-Robin boundary conditions, and yields symmetric positive-definite systems suited to variational tensor-network solvers. Parameters and random inputs enter as additional coordinates, so the same construction returns the solution over an entire parameter space in a single solve. In linear elasticity, the method retains spectral convergence and reaches errors below $10^{-8}$ on a three-dimensional benchmark, beyond those attained by QTT finite differences and Q2/Q4 FEniCSx finite elements over the tested range. A six-parameter cantilever design study covering $1.7\times10^{7}$ designs is solved in a single 92s run, and a stochastic problem with ten random coordinates is represented with $2.4\times10^{6}$ times less memory than its full coefficient array.

Conclusion: FEM-derived elastographic parameters showed potential for distinguishing CKD from healthy tissue and may provide an alternative for renal biomechanical assessment without dedicated MRE acquisition.

CloakFEM: Agentic FEM Code Generation for Red-Teaming Computational Resource Exhaustion

Li Zheng, Simin Chen

Finite element method (FEM) solvers are foundational to scientific and engineering simulation, yet their susceptibility to \emph{efficiency attacks} remains largely unexplored. An adversary can craft syntactically valid and runnable FEM programs that complete successfully while forcing excessive computational work, enabling computational denial-of-service (DoS) attacks. Finding such inputs is challenging because FEM programs occupy a vast, structured space constrained by numerical, geometric, and constitutive requirements. We introduce CloakFEM, a domain-guided LLM-agent framework for automatically generating runnable adversarial FEM programs that amplify solver computation. CloakFEM combines LLM-guided search with an FEM-specific intermediate representation and a deterministic validity gate that enforces runnable constraints. Across three FEM stacks, 17 problem classes, and 1,068 seeds, CloakFEM discovers adversarial inputs that substantially amplify solver work while remaining valid and solvable. In the most extreme case, solver iterations increase from 43 to 247,367, a \(5{,}752.72\times\) amplification. CloakFEM outperforms five generic black-box baselines and retains an advantage over evolutionary search under a controlled bounded action space. Cross-solver analysis further reveals both problem-intrinsic and solver-specific sources of vulnerability. Our results uncover an underexplored efficiency-attack surface in FEM solvers and show that runnable FEM programs can serve as adversarial inputs.

In this work, we develop a hybrid quantum-classical coarse-to-fine finite element framework for heterogeneous Darcy flow. We investigate whether a measured quantum coarse solution improves fine-grid initialization and reduces subsequent classical solver work. The effect on classical solver iterations depends on the problem. All quantum computations were simulated using Qiskit Aer.

Geometric Multigrid Preconditioners for conforming Finite Element Methods on Arbitrarily Agglomerated Meshes

Santiago Badia, Jordi Manyer

Geometric multigrid methods for conforming finite element discretisations classically require a nested mesh hierarchy, forcing coarse meshes to be conforming, which is unsuitable for general domains. In order to eliminate this geometric constraint, we introduce and analyse a family of multilevel auxiliary space preconditioners. At the first level, a conforming Lagrangian discretisation on a fine simplicial mesh is preconditioned by a non-conforming polytopal discretisation posed on an arbitrarily agglomerated coarse mesh. At the subsequent levels, the coarse problem is solved by a geometric multigrid method for that polytopal discretisation on further agglomerated meshes, so that the whole hierarchy is generated from the fine mesh by agglomeration alone. We consider two polytopal discretisations, a symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method and a hybrid high-order method. The prolongation operator at the first level is an element-wise interpolation onto the fine mesh followed by nodal averaging, preceded in the hybrid high-order case by a local potential reconstruction. For point-Jacobi and vertex-star patch additive Schwarz smoothers, we prove that the condition number of the preconditioned system is bounded by $O((H/h)^3)$, where $H/h$ is the coarsening ratio, and by $O((H/h)^2)$ for the DG variant with penalty proportional to $H/h$. Numerical experiments in two and three dimensions, on structured and unstructured meshes with agglomerates generated by automatic clustering algorithms, confirm the robustness of the preconditioners.

Mass-lumped finite elements with maximum-bound preservation and original-energy dissipation for the degenerate-mobility Allen-Cahn equation

Himanshu Kumar Dwivedi, Matthias Ehrhardt

In this work, we propose a mass-lumped finite element method for the Allen-Cahn equation that incorporates nonlinear mobility which may degenerate. The spatial approximation uses continuous, piecewise linear, mass-lumped finite elements, while convex splitting is employed for the double-well potential in time. Under the standard non-obtuse mesh condition, it is shown that the resulting fully discrete method preserves the maximum-bound principle unconditionally: if the initial approximation takes values in $[-1, 1]$, then so does the numerical solution at every time level. Additionally, the method dissipates the original discrete Ginzburg-Landau energy without any time-step restriction, even when the mobility vanishes at pure phases. This analysis relies on the monotonicity of the lumped finite element operator and the convexity of the split potential. Numerical experiments confirm these theoretical properties and demonstrate the robustness of the method in phase-separation dynamics involving degenerate mobility.

Locating Contact on Tendon-Driven Continuum Robots From Tendon Measurements Alone via Constrained Moving Horizon Estimation

Haitham El-Hussieny

Tendon-driven continuum robots can reach through constrained spaces, but contact along their bodies is invisible to the sensors at their motors. We show that this is a structural limitation rather than a tuning problem: the map from contact forces to tendon measurements has a null space of dimension at least seven, so no estimator that leaves the contact support free can locate contact. When the obstacle geometry is known, however, non-penetration and complementarity make the contact force locally identifiable, and we prove that the resulting system with unknown contact inputs is locally exponentially strongly detectable. We present a constrained moving horizon estimator (MHE) that encodes these conditions and runs in real time as one quadratic program per sample, and we give a local, mode-wise robust stability result for it. In a finite- element benchmark of a two-section robot pressing and sliding on a wall, the estimator located contact to 0.9 mm and estimated contact force to 0.22 N, compared with 65 mm and 0.72 N for a tuned extended Kalman filter (EKF) with state projection. The estimator tolerated millimeter- level errors in the obstacle position and unmodeled wall friction, under which the projected EKF failed. Under randomized model mismatch over 40 trials, including unmodeled tendon friction, it remained better in force and location in every trial. Without contact, the MHE offered no advantage over the EKF, which confirms that its benefit comes from the contact constraints. This report extends a letter submitted to IEEE Control Systems Letters with complete proofs and additional experiments.

We report a modified optical floating-zone method in which growth is performed inside a transparent sapphire crucible, stabilising the molten zone while preserving optical heating and visual monitoring. These results show that deviations from ideal Scheil--Pfann behaviour arise from the evolving molten-zone geometry and solidification conditions. The experimentally constrained model therefore provides a practical means of reconstructing otherwise inaccessible growth conditions and a basis for optimisation of sapphire-confined floating-zone growth of incongruently melting alloys.
