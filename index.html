hp-Adaptive Finite Element Modeling of Static Cracks in Density-Dependent Media

Vicente Valle Martinez, Saugata Ghosh, S. M. Mallikarjunaiah, Dambaru Bhatta

The elastostatic crack-tip fields within a density-dependent continuum are numerically investigated under several macroscopic loading configurations. The governing nonlinear boundary value problem is formulated using an algebraically nonlinear hyperelastic constitutive relationship in which the material moduli are intrinsically coupled to volumetric strain. Existence and uniqueness of the weak solution to the nonlinear displacement-based variational formulation are rigorously established at the continuous level via the Minty-Browder theorem. The variational formulation is then linearized at the partial differential equation level to construct a continuous damped Newton-Raphson scheme. To accurately capture the severe field gradients and internal singularities inherent to this model, the resulting sequence of linear operator equations is discretized and solved via a conforming finite element method within an $hp$-adaptive framework. A comparative analysis is conducted across five distinct boundary loading profiles (encompassing uniform, piecewise-slope, and concentrated point loads) applied to a stationary cracked geometry. Within each configuration, the density-dependent constitutive parameter is systematically varied to assess its regulatory impact on the fracture process zone, focusing on the spatial evolution of normal stress, normal strain, and strain energy density near the crack tip. Numerical results demonstrate that local kinematic deformability is strictly bounded by the strain-limiting nature of the continuum, whereby static equilibrium is sustained through severe, localized stress amplifications. Furthermore, the mechanical influence of the modeling parameter is fundamentally dictated by the topological interplay between internal crack geometry and the spatial distribution of applied macroscopic loads.

Large-deformation constitutive modeling of viscoelastic foams: Application to an open-cell foam material

Jialiang Tao, Alexander K. Landauer, Zicheng Yan, Xiuqi Li, Christian Franck, David L Henann

Open-cell elastomeric foams are widely utilized for impact protection and cushioning applications. These foams can demonstrate remarkable elastic compressibility and rate sensitivity over a wide range of force and acceleration protection levels. They often exhibit volumetric-distortional coupling in their mechanical response and potentially compaction banding when subjected to dynamic loads. Here, a phenomenological modeling framework, adopted previously for closed-cell foams, is specified using an experimental characterization-based approach for calibrating and validating the large-deformation, viscoelastic material model. Homogeneous simple compression and tension experiments were performed on isotropic, open-cell viscoelastic foam materials, and stress-strain and lateral-axial strain data were used for model calibration. Data were collected at decadal strain rates to inform the rate-sensitive aspects of the model. The modeling approach involved decomposing the total material response into an equilibrium hyperelastic contribution and several nonlinear, Maxwell-like contributions and utilized invariants of the logarithmic strain to account for large deformations and the strong volumetric-distortional coupling effect. A new strain localization detection algorithm was developed and used to check for the formation of compaction bands, which would invalidate modeling assumptions for the calibration experiments. To validate the predictive capability of the constitutive model, experiments were conducted that involved intentionally inhomogeneous deformations over a spectrum of strain rates and deformation types. The consistency between the measured responses from the validation experiments and the model predictions obtained from finite-element-based numerical simulations was largely within the experimental uncertainty bounds, demonstrating the predictive capability of the model.

Physics-Informed Fusion-MIONet with IFENN: A Generalized Framework for Coupled Poroelasticity Problems

Fouad M. Amin, Rigoberto Moncada, Panos Pantidis, Diab W. Abueidda, Fan Fei, Mostafa E. Mobasher

Complex, high-dimensional, large-scale poroelasticity simulations using traditional numerical solvers can be prohibitively expensive in terms of computational resources. We introduce an innovative framework for poroelasticity problems that integrates physics-informed machine learning (PI-ML) models with high-performance FEM solvers. This integration is achieved through the Integrated Finite Element Neural Network (IFENN) setup, which uses a staggered scheme between an FEM solver for mechanical equilibrium and a PI-ML model for fluid-flow predictions. We introduce a novel Physics-Informed Fusion-MIONet (PIF-MIONet) architecture, in which a fusion network encodes the outputs of the MIONet branches. The MIONet encodes different input functions, and the fusion network enables early interaction between the inputs, supporting generalization across multiple scenarios while adhering to the underlying physics of the poroelasticity problem. We study different examples, covering 2D and 3D domains and considering variable material properties and time discretizations. We test the models on unseen loading histories, demonstrating accuracy and generalizability across scenarios. For complex geometries and boundary conditions, we show that with both physics and data considered in the loss function, a PI-ML model can be trained using only 0.1\% of the training increments used for pure data-driven training. In addition, we expand the PIF-MIONet to incorporate a domain decomposition approach, which was found to be particularly useful when modeling flow over more complex geometries with more localized phenomena. For the first time, IFENN is implemented using the open-source multiphysics simulator GEOS for the FEM part, providing a path for the framework's application to industrial-scale simulations and complex multiphysics problems.

Convergence and error analysis of problem-independent machine learning (PIML) with linear completeness

Ke Tan, Chong-Jun Li, Xu Guo

Problem-independent machine learning (PIML) is an effective computational approach that combines the finite element method with machine learning. PIML uses neural networks to learn the mapping from material distributions or element geometries to the numerical shape functions of substructures. This approach avoids repeated computation while preserving the conformity of the underlying finite element discretization, thereby significantly improving the efficiency of substructure-based finite element analysis. However, existing studies on PIML have primarily focused on numerical accuracy and computational efficiency, while rigorous theoretical analyses of its approximation errors are still limited. In this paper, we establish an error analysis framework for PIML from a classical finite element perspective, in which the numerical error is bounded in terms of the finite element discretization error and the network generalization error. Our analysis shows that, when only rigid-body displacement invariance is enforced, the resulting error estimate contains a learning-induced term that does not necessarily decrease under mesh refinement. To ensure convergence, we propose an improved PIML scheme with linear completeness. By using linear displacement field constraints to reconstruct the corresponding columns of the transition matrix, we ensure that the resulting numerical shape-function space contains the space of linear polynomials. We derive error estimates and establish convergence results of the improved scheme in the H1 and L2 norms. Numerical experiments verify the theoretical results and demonstrate that the improved PIML achieves optimal convergence rates, good generalization performance, and numerical stability.

Projector Subspace Optimization for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning

Hao Yu, Quan Cheng, Lijun Zhang

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve strong performance through instruction tuning, yet adapting them to sequential tasks requires Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT). Existing replay-free methods expand prompts or routed LoRA experts across multi-layer decoders, incurring heavy parameter and routing overhead while shifting internal feature distributions. Instead, we identify the final linear layer of the vision-language projector as a high-capacity, coordinate-stationary bridge, where sequential updates easily induce interface drift by displacing historical visual tokens. While low-rank subspace adaptation offers a compact remedy, selecting directions solely from historical statistics or task gradients sacrifices plasticity or stability, and unconstrained multi-step updates still accumulate large drift. To resolve both issues, we propose Projector Refinement through Optimized Update Directions (PROUD), a continual subspace optimization framework for the terminal projector layer. At task onset, PROUD combines retained input statistics with initial gradients to analytically extract a metric-orthonormal subspace maximizing first-order task gain under a drift budget. During training, it regularizes updates within this fixed subspace via an exact Frobenius penalty before losslessly merging them with zero inference or routing overhead. Experiments on UCIT and CoIN across MLLM backbones show that PROUD outperforms recent replay-free baselines in continual accuracy while curbing forgetting with a fraction of trainable parameters.

OAT3D: A Generative Model for 3D Shape- and Resolution-Free Structural Topology Optimization

Hongrui Chen, Kittipong Thiamchaiboonthawee, Zhen Wei, Amin Heyrani Nobari, Faez Ahmed

Topology optimization determines how material should be distributed within a design domain to optimize structural performance under prescribed loads, supports, and constraints. The problems are highly nonconvex, and conventional solvers require repeated finite element analyses, making high-resolution 3D topology optimization computationally expensive. Data-driven approaches face an additional bottleneck: generating training datasets requires solving large numbers of these costly problems, so existing work in data-driven topology optimization primarily focuses on 2D problems. Large-scale 3D datasets remain scarce, and existing models remain limited in problem scale and boundary-condition diversity. Here, we introduce Optimize Any Topology-3D (OAT3D), a generative model for high-resolution 3D structural topology optimization. OAT3D introduces a novel sparse problem encoding to represent load and fixity conditions and combines a sparse volumetric variational autoencoder with a conditional diffusion transformer. We construct a dataset of 501,865 optimized topologies, generated using 51,800 GPU-hours and encompassing structures with up to one million elements and substantially more complex loading and support configurations than any existing structural topology optimization dataset. To our knowledge, this is the largest dataset for 3D topology optimization structures. Trained on this dataset, OAT3D generates structures at 512$^3$ resolution and achieves a $44.9\times$ speedup for raw candidate structure generation or $3.65\times$ speedup to reach similar compliance over conventional topology optimization. These contributions expand the scale and diversity of data-driven topology optimization for three-dimensional structural design.

Atomistic insight into defect-mediated boron redistribution and high-temperature strengthening in TiAl alloys

Xiang Meng, Shuhei Shinzato, Zhiqiang Zhao, Linfeng Yu, Jian Guo, Liangli Chu

Boron (B) microalloying can effectively refine microstructures of as-cast TiAl alloys and regulate their mechanical properties. Yet, how dilute B exists in TiAl and interacts with defects and how these interactions govern high-temperature deformation remain elusive. Here, we systematically elucidate defect-mediated B redistribution in TiAl alloys and its consequences for their deformation using hybrid grand-canonical Monte Carlo/molecular dynamics simulations augmented with a newly developed high-fidelity machine-learning potential. As a typical product of B microalloying, borides are markedly stiffer than the TiAl matrix and act as brittle reinforcing phases, while borides/matrix interfacial adhesion depends on surface termination and stacking sequence. In the bulk, interstitial B tends to be trapped at Ti-rich octahedral sites by high migration barriers but can kinetically evolve into substitutional dopants upon approaching Al vacancies. Increased tensile stress and vacancy concentration further promote B enrichment in the bulk. B is enriched at semi-coherent $γ/α_2$ boundaries and preferentially partitions into the $α_2$ phase. In polycrystalline TiAl, B accumulates primarily in disordered regions, raising the average flow stress by 9.4% at 1 at.% B and 900 K. This strengthening is accompanied by reduced shear strain accumulation. Simulated microstructural evolution and stacking-fault energetics indicate that B impedes dislocation glide via local pinning and suppresses deformation twinning, thereby increasing resistance to plastic deformation. By linking solute redistribution to defect interactions and deformation, these findings establish an atomistic framework for understanding and designing high-performance alloys based on interstitial solutes.

Constrained Allen-Cahn phase-field topology optimization of flexoelectric devices

Hao Zhen, Sheng Mao

Topology optimization provides an effective approach to exploring performance-oriented designs for miniature devices. In this work, a phase-field framework for constrained topology optimization of microscale flexoelectric devices is developed using a continuum model under the assumption of infinitesimal deformations. The material distribution is optimized at the device level with the output voltage as the objective. The framework also accommodates objectives based on capacitance or stored electrostatic energy. Manufacturing variations are incorporated through a worst-case objective over the prescribed geometric realizations, with spectral constraints imposed to maintain the structural connectivity. The framework is applied to two-dimensional pressure and bending sensors and a three-dimensional thin-film device. The optimized nominal output voltages reach approximately 4.8 times the initial value for the pressure sensor and 2.5-3.4 times the initial values for the bending sensors. The voltage gains arise primarily from increased mechanically induced source charge, with capacitance reduction providing an additional contribution that varies with the device configuration. Erosion analyses of representative designs further show that structural connectivity is well preserved when structural members become locally narrower, with the output voltage remaining close to that of the nominal configuration. Combined with specific microfabrication processes, this optimization framework is expected to provide a numerical basis for configuration screening and prototype development of flexoelectric devices.

A mixed-primal FEM for Poisson-Fermi interfacial problems

Siyad Rahman Mandottil, Neela Nataraj, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier

This paper presents a mixed-primal FEM for a linearised Poisson--Fermi interface problem arising from the Bazant--Storey--Kornyshev theory, which describes electrostatic interactions in ionic solutions. It leads to a transmission problem coupling a fourth-order equation in the solvent region with a second-order equation in the solute region. To precisely compute the electrostatic stress and the interactive force in the electrolyte, we reformulate the fourth-order problem as a mixed system by introducing the gradient of the electrostatic potential, the divergence of its gradient, and the gradient of its divergence as additional independent variables. The second-order problem is studied in its primal form as this approach eliminates the requirement for Lagrange multipliers to enforce the transmission conditions. The resulting formulation is discretised using Raviart--Thomas finite elements for the vector variables and piecewise polynomial finite elements for the scalar variables. Well-posedness of the mixed-primal formulation and optimal a priori error estimates for the field variables as well as for the postprocessed Maxwell tensor are established under suitable regularity assumptions, in parameter-weighted norms in which the inf-sup constant is independent of the correlation length and of the permittivity ratio. The constants in the error estimates are independent of both parameters as well, up to a loss of at most half an order when the mesh does not resolve the length scale (square root of the relative permittivity); the Sobolev norms of the solution may still depend on the parameters through boundary layers. The postprocessed Maxwell stress converges with optimal order on quasi-uniform meshes, except for the lowest-order scheme in 3D. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical convergence rates and demonstrate the properties of the proposed method for problems in 2D and 3D.

What a Drop Reveals: Sim-to-Real Material Estimation for Deformable Objects from Video

Ziyue Peng, Yanxi Hu, Junyoung Kim, Andrew Chen, Jin Yang, Chandrajit L. Bajaj

How an object deforms when it hits the ground reveals what it is made of. From a single real video of a drop, we estimate a deformable object's density-normalized stiffness $E/ρ$, and its yield stress $σ_Y/ρ$ when the impact causes plastic deformation, with a feed-forward predictor trained only in simulation. Real videos carry no material labels, so such predictors are developed and validated on synthetic data alone. We show that synthetic validation ranks their geometric inputs in the wrong order. Material acts through 3D deformation, and tracklets lifted to 3D with estimated depth are indeed the most accurate input in simulation. However, on real drops they are the least accurate, and better depth does not repair them: the failure persists with simulator depth in training, LiDAR depth at test time, and exact depth on rendered replays of the real drops. Short 2D optical-flow tracklets do the opposite: they improve nothing in simulation, yet they transfer best. Material is therefore better read from what perception measures consistently in both domains than from what is most informative in simulation. Our predictor, PhysReader, reads the video and 2D tracklets and learns the lift to 3D in simulation by matching a teacher that sees exact 3D deformation. To measure transfer, we introduce PhysReader-Real: 16 everyday objects dropped five times each, with reference materials fit jointly to each object's drops and checked against tensile tests. On PhysReader-Real, PhysReader is closer to the reference materials than every other input and baseline. The one exception is a per-scene optimizer started from PhysReader's prediction, which ends slightly closer but takes 49 minutes per video instead of 12 seconds.

Verifying the Outputs of PDE Solvers: From Finite Elements to Neural Surrogates

Konstantinos Dovelos
