Finite Element Modeling of Fracture in Brittle Foods with Physically Consistent Parameter Calibration: A Practical Case Study on Cooled Dark Chocolate

Patrick Kurzeja, Felix Rörentrop, Kai Langenfeld, Jörn Mosler

Fracture in food can be undesired during production and transportation but also intended during cutting, grinding or mastication. The associated peak load, the deformation at failure and the resulting fragment geometry are all product properties. This work condenses the modeling of such fracture into a physically rigorous recipe that considers the practical constraints imposed by finite element simulations where necessary and applies idealized theoretical assessments where applicable. Cooled dark chocolate serves as a reproducible model material. Two well-established modeling frameworks for Griffith-type brittle fracture are assessed at an affordable spatial discretization: a sharp-interface and a diffuse phase-field approach. The assessment covers the choice of numerical framework, its discretization and a staged identification of the model parameters. Each parameter is identified from the loading stage that governs it: elastic parameters from pre-crack tension and compression, critical energy release rate from three-point-bending peak force, and the phase-field width last. The phase-field width interferes with the structural response, crack curvature and stress fields, depending on its lower bound set by the affordable spatial resolution. Elastic slope, peak force and crack path therefore cannot be met practically by a single parameter set. The objective must be fixed for the calibration of the model parameters and reported together with the numerical parameters and the boundary value problem.

Retention-constrained fine-tuning of a pre-trained aluminium MACE potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity

Yue Wu, Yuan Tian, Deng Pan, Mingwei Chen

Adapting a pre-trained machine-learning interatomic potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity requires improving defect accuracy without degrading the near-equilibrium elastic and vibrational response. Here we achieve this balance for the aluminium MACE potential QP17 by integrating conditional readout uncertainty, iterative full-parameter updates and protocol-compatible relative-energy anchoring. Sensitivities over 144 linear readout weights guide acquisition, with within-batch covariance updates and evolving neural representations. After four active-learning rounds (32 acquired configurations and 400 gradient updates), force-uncertainty acquisition reduces mean development force error from 22.43 to 21.52 meV/Angstrom, outperforming random sampling (22.70), geometry-diversity selection (22.46) and uniform training across all 104 candidate labels (22.71) under the same update budget. All three force-uncertainty runs satisfy bulk-mechanical retention criteria: historical relative-energy error decreases from 0.375 to 0.207 meV/atom, while harmonic phonon root-mean-square drift remains below 0.05 THz. Matched objective ablations reveal that relative-energy anchoring suppresses harmonic drift. Out-of-sample evaluations across planar faults, twin boundaries and grain boundaries demonstrate transferable gains in defect energies and deviatoric stresses, even though virial stresses are never fitted. A closed-loop molecular dynamics and density functional theory (MD-DFT) pilot confirms that automated acceptance gating intercepts updates that violate historical elastic-group energy or vibrational tolerances. Together, these components establish a retention-constrained fine-tuning protocol for improving plastic-deformation observables while preserving the tested bulk mechanical response.

Total Deformation-based MPM for Perfectly Elastic Solid Simulation with Strong Deformations

Timna Böttcher, Rick Ridder, Jan Bender

We present a new MPM-based solver for perfectly elastic solids, which can recover from strong deformations and naturally allows contact handling. Typical MPM solvers suffer from numerical fracture, where objects break apart even when no plasticity is included in the elasticity model. Previous approaches for perfectly elastic solids solve this by computing forces on a fixed grid in the reference configuration. However, this makes them incompatible with standard collision handling methods. We instead propose a method for deformable solid simulation without numerical fracture, that still computes elastic forces on the current grid. Our method can easily include grid-based collision handling, as commonly used in MPM frameworks. Additionally, we compute the deformation directly from the particle positions and a fixed reference grid, without any updates based on velocity gradients. This facilitates the inclusion of particle-level collision handling and zero-energy mode suppression, which counteracts artifacts from non-linear deformations. As a result, we can efficiently simulate complex scenarios of elastic objects undergoing large deformations, including contact.

A minimizing-movement framework for parametric finite element approximation of anisotropic curve shortening flows

Wei Jiang, Chunmei Su, Ganghui Zhang

We propose a minimizing-movement framework for constructing parametric finite element methods (PFEMs) for anisotropic curve shortening flows. The cornerstone is an admissible modified energy: a smooth, convex, and iteration-dependent surrogate that majorizes the original anisotropic energy and agrees with it at the current iterate. For any fixed finite element degree $n\geq1$ and any positive surface energy density $γ\in C^2(\mathbb{S}^1)$, the unconstrained one-step problem has a unique solution for any time-step size whenever the parametric derivative of the current discrete curve does not vanish on any mesh element. It also satisfies a one-step discrete energy dissipation inequality. It therefore preserves the variational structure of the underlying $L^2$-gradient flow. The framework recovers a variational interpretation of Dziuk's isotropic scheme and applies directly to the weak anisotropies. For strong anisotropies, a convex splitting removes the weak-anisotropy restriction on the original density. A scalar constraint further extends the framework to area-preserving flows. Numerical experiments exhibit approximately the expected convergence for smooth isotropic and weakly anisotropic examples. They also confirm energy dissipation and area preservation. In the strongly anisotropic regime, the computations produce sharp facet-like profiles but reveal mesh-scale oscillations under the grid refinement.

Bartosz Bucholc, Marcin Maździarz, Szymon Nosewicz

This paper focuses on the numerical and analytical determination of the mechanical parameters of aluminum-matrix nanocomposites using atomistic-scale simulations within a multiscale modeling framework. Molecular statics simulations were employed to evaluate the tensile and compressive behavior of Al based composites reinforced with nanosized alumina particles. Two classes of atomistic models were investigated. First, a unit-cell model containing a single Al2O3 monocrystal embedded in an aluminum matrix was used to examine the influence of the volume fraction of the ceramic inclusion on the effective mechanical properties. Second, a polycrystalline RVE model comprising an alumina particle was analyzed to assess the effect of the number of Al grains on effective properties. For each case, the mechanical response was characterized by stress-strain curves, enabling the identification of upper-scale material parameters associated with the elastic regime (bulk, shear and Young's modulus) and the damage stage (ultimate strength, ultimate strain, and failure strain). Finally, atomistic-scale simulation outputs provided insight into the dominant deformation/damage mechanisms (brittle versus ductile) and fracture modes (transgranular versus intergranular).

Constrained neural softening functions for the Mullins effect in parameter-dependent materials

Dominik K. Klein, Jonas Fey, Maximilian Kannapinn, Oliver Weeger

Accurately capturing the Mullins effect across varying material compositions remains challenging, as constitutive models must unify flexible representations of stress softening with physical consistency and robust parameter calibration. In this work, we investigate parameter-dependent physics-augmented neural network constitutive models within the pseudo-elastic modeling framework, focusing on structural constraints that ensure physically admissible neural softening functions. This paper has three key contributions: (1) We formulate different neural softening functions within the pseudo-elastic framework, including a multiplicative decomposition of the softening function that enables parameter-dependent ansatz functions satisfying common mechanical constitutive conditions by construction. (2) We derive a sufficient condition for ellipticity of pseudo-elastic constitutive models, which can be enforced in the constitutive models by construction. (3) We systematically investigate how flexibility and structural constraints influence the performance of neural softening functions in terms of prediction accuracy and calibration robustness. For this, we consider multiaxial experimental data of carbon black-filled EPDM rubber synthesized with varying amounts of curatives. The findings of our work suggest that neural softening functions are a promising extension of the classical pseudo-elastic framework and, when subject to suitable constraints, can improve predictive accuracy for highly nonlinear, parameter-dependent materials.

A hyper-reduction strategy for elastoplastic hydro-mechanical problems in partially saturated soil

Alba Muixí, Lluís Monforte, Antonio Rodríguez-Ferran, Sergio Zlotnik

This work proposes a hyper-reduced order model for coupled hydro-mechanical simulations of elastoplastic deformation and water flow in partially saturated soils. These simulations are used to monitor and assess the safety of geotechnical structures such as earthfill dams, where calibration, uncertainty quantification and scenario analysis require solving the same transient model many times for different parameter values. The displacement and the pore pressure are approximated in a field-split reduced basis obtained by proper orthogonal decomposition, and the Galerkin-projected equations are integrated with decomposed cubature rules, one per balance equation, built with the energy-conserving sampling and weighting (ECSW) method. Each residual is split into an affine part and a nonlinear correction: the affine part is computed exactly, at negligible online cost, so that the cubature rule only needs to approximate the correction, which is localized in space. The construction is independent of the constitutive model choices and is implemented in FEniCSx by assembling the original variational forms on the small set of elements selected by ECSW. The method is tested on two- and three-dimensional dams retaining tailings and water, simulating a raise of the dam and the subsequent consolidation phase, with the friction angle, stiffness and hydraulic conductivity of the dam material and the height of the raise as varying parameters. The hyper-reduced model reproduces the displacement and pressure fields with errors below $0.5\%$ with respect to the finite element solution, achieving speed-ups of up to $43\times$ in two dimensions and $77\times$ in three. These performance gains grow with the extent of the plastic yielding, and the selected elements concentrate in the plastified and unsaturated zones, whose size is set by the physics rather than by the mesh resolution.

Universal neural network approximation of symmetric polyconvex energies and envelopes

Florian Gammer

Symmetric polyconvexity provides a sufficient condition for weak lower semicontinuity in small-strain elasticity and a tractable lower bound for the relaxation of non-(quasi)convex energies. Since classical algorithms for computing such functions can be computationally expensive, neural network approximations have recently emerged as an alternative. A central difficulty with establishing such neural network based approaches for symmetric polyconvexity is the additional monotonicity property. As we show, the corresponding weight restrictions on ReLU-based input-convex networks do not yield universal approximation of the lifted representers. Here, we introduce symmetric polyconvex neural networks (SPCNNs) for constitutive modeling and approximation of symmetric polyconvex envelopes. The deep architecture combines convexity with the monotonicity required of a representer in the space of minors. We prove a universal approximation theorem for symmetric polyconvex energies, without requiring isotropy, and quantify the number of affine pieces attainable by the lifted network and derive reduced architectures for isotropic energies. Numerical examples illustrate stress-based regression and approximation of the symmetric polyconvex envelope of a double-well potential. Symmetric polyconvexity is guaranteed by construction, while the accuracy of the fitted energies and envelopes is assessed numerically.

Yuchen Zhang, Yaxiong Wang, Yujiao Wu, Lianwei Wu, Qi Zhang, Li Zhu

Multimodal forgery detection is commonly studied under a static assumption: training attacks are expected to approximate those encountered after deployment. This assumption becomes increasingly fragile as generators, editing systems, and multimodal large language models continually introduce new forgery mechanisms and forensic evidence. We formulate this problem as attack-incremental continual multimodal forgery detection, where the binary real/fake interface remains fixed while emerging attacks must be learned without forgetting previous ones. We introduce Fable-Bench, which organizes attack evolution through complementary joint-modality, text-side, and image-side protocols. We further propose ARISE (Authentic Reference-Informed Stability Enhancement), motivated by the open-ended forged class and the comparatively reusable geometry of authentic content. ARISE fits a real-only Gaussian mixture model to projected authentic replay representations as a compact historical reference. Meanwhile, the proposed Gaussian-Responsibility-Aware Stability Preservation (GRASP) derives a sample-adaptive anisotropic metric that strongly constrains historically stable representation directions while retaining plasticity elsewhere. Our Vulnerability-Aware Replay (VAR) further estimates stage-wise vulnerability from authentic-density degradation, forged-to-authentic boundary confusion, and authentic-mode occupancy drift, then assigns bounded replay emphasis to fragile stages. All adaptive signals are obtained from training-time replay statistics, while inference uses only the original multimodal backbone and binary classification head. Experiments across three attack-evolution protocols and multiple detector architectures demonstrate improved historical retention and final average performance over representative continual-learning baselines.

These results demonstrate that full-field normal strain measurements provide an effective basis for identifying the stiffness parameters of bimodulus granular micromechanics models. No friction required: Self-locking in entangled assemblies of curved filaments

Benjamin R. Greenvall, Jay van den Berg, Ross E. Meinhart, Patrick A. Staudenmayer, Alfred J. Crosby, Gregory M. Grason

Filaments are incredible material building blocks that, when entangled together, form robust structures with remarkable adaptive mechanics, from stiff cables to stretchable knits. Basic questions about the mechanism that holds together such structures, especially assemblies like rope, without attractive physical or chemical bonds between constituents are among the oldest considered in materials science. Contradicting a long-standing picture that structural integrity of rope-like assemblies rely on a combination of external loading and inter-filament friction to prevent spontaneous detangling (e.g. fraying of a cut rope), we demonstrate that assemblies of intrinsically-curved filaments can achieve structural integrity purely from the competition of elastic forces that resist shape-change and (traction-free) repulsive forces that prevent overlap. We analyze a friction-free model of assembly of intrinsically-helical filaments, and show that depending on filament number and preferred filament shape, overcrowding in the assemblies generate elastic pre-stresses that give rise to either self-locking entanglement or spontaneous detanglement through propagation of a soliton-like detanglement front through assemblies. We test a generalized fracture criterion for spontaneous detangling and confirm the role of shape-elastic costs to self-locking via experiment and simulation of frictional filaments, demonstrating a range of bundled structures which cannot be made self-locking for any value of friction. The mechanism of ``purely elastic cohesion" illuminated here sheds light on self-locking in a broader class of entangled structures, and further, offers design principles for new classes of active filament materials capable of stimuli-responsive assembly and disassembly.

Evolution of microstructure and crystallographic texture in a novel Zr-4Nb-2Al alloy during hot rotary swaging

A multi-step induction-assisted hot rotary swaging route was developed for the novel dual-phase Zr-4Nb-2Al alloy to investigate the evolution of microstructure, crystallographic texture, and mechanical properties during sequential deformation. Phase-transformation temperatures determined by electrical resistivity measurements were used to select processing conditions in the single-phase $β$ field at 880°C and the $α+β$ field at 780°C. Induction heating with thermographic temperature control enabled reduction of the initial 12 mm rod to 6.3 mm, corresponding to a total area reduction of 72%. Swaging in the $β$ field refined millimetre-sized prior-$β$ grains to approximately 100 $μ$m, while subsequent cooling produced a fine lamellar $α+β$ microstructure. Further deformation in the $α+β$ field disrupted the lamellar structure, promoted globularisation and recrystallisation of the $α$ phase, and refined the $β$ phase.
