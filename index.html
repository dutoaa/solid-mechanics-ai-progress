Physics-Informed Fusion-MIONet with IFENN: A Generalized Framework for Coupled Poroelasticity Problems

Fouad M. Amin, Rigoberto Moncada, Panos Pantidis, Diab W. Abueidda, Fan Fei, Mostafa E. Mobasher

Complex, high-dimensional, large-scale poroelasticity simulations using traditional numerical solvers can be prohibitively expensive in terms of computational resources. We introduce an innovative framework for poroelasticity problems that integrates physics-informed machine learning (PI-ML) models with high-performance FEM solvers. This integration is achieved through the Integrated Finite Element Neural Network (IFENN) setup, which uses a staggered scheme between an FEM solver for mechanical equilibrium and a PI-ML model for fluid-flow predictions. We introduce a novel Physics-Informed Fusion-MIONet (PIF-MIONet) architecture, in which a fusion network encodes the outputs of the MIONet branches. The MIONet encodes different input functions, and the fusion network enables early interaction between the inputs, supporting generalization across multiple scenarios while adhering to the underlying physics of the poroelasticity problem. We study different examples, covering 2D and 3D domains and considering variable material properties and time discretizations. We test the models on unseen loading histories, demonstrating accuracy and generalizability across scenarios. For complex geometries and boundary conditions, we show that with both physics and data considered in the loss function, a PI-ML model can be trained using only 0.1\% of the training increments used for pure data-driven training. In addition, we expand the PIF-MIONet to incorporate a domain decomposition approach, which was found to be particularly useful when modeling flow over more complex geometries with more localized phenomena. For the first time, IFENN is implemented using the open-source multiphysics simulator GEOS for the FEM part, providing a path for the framework's application to industrial-scale simulations and complex multiphysics problems.

OAT3D: A Generative Model for 3D Shape- and Resolution-Free Structural Topology Optimization

Hongrui Chen, Kittipong Thiamchaiboonthawee, Zhen Wei, Amin Heyrani Nobari, Faez Ahmed

Topology optimization determines how material should be distributed within a design domain to optimize structural performance under prescribed loads, supports, and constraints. The problems are highly nonconvex, and conventional solvers require repeated finite element analyses, making high-resolution 3D topology optimization computationally expensive. Data-driven approaches face an additional bottleneck: generating training datasets requires solving large numbers of these costly problems, so existing work in data-driven topology optimization primarily focuses on 2D problems. Large-scale 3D datasets remain scarce, and existing models remain limited in problem scale and boundary-condition diversity. Here, we introduce Optimize Any Topology-3D (OAT3D), a generative model for high-resolution 3D structural topology optimization. OAT3D introduces a novel sparse problem encoding to represent load and fixity conditions and combines a sparse volumetric variational autoencoder with a conditional diffusion transformer. We construct a dataset of 501,865 optimized topologies, generated using 51,800 GPU-hours and encompassing structures with up to one million elements and substantially more complex loading and support configurations than any existing structural topology optimization dataset. To our knowledge, this is the largest dataset for 3D topology optimization structures. Trained on this dataset, OAT3D generates structures at 512$^3$ resolution and achieves a $44.9\times$ speedup for raw candidate structure generation or $3.65\times$ speedup to reach similar compliance over conventional topology optimization. These contributions expand the scale and diversity of data-driven topology optimization for three-dimensional structural design.

Atomistic insight into defect-mediated boron redistribution and high-temperature strengthening in TiAl alloys

Xiang Meng, Shuhei Shinzato, Zhiqiang Zhao, Linfeng Yu, Jian Guo, Liangli Chu

Boron (B) microalloying can effectively refine microstructures of as-cast TiAl alloys and regulate their mechanical properties. Yet, how dilute B exists in TiAl and interacts with defects and how these interactions govern high-temperature deformation remain elusive. Here, we systematically elucidate defect-mediated B redistribution in TiAl alloys and its consequences for their deformation using hybrid grand-canonical Monte Carlo/molecular dynamics simulations augmented with a newly developed high-fidelity machine-learning potential. As a typical product of B microalloying, borides are markedly stiffer than the TiAl matrix and act as brittle reinforcing phases, while borides/matrix interfacial adhesion depends on surface termination and stacking sequence. In the bulk, interstitial B tends to be trapped at Ti-rich octahedral sites by high migration barriers but can kinetically evolve into substitutional dopants upon approaching Al vacancies. Increased tensile stress and vacancy concentration further promote B enrichment in the bulk. B is enriched at semi-coherent $γ/α_2$ boundaries and preferentially partitions into the $α_2$ phase. In polycrystalline TiAl, B accumulates primarily in disordered regions, raising the average flow stress by 9.4% at 1 at.% B and 900 K. This strengthening is accompanied by reduced shear strain accumulation. Simulated microstructural evolution and stacking-fault energetics indicate that B impedes dislocation glide via local pinning and suppresses deformation twinning, thereby increasing resistance to plastic deformation. By linking solute redistribution to defect interactions and deformation, these findings establish an atomistic framework for understanding and designing high-performance alloys based on interstitial solutes.

Hip fractures represent a significant clinical challenge because they cause excess disability and mortality, and have a growing incidence in aging populations, as bone fragility increases. Although finite element analysis (FEA) provides biomechanical assessment of femoral strength, its clinical applicability is mainly limited by high computational cost. Conventional machine learning (ML) approaches have been explored as surrogate models for FEA; predictions are fast but fail to capture the spatial and topological structure of finite element meshes. Evaluation results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid architecture achieves the strongest predictive performance among all evaluated models. The proposed hybrid model achieves the best performance with an R^2 score of 0.8022 and mean absolute error of 2.879 MPa, highlighting the importance of incorporating mesh topology in biomechanical prediction tasks. The proposed framework provides a scalable and computationally efficient alternative for FEM-based orthopedic analyses and fracture risk assessment. The code is publicly available on GitHub.

Topology-Prior-Guided Regional Latent Dynamics for Transient Structural Response Prediction

Yuan Zhang, Mohammad Amin Nabian, Shaotong Fu, Yiding Liu, Chengcheng Chu, Xuelin Tao

Predicting the transient nonlinear responses of complex engineering structures remains challenging due to large finite element meshes, heterogeneous structural components, and history-dependent mechanical behavior. This study proposes a topology-prior-guided regional latent dynamics (TPLD) surrogate model that combines physically structured regional representations with dynamics-aware latent learning for full-field time-history prediction. Based on mesh topology and engineering priors, structural responses are encoded into spatially coupled regional latent variables through region-aware feature extraction and topology-constrained interactions. An auxiliary one-step prediction task enhances latent-space dynamical predictability by introducing state-transition supervision. A load-conditioned spatiotemporal Mamba model then predicts regional latent trajectories by integrating temporal causal scanning and bidirectional spatial scanning, while a shared coordinate-conditioned decoder reconstructs full-field nodal responses. The framework is evaluated on three finite element datasets: a parametric stiffened panel, a partitioned pressure impact plate, and a Toyota Yaris full-vehicle crash model. The relative L2 errors for the three displacement components range from 4.00% to 8.21% on the stiffened panel and from 1.41% to 12.22% on the approximately 1.49-million-node vehicle model. On the BIW crash benchmark, TPLD achieves a relative L2 position error of 0.620%, representing a 30.7% reduction compared with the best-performing GeoTransolver configuration. Ablation studies demonstrate the advantages of topology-guided regional representations over global latent and learnable soft-slice alternatives, while auxiliary one-step prediction particularly improves the prediction of accumulated plastic strain.

Verifying the Outputs of PDE Solvers: From Finite Elements to Neural Surrogates

Konstantinos Dovelos

Physics simulation outputs increasingly come from solvers that no expert inspects, such as third-party packages in automated pipelines, programs written by large language models (LLMs), and learned surrogates. In earlier work on cardiac pulsed field ablation, we proposed that solvers return a certificate with their answers. The certificate consists of an admissible potential and an exactly charge-conserving current, and a small independent checker turns it into guaranteed error bounds against a written specification of the physics. Here, the same checker verifies the outputs of other solvers on the same problem. Specifically, FEniCSx computes both fields and receives the same bounds as our own solver. Five plausible bugs in a correct solver, one of them about 0.2% from the true impedance, all converge and pass a grid convergence study, yet the certificate rejects or refutes all five. Of three solvers written by LLMs, one is correct and certified to 0.1%, and one never solves the equation but converges. Neural networks that predict the coefficients of a reduced basis emit admissible fields and receive valid per-prediction bounds, at most 0.0021% wide inside their training range and 0.3-25% wide outside it, where 90% conformal intervals cover none of 14 tests. Untuned Fourier neural operators with free nodal outputs receive valid but uninformative bounds. On a uniform grid, interpolation onto the certificate grid limits them, and on the certificate grid, thin cells turn small nodal errors into large energy errors. Energy-norm training narrows the certified interval tenfold, and projecting the nodal-loss outputs onto the reduced basis narrows it by two to four orders of magnitude, still uninformative. The guarantee concerns the stated model and holds for any output, wherever its input lies. It is informative only when the output is accurate in energy on a grid that resolves the problem.

A geometric-IEQ approach for curvature-driven flows

Harald Garcke, Yifei Li, Robert Nürnberg, Quan Zhao

We introduce a geometric invariant energy quadratization (IEQ) formulation for the $L^2$-gradient flow of a class of curvature-dependent energies of closed hypersurfaces, where the energy density depends on the mean curvature and the unit normal. A square-root auxiliary variable converts the geometric energy into a quadratic functional. Combining its evolution equation with a skew-symmetric treatment of surface transport yields a weak formulation whose energy law depends only on the normal velocity and is therefore independent of the chosen tangential motion. We construct monolithic nonlinear parametric finite element methods by coupling a common IEQ discretization with one of two geometric formulations that accommodate either the Barrett-Garcke-Nürnberg (BGN) tangential velocity or the minimal-deformation-rate (MDR) tangential velocity. Both fully discrete approximations satisfy an unconditional decay estimate for the discrete quadratic energy. We further extend the construction to anisotropic Willmore flow, and to $H^{-1}$-gradient flows. A variety of numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate the robustness and accuracy of the introduced methods.

Constitutive model identification aims to recover compact, interpretable models that accurately describe material behavior. In this work, we identify hyperelastic constitutive models using artificial neural networks with sparsity-promoting constraints and regularization. The proposed method combines the expressiveness of neural networks with the structural interpretability of sparse constitutive models, such as those identified by EUCLID, by representing the strain energy density as a sum of products of univariate functions of the invariants. The resulting decomposition exposes the contributions of individual invariants and their interactions. The method is demonstrated on synthetic data in supervised and unsupervised learning scenarios, showing that it can accurately identify interpretable constitutive models with few active modes.

AI agents can redesign engineering parts with more than a thousand CAD parameters and nonlinear simulations

Alexandra Starostina, Alexander Hermann, Mahish Guru, Kartik Bali, Roland Aydin, Christian C. Cyron

Simulation-driven design of real engineering components has to meet three conditions at once. It runs on few solves, since a nonlinear finite-element simulation of a 3D part takes hours; its design space has many values, the hundreds to thousands of values of the part's CAD model; and a better design often needs a redesign, coordinated changes of the part's proportions rather than local polishing of its surface. Automated optimisers meet at most two of these conditions. Classical optimisers, Bayesian or derivative-free, need a person to cut the design down to a few parameters, and on all the values they need far more solves; industrial shape optimisers move every surface node of the mesh in one solve per cycle, but only in small steps that polish rather than redesign. We ask whether a general-purpose large language model (LLM) can meet all three by joining what it knows with what the simulation shows. GPT-6 Astra edits a magnesium stent through a CAD geometry parametrisation of about 1,650 values, with a budget of 15 simulations; every proposal is built in CAD, meshed and simulated with large-deformation plasticity and contact, and the LLM reads the full results of every solve. Within that budget it lowered the objective, a high percentile of the strains in the stent's bends, by 32%; on the same budget, the industrial shape optimiser Tosca improved the original design by a tenth and then stopped improving. An earlier GPT-6 Astra design, 25% lower in the same objective, was checked in an independent, higher-fidelity model of the full deployment with crimping and damage: it was 14% less strained than the original after the full chain, and its most damaged spot began to fail later and carried 63% less damage at full expansion. LLMs can thus join their learned knowledge with simulation data to adjust more than a thousand design values and reach valid designs with real improvements.

Stabilized Isogeometric Topology Optimization on Trimmed Geometries

Haoyu Su, Giuliano Guarino, Xiaodong Wei

This paper presents a novel stabilized isogeometric topology optimization method for complex design domains represented by trimmed geometries. Minimum compliance is taken as the model problem, where a generalized Cahn-Hilliard model, a time-dependent fourth-order partial differential equation, is solved and its steady-state solutions yield the optimized structures. This approach does not require sensitivity analysis and can naturally accommodate material constraints and complex topological changes. To impose Dirichlet boundary conditions and solve the instability issue caused by trimming, Nitsche's formulation with minimal stabilization is proposed for the high-order problem. Moreover, the immersed boundary-conformal method is extended to handle complex non-design domains, allowing material-void interfaces to be treated in a unified manner. Finally, a three-dimensional biharmonic problem and several two-dimensional topology optimization examples are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness, stability, and flexibility of the proposed method in dealing with complex design domains.

Constitutive Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks in Finite Element Model Updating: Closed-Form Hyperelastic Strain Energies from Heterogeneous Experiments

Kian P. Abdolazizi, Kevin Linka, Christian J. Cyron

Calibrating a hyperelastic constitutive model for a soft material traditionally poses two problems: an expert must choose its functional form, and several homogeneous tests must determine its parameters. Neural constitutive models solve the first, but either remain black boxes or draw on a library of candidate terms fixed in advance. Full-field measurements, such as those from digital image correlation (DIC), promise to solve the second, replacing those tests by a single heterogeneous one. Two gaps remain: flexibility during training with interpretability afterwards, and a way to train such a model on one heterogeneous test. Here we close both. We embed a constitutive Kolmogorov-Arnold network (CKAN) in finite element model updating (FEMU) and train it by minimizing the mismatch between the predicted and the measured kinematic fields and reaction forces. Pruning and symbolic regression then reduce the trained network to a compact closed-form strain energy that satisfies every constitutive requirement. On perforated natural-rubber membranes, the CKANs reproduce the DIC displacement fields with median errors between one and two percent, and transfer to a hole pattern withheld from training. On a perforated silicone plate, a CKAN trained on one stretch profile at a single load reproduces that profile and the measured reaction force to within a few percent. On an aortic specimen inflated under pressure, an anisotropic CKAN predicts the inflated three-dimensional shape. The discovered closed-form energies match the CKANs they replace on the training data and can be compared directly with established models.

Failure Modes and Data-Efficient Remedies for Transformer-Based Prediction of Viscoplastic Flows

Maedeh Saberi, Hugo Franca, Maziyar Jalaal, Safa Jamali

Fast and accurate prediction of viscoplastic flows remains a challenge in computational rheology due to yield surfaces, discontinuous gradients, and strongly nonlinear transitions between yielded and unyielded regions. Transformer-based neural operators such as RheOFormer have recently shown strong potential for surrogate modeling of non-Newtonian flows. However, their applicability to viscoplastic systems and their efficacy in recovering realistic flows remain poorly understood. In this work, we present the first systematic investigation of RheOFormer applied to steady Bingham channel flow. By isolating the problem to a simple geometry and constitutive model, we examine how dataset structure, numerical value range, and input representation govern the model's ability to interpolate and extrapolate within the parameter space. The data regimes in which RheOFormer remains accurate and those in which errors emerge are identified, particularly for plug-dominated flows and for conditions outside the velocity scales or flow structures represented during training. Upon identifying these failure modes, targeted enhancements to the input representation and loss formulation are introduced to better predict low-gradient plug regions. Logarithmic velocity representations and gradient-aware loss formulations substantially improve accuracy in challenging regimes where the baseline formulation fails. In addition, extrapolation errors are reduced by augmenting the training set with only a few strategically selected cases spanning the missing physical regimes. Finally, a single partially developed snapshot suffices for an accurate steady-state prediction, and the model outperforms simply copying its input throughout flow development. These results highlight the importance of appropriate field representation and parameter-space coverage for data-efficient neural-operator prediction of viscoplastic flows.

Certified Finite Element Error Bounds for Diffusion Equations with Measure-Valued Principal-Part Delays

Hiroki Ishizaka

We derive computable a posteriori finite element error bounds for diffusion equations whose highest-order spatial operator carries a non-negative measure-valued delay. Although the delay law is replaced by finitely many atoms in computations, the bounds measure the error with respect to the solution for the original law. An equilibrated Raviart--Thomas flux controls the spatial residual, and a transport estimate on the computed time reconstruction controls the delay-law residual without additional temporal regularity of the exact solution.
