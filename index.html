Operator Learning of Microstructure-Dependent Internal-Variable Models for Inelastic Homogenization: Approximation and Complexity

Lianghao Cao, George Stepaniants, Andrew M. Stuart

We study the learning of homogenized constitutive laws for inelastic heterogeneous materials, aiming to replace costly concurrent unit-cell simulations with surrogates that generalize across loading histories and microstructures. We focus on the recurrent neural operator framework, motivated by the explicit form of homogenized Kelvin--Voigt linear viscoelasticity. In this framework, internal variables enable a Markovian approximation of loading-history dependence, and neural operators represent the microstructure-dependent constitutive response. For periodic Kelvin--Voigt viscoelasticity, we prove a universal approximation theorem showing that the model can approximate the homogenized constitutive law to any prescribed accuracy, uniformly in time and over a wide set of admissible strain trajectories and microstructural property fields, in two and three dimensions. This comprises a significant extension of the existing, somewhat limited, one-dimensional theory that uses explicit solutions for piecewise constant materials. Instead, in two and three dimensions, we adopt a more versatile approximation based on the spectral representation of the memory kernel that arises from homogenization. This also enables us to give an explicit bound on the internal-variable dimension, sufficient for approximation, exhibiting logarithmic growth in the inverse relative stress error and in material contrast. Numerical experiments on viscoelasticity and crystal plasticity complement the theory, illustrating the roles of internal-variable dimension and sample complexity in learning homogenized responses from unit-cell data. Theoretical results are formally verified in Lean 4.

Anchor Behavior, Not Parameters: Rehearsal-Free Continual Learning with a Single Shared Prompt

Duy-Cuong Bui, Trung-Anh Dang, Vincent Nguyen, Duc V. Le, Ngoc-Son Vu

Prompt-based continual learning adapts a frozen pre-trained vision transformer by tuning a small set of prompt tokens. Recently, one-prompt methods which share one single prompt across all tasks make this paradigm maximally parameter efficient. The open question is how a shared prompt can keep the knowledge of old classes while being updated for new ones. Existing methods protect old knowledge in parameter space, where they freeze task-specific prompts, expand new components, or sparsely gate which tokens receive gradients. However, this method sacrifices plasticity, yet the drift it still permits in shared tokens can corrupt the prompt's function on past classes. What must be preserved is the prompt's behavior. We propose TEA-Prompt, which couples (i) a token evaluation module that assigns each prompt token a continuous, input-conditional relevance score injected into the attention logits, softly specializing tokens across tasks without discarding any of them, and (ii) an attention-behavior anchoring loss that constrains each token's attention output around class-specific query regions using only compact Gaussian query statistics without stored old images, weighted by the token's past relevance. Anchoring the attention value allows the parameters to continue updating freely, as long as their interaction remains unchanged. TEA-Prompt achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with markedly lower forgetting on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, and CUB-200 with only 0.38M parameters. Code is publicy available at https://github.com/cuonga1cvp/TEA-Prompt.git

Are UFOs Real? Introducing Unified Factored Organizers

Thomas X. Sheppard, Alon Albalak, Joel Lehman, Kenneth O. Stanley

Implicit in much research in deep learning is the assumption that if a network achieves low loss, then it must have learned a good representation of its training data. Recent work by Kumar et al. (2025, arXiv:2505.11581) questions this assumption, arguing that networks trained with conventional gradient descent achieve low loss despite learning fractured entangled representations (FER), leaving related concepts disconnected and unrelated concepts entangled inappropriately. Kumar et al. further show that dramatically better representations are possible: a network evolved through an open-ended, human-in-the-loop process learned unified factored representations (UFR) that capture the regularities of the domain far better than a counterpart with nearly identical outputs trained with conventional gradient descent. Inspired by UFR, this paper introduces unified factored organizers (UFOs), networks trained to model regularities in the representations of other networks. A network can then be trained both to achieve low loss on its task objective and to align its representations with the model learned by the UFO. Networks trained with UFOs are shown to learn qualitatively superior representations in an image-regression domain where representations are easily interpretable. To provide quantitative evidence for the impact of UFOs on representation learning, a transformer is trained with and without UFO regularization to grok a modular arithmetic problem set. When combined with weight decay, UFOs can reduce the number of transformer training steps needed to grok by more than 10x relative to weight decay alone. Without weight decay, UFOs can reduce the number of transformer training steps needed to grok by more than 100x relative to an unregularized baseline. If UFOs are real, they suggest a new paradigm where loss functions themselves are trained to learn an effective model of good representation.

A transversely isotropic constitutive model for semisoft elasticity in main-chain liquid crystal elastomers

Donghao Li, Xin Yi, Fan Feng

Monodomain main-chain liquid crystal elastomers exhibit pronounced stiffness anisotropy and broad semisoft plateaus following finite onset strains. At fixed chain anisotropy, the neo-classical model predicts equal principal Young's moduli, while its transversely isotropic extension with a constant semisoft modulus constrains the relation between reorientation onset and subsequent stress growth. We develop a finite-strain, isothermal elastic model combining transversely isotropic network elasticity with a deformation-dependent quadratic semisoft penalty. Within a weak-fluctuation approximation, a reduced statistical closure based on assumed linear contraction of effective local anisotropy fluctuations at fixed mean anisotropy yields an exponential transition from an initial to a residual semisoft modulus. The network energy preserves ideal soft modes, and the formulation recovers the de Gennes strain-director coupling in the infinitesimal limit. Analytical relations for perpendicular tension show that the rotating-stage stress depends on both the current semisoft modulus and its derivative with respect to the first deformation invariant. This permits a finite onset followed by a substantially flatter rotating response than in the constant-modulus limit. Oblique tension couples director rotation, shear and lateral contraction. Comparisons with orientation-dependent measurements using a common parameter set capture the principal stiffness contrast and broad perpendicular plateau, with discrepancies remaining in oblique stresses and intermediate-angle initial moduli.

An open source Python code for three-dimensional topology optimization in single precision on a consumer GPU

Leonilson Toffoli Pedrini, Rodolfo G. M. Andrade, Rafael M. Ferro

Three-dimensional topology optimization solves linear systems with millions of unknowns at every design iteration. Consumer graphics cards run far faster in single than in double precision, and open codes for them mostly address minimum compliance. This educational article presents TOF3D, an open source Python code for density-based topology optimization in single precision on one consumer GPU. It solves four classes of problems: stress constrained minimum compliance, robust minimum compliance against manufacturing errors, compliant mechanisms and bone-like porous infill. The stiffness operator is applied without assembly, and the conjugate gradient method is preconditioned by geometric multigrid. Rounding the element matrix to single precision gave rigid body motions a spurious energy and changed compliances by up to 5.4%. Forming the element products from differences of nodal displacements reduced this error to $1.3\times 10^{-5}$ at no cost in time. Adjoint sensitivities agreed with finite differences to within $1.7\times 10^{-6}$, and the solutions agreed with Ansys Mechanical APDL to within $4.1\times 10^{-10}$ with the same element. On an RTX 3060 with 12 GB, one design iteration of a cantilever took 0.46 s with 0.5 million elements and 15 s with 33.5 million. On the same card and problem, an open GPU code that applies its operator in double precision took 3.7 and 21 s with 0.5 and 4.2 million elements and ran out of memory on larger meshes. Eight cases fixed in a protocol before the runs took 9 to 35 minutes each with 4.2 to 5.2 million elements.

A new approach to measuring both the steady flow curve and the planar extensional viscosity of complex fluids using exponential shear

Nicholas King, Eugene Pashkovski, Reid Patterson, Gareth H. McKinley

We revisit the protocol known as Exponential Shear (ES), first proposed by Doshi and Dealy in 1986. This is a transient strong flow that can be readily imposed in the laboratory using commercial strain-controlled (separate motor and transducer) rheometers which accurately measure both the (exponentially) time-varying torque and normal force exerted by the sample. Numerous studies over the years have proposed competing definitions of an appropriate material function for reporting data obtained from the exponentially time-varying imposed deformation. We revisit this unresolved challenge and carefully reconsider the exponential material kinematics involved and the proper definition of the corresponding material functions. We show that by understanding the time-varying eigenvalues and eigenvectors of both the shearing kinematics and the evolving state of stress in the sample it is, in fact, possible to use an exponential shear test to quantitatively map out \textit{both} the full flow curve (of shear viscosity vs. shear rate) and the steady planar extensional viscosity curve as a function of deformation rate. These findings are supported by numerical simulations of a number of nonlinear viscoelastic constitutive models, asymptotic analysis, as well as experiments with several polymeric liquids.

Parameterized Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Efficient Simulation of Embedded Microfluidic Chip Cooling

Weiheng Li, Zhiyao Jiang, Bai Song

Embedded microfluidic cooling is a promising technology for dissipating the intense heat generated by high-performance electronic chips. However, the design and optimization of such coolers impose prohibitive computational costs when using conventional numerical solvers. Here, we present a parameterized physics-informed neural network (PINN) as a deep learning framework for rapidly and accurately predicting the thermohydraulic performance of embedded manifold microchannels. By incorporating four geometric dimensions along with three spatial coordinates as the network inputs, the proposed model enables multiphysics prediction across a continuous design space. Remarkably, our predicted velocity and temperature fields differ from finite-element simulations by relative errors of only 8.1% and 5.6%, respectively, while reducing the computational time and cost by over four orders of magnitude. Key to this framework is a composite strategy combining geometric ansatz embedding, the SOAP optimizer, and a thermal-conductivity-based curriculum learning scheme, which helps overcome the convergence challenges inherent in training PINNs for complex three-dimensional flows. Notably, by leveraging a highly optimized JAX backend, the network achieves convergence on a single consumer-grade GPU in less than four hours. Our approach offers a physics-consistent, real-time surrogate model that can substantially accelerate innovations in advanced microfluidic cooling of future electronics.

Enhancing valley splitting in Si/SiGe Wiggle Well quantum dots through integrated stressor-gate architectures

E. S. Joseph, Talise Oh, D. E. Savage, D. S. Stone, DeAnna Campbell, Benjamin D. Woods

Engineering large and reproducible valley splittings is essential for the scalable operation of silicon spin qubits. In Si/SiGe quantum dot devices, valley splitting arises predominantly from atomic-scale disorder and is therefore inherently random, motivating approaches that introduce a large deterministic contribution. Strain-assisted Wiggle Wells provide a route to deterministic valley splitting by combining an oscillatory Ge concentration in the Si well with shear strain; however, generating the required strain in a manner compatible with large-scale architectures remains a challenge. Here, we propose patterned stressed thin films as a scalable approach for generating shear strain in the quantum well of Si/SiGe quantum dot devices. Finite-element simulations show that compressively stressed TiN films can generate shear strains exceeding 0.1\% at the quantum well, sufficient for deterministic valley splittings of hundreds of $μ\text{eV}$ in strain-assisted Wiggle Wells. We show that this strain can extend across multiple quantum dots and is maintained in realistic gate geometries, providing a fabrication-compatible approach for scaling deterministic valley splitting to quantum dot arrays.

SSF-Net: A Spatial-Spectral Fusion Network for Accurate PDE Operator Learning

Siqi Zhao, Flora D. Salim

Machine learning methods for solving partial differential equations (PDEs) significantly improve computational efficiency over traditional numerical solvers such as finite element method (FEM). However, PDE dynamics inherently span both local and global dynamics: disturbances propagate and decay gradually through the domain, while global coupling effects can influence the whole system simultaneously. Capturing both types of dynamics with a low complexity model remains a key challenge. To address this, we propose the Spatial-Spectral Fusion Network (SSF-Net), built around a dual-branch Spatial-Spectral Fusion (SSF) block comprising a spatial branch and a spectral branch. The spatial branch employs a multi-directional Mamba, which mitigates its inherent causal, single-direction scanning bias and capture local interactions with reduced directional bias at linear complexity. The spectral branch leverages a Fourier transform with a block-diagonal MLP to efficiently capture global spectral features with reduced parameter count and complexity. The SSF block is embedded in a multiresolution convolutional encoder-decoder, which extracts multi-scale features. We evaluate SSF-Net on a comprehensive PDE benchmark spanning multiple task types and assess its long-horizon stability. Compared with attention-based and convolution-based baselines, SSF-Net achieves lower prediction error with fewer parameters while retaining near-linear asymptotic complexity in the number of spatial tokens.

Mathematical analysis and vectorized FEM implementation of logarithmic strain space framework for finite strains: a case study on elastoplasticity

Miroslav Frost, Martin Kružík, Alexej Moskovka, Jan Valdman

We investigate the mathematical and computational aspects of the logarithmic strain space framework for finite-strain models in nonlinear continuum mechanics, focusing on the classical von Mises elastoplastic model with linear kinematic hardening. To overcome the lack of coercivity and lower-semicontinuity in the quadratic Hencky energy, we introduce higher-gradient regularization and prove the existence of energetic solutions for rate-independent quasistatic evolution under time-dependent Dirichlet loading. Complementarily, we develop a vectorized MATLAB finite-element implementation of the framework leveraging page-wise matrix functions and operations, and our custom vectorization library. Computational benchmarks show that local tensor operations are significantly accelerated, the logarithmic-strain approach does not substantially increase computational costs, and the implementation robustly handles large rotations and deformations.

The Outer Eshelby Problem in Amorphous Solids with Odd Dipole Screening

Avanish Kumar, Jin Shang, Itamar Procaccia

The so-called "2-dimensional Eshelby problem" was defined by JD Eshelby, where a circle is cut out of an elastic sheet, deformed into an ellipse, and forced back into the material. This creates a quadrupolar response which turned out to be very relevant for describing plastic deformations in amorphous solids. Moreover, the resulting strain field has been used to describe how elasto-plastic models respond to external strains. It is thus important to examine carefully the Eshelby problem in amorphous solids, especially when anomalous elasticity is at play. Here we solve the Eshelby problem in amorphous solids with both even and odd dipole screening and a complete analytical result is obtained for the displacement and strain fields. The analytical results are crucial for understanding the anomalous responses in displacement fields measured directly from experiments and numerical simulations. The computation of strain fields clearly demonstrates the deviations from their classical counterparts for perfectly elastic solids. The decomposition of the displacement field shows the excitation of higher-order Fourier modes. The presence of higher excitations other than the imposed Eshelby geometry therefore suggests the possible nonlinear mode coupling that can influence and modify the anomalous responses.

Finite-strain second-gradient elasticity with an energetic surface: existence of minimizers, incremental theory and elastocapillary beading

Michal Šmejkal, Martin Horák, Martin Kružík

We develop a finite-strain theory of second-gradient elasticity for solids with a material surface endowed with its own constitutive energy. The bulk second gradient plays two complementary roles. First, under suitable coercivity assumptions and convexity with respect to the second gradient, we prove the existence of minimizers for nonnegative, lower semicontinuous surface energy densities, without requiring convexity, polyconvexity, positive one-homogeneity, or an upper growth bound on the surface energy. Second, it introduces an intrinsic bulk length scale that affects the onset of instability and wavelength selection. To investigate these effects, we derive an incremental small-on-large formulation in the finitely deformed configuration and apply it to the elastocapillary beading of an axially stretched soft cylinder. For an infinite cylinder and the parameters considered, increasing the second-gradient modulus delays bifurcation and shifts the critical mode towards longer wavelengths. Above a finite threshold of this modulus, the critical stretch saturates in the long-wave limit. The model is implemented in the open-source finite element code OOFEM using C1-continuous isogeometric analysis. Finite-cylinder simulations yield onset stretches in close agreement with the analytical predictions. Selected post-bifurcation branches traced by arc-length continuation exhibit beaded states and supercritical bifurcation at small second-gradient moduli, whereas larger moduli are associated with subcritical bifurcation and the formation of distinct thick and thin segments.

Consistent Mapping for Fluid Structure Interaction with Geometrically Nonlinear Isogeometric Beams

Juan Ignacio Camarotti, Ricky Aristio, Roland Wüchner

Isogeometric beam formulations provide an efficient reduced-dimensional representation of slender structures, combining accurate geometry and high-order continuity with few structural degrees of freedom. Their application to fluid--structure interaction (FSI) requires transferring motion and loads between the one-dimensional beam centerline and the surrounding fluid interface. While mapping strategies exist for conventional finite element beams, geometrically nonlinear isogeometric formulations require operators consistent with their spline-based kinematics and distinct descriptions of cross-sectional rotation. This work develops consistent beam-to-surface mappings for geometrically nonlinear isogeometric Bernoulli and Timoshenko beams in partitioned FSI. Fluid-interface points are attached to the beam in the reference configuration and reconstructed using the nonlinear kinematics of each formulation. Consistent linearization yields a configuration-dependent mapping operator whose transpose ensures work-conjugate load transfer. The formulations are verified through analytical deformation tests, convergence studies, and virtual-work checks, and evaluated in beam-based adaptations of the Mok and Turek--Hron benchmarks and the Goland wing flutter benchmark. The results confirm accurate interface reconstruction and work-conjugate load transfer under large deformations, while demonstrating the applicability of both beam formulations to coupled FSI problems.

Law-Invariant Operators for Stochastic Mechanics

Vissarion Papadopoulos

A central objective in stochastic mechanics is to separate deterministic physical behavior from the probability laws governing uncertain inputs. While classical spectral approaches achieve this separation in linear settings, nonlinear responses introduce interactions beyond conventional second-order variability response functions (VRF). Inspired by the spirit of VRFs, this work establishes that, for a fixed physical system, uncertainty propagation is described by a law-invariant operator mapping input realizations to responses, governed by the underlying physics and the deterministic parameters of the system. Different probability laws affect the input sampling and resulting response statistics, but not the mapping operator. We establish this law invariance under complete, invertible coordinate transformations and develop complementary, Fourier, fixed-reference polynomial-chaos (PCE) and finite element method (FEM) transfer formulations. Fourier captures nonlinear cross-frequency interactions and recovers the classical VRF in the appropriate linear limit, PCE describes coupling among stochastic modes, while FEM expresses the deterministic inverse-stiffness action. Neural approximations of these deterministic operators are investigated through analytical and finite-element benchmarks, examining cross-law generalization, the influence of training distributions and stochastic excitation, and the prediction of response fields and statistics. The results demonstrate the potential for reusing learned physical operators across different stochastic laws without retraining, while highlighting the importance of adequate training coverage and the challenges of accurately estimating low-probability events.

This paper explores an unexpected connection between the quality of a neural network's (NN's) internal representation and its vulnerability to catastrophic forgetting. The representational forgetting hypothesis is that networks with unified, factored representation (UFR) can resist catastrophic forgetting without any modification to the underlying fine-tuning algorithm or network architecture. Further experiments show that a training loss that distills UFR representations into fresh NNs reduces forgetting; along with several additional controls, these results help isolate representation as the critical factor. Together these results suggest that representational quality is an underappreciated contributor to catastrophic forgetting, and that the encouragement and maintenance of such quality may require attending to what patterns a particular NN is ready to learn.
