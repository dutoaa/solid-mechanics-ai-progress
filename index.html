A deep energy method formulation for phase-field modelling of ductile fracture

Nojus Plungė, Peter Brommer, Rachel S. Edwards, Emmanouil G. Kakouris

This work extends the Deep Energy Method to phase-field modelling of ductile fracture in elasto-plastic solids. Its key contribution is an incremental DEM-based formulation that couples history-dependent J2 plasticity and plasticity-dependent fracture toughness with separate energy-optimised displacement and phase-field representations. The formulation combines the second-order Ambrosio-Tortorelli phase-field fracture model with small-strain J2 plasticity and linear isotropic hardening. A volumetric-deviatoric split defines the elastic crack-driving energy, while accumulated equivalent plastic strain governs the degradation of local fracture toughness. Plastic internal variables are updated locally through radial return. The reduced energy is minimised alternately with respect to the displacement and phase fields, represented by a ReZero residual neural network and spatially localised Gaussian radial basis functions with trainable centres, respectively. This split addresses the diffuse phase-field profiles observed in preliminary calculations with a shared global network. Alternating optimisation is combined with transfer learning to reuse trainable parameters and optimiser states across loading increments, while the converged constitutive history is carried forward separately at quadrature points. The numerical results demonstrate close agreement with finite-element reference solutions in predicting force-displacement responses, energy evolution, plastic deformation and crack propagation. The framework captures the main features of ductile fracture, although accuracy becomes more sensitive to loading increments near abrupt fracture events.

Orientation-Resolved Nanoindentation of Single Crystal Ti-15Mo: Inverted Elastic Anisotropy and Yield-Onset Behavior Beyond Simple CRSS Predictions

Kidus Abebe, David Gaetano, Aurelia Moriyama-Gurish, Jana Šmilauerová, Yanfei Gao, Udo D. Schwarz

Ti-15Mo (wt%) is a well-established $β$-stabilized alloy whose mechanical response is strongly governed by phase stability and deformation mechanisms that operate at the single-grain scale. Because these intragranular mechanisms are intrinsically anisotropic, understanding the orientation dependence of the elastic and plastic response is critical to the texture-engineered processing of wrought components. Here, we quantify the nanomechanical anisotropy of single-crystal Ti-15Mo by combining EBSD with nanoindentation to correlate hardness, indentation modulus, strain rate sensitivity, and indentation yield response with the crystallographic surface normal near $\langle111\rangle,\langle124\rangle,\langle011\rangle,\langle023\rangle$, and $\langle013\rangle$ directions. While the modulus anisotropy is small (~10%), contrary to the prediction for a cubic crystal with A>1, orientations near $\langle111\rangle$ exhibit the lowest moduli. Yield onset is likewise softest near $\langle111\rangle$ and spans 41%, while hardness spans only 6.6% and follows neither the modulus nor the yield ordering. The alloy also exhibits yield behavior not captured by the simple CRSS/Schmid-factor models evaluated here. Strain-rate-jump tests yield V*=18-31b$^3$, consistent with kink-pair-nucleation-controlled plasticity; based on V*, the orientations separate into two groups suggestive of glide-dominated versus twin/transformation-participating response. We reveal an inversion of the elastic anisotropy expected for a homogeneous cubic $β$-phase and an orientation-dependent yield-onset response that is not reproduced by the simple Schmid-factor models examined here. Retained athermal $ω$ is proposed as the contributor because $β\rightarrowω$ collapse occurs on {111}$_β$ planes and may provide anelastic compliance through the reversible $ω\rightarrowβ$ transformation.

A physics-coupled graph neural operator for mid-plane six-component stress tensor field prediction in concrete penetration

Wenpu Du, Peng Zhou, Yunlong Xia, Sinuo Xin, Congcong Zhang, Boyang Zhang

The penetration-resistant design of concrete structures requires the spatiotemporal evolution of the stress field within the target, whereas in design only the strength grade, the geometry and the projectile parameters are known, and the aggregate arrangement is not. Empirical formulae and scalar-output surrogates serve design screening, and mesoscale finite element models resolve the aggregate-scale response, but one solution cannot cover random aggregate samples at scale, and no framework links mesoscale heterogeneity to field-level prediction. A physics-coupled graph neural operator (PC-GNO) is proposed, which learns in function space the evolution of the mid-plane six-component stress tensor field. The training data are generated by full-scale, aggregate-resolved finite element models and comprise 359 cases at the impact velocities of 100, 135 and 165 m/s, corresponding to compressive strengths of 14, 23 and 48 MPa; the data source is verified by laboratory measurements. The coupling strength of message passing is constructed from the impedance contrast across the material interface, and the update is constrained by spectral normalization. Under the single-step primary setting, the mean accuracy of the three levels is 80.16% (91.45%, 77.80% and 71.22%), i.e. 27.81, 42.46 and 34.16 percentage points above the preceding-frame field of the same window; with 20 664 parameters it exceeds the 87.9% of the best Fourier neural operator configuration (about 812 times as many parameters) and the 71.0% of the closed-form linear regression. The output is the complete six-component tensor field rather than a fixed engineering quantity, so that strength criteria and energy indicators follow from one set of weights. For field-level response prediction in concrete penetration, this result provides a data-driven route, with conditions of applicability defined by the training and the evaluation.

LizTail: Unifying Shape Adaptation and Spatial Parallelism with Segment-Reconfigurable Systolic Arrays

Gang Liu, Chen Yang, Junyan Hu, Jianguo Chen, Lixin Duan

Systolic arrays are widely used to accelerate neural-network inference, yet fixed two-dimensional arrays struggle with the substantial diversity in both matrix geometry and available parallelism across CNNs and Transformer-based large language models. This mismatch is particularly severe for skinny matrices, small-batch autoregressive decoding, and operators containing multiple independent partitions, leading to low processing-element (PE) utilization and repeated spatial folding. Existing reconfigurable arrays typically improve either shape adaptation or distributed execution, whereas supporting both through fine-grained routing incurs considerable interconnect, buffering, and control overheads. We present LizTail (named after lizard tail autotomy, where predefined fracture planes allow segments to detach and remain independently active, analogous to LizTail's predefined reconfiguration boundaries), a multi-mode systolic array that enables constrained segment-level reconfiguration by partitioning a $128\times128$ array into 32 regular $128\times4$ segments and adding connectivity only at predefined endpoints. The resulting substrate supports trimming, long-path extension through $180^\circ$ turn-back connections, and distributed execution in which each subarray can itself be trimmed or extended. Differentiated PE datapaths and segment-aware CEB/SIB buffers provide the required compute and data-delivery flexibility without unrestricted PE-level routing. A hardware-constrained mapper selects the execution organization, dataflow, task partition, and tiling. Across representative workloads, LizTail achieves geometric-mean speedups of $5.02\times$ on CNN inference and $25.67\times$ on LLM decoding over a TPU-like array, while reducing EDP by up to $38.23\times$. LizTail is open-sourced at https://github.com/kmjbwxa/LizTail.

Low-cost non-iterative Sylvester solvers for dynamical low-rank approximation of reaction-diffusion equations

Joy Saha, Jun Yang, Peimeng Yin

In this work, we develop low-cost non-iterative Sylvester solvers for dynamical low-rank approximation of reaction-diffusion equations and incorporate them into a basis-update \& Galerkin mass-lumped finite element method (\mbox{BUG-MLFEM}). By incorporating mass lumping via Gauss-Lobatto quadrature, the spatial discretization yields a matrix differential equation equipped with diagonal positive-definite mass matrices. We establish the weighted dynamical low-rank approximation (WDLRA) within the Galerkin framework of the MLFEM system, proving the theoretical equivalence between the BUG integrator and MLFEM on low-rank subspaces, which guarantees the discrete well-posedness of the implicit BUG steps. To overcome the severe computational bottleneck of standard $O(m^3+n^3)$ Sylvester solvers, we develop tailored, low-cost non-iterative solvers that exploit the inherent sparse-by-small algebraic structure. Under Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions (BCs), the large coefficient matrices are banded, enabling efficient diagonalization of the small $r \times r$ factor matrix and reducing the per-step computational complexity to $O((m+n)r^2)$ and storage to $O((m+n)r)$. For periodic BCs, we combine this diagonalization strategy with a rank-2 Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury update to handle wrap-around corner entries at negligible additional cost. Numerical experiments demonstrate first-order temporal and $(k+1)$th-order spatial convergence of the proposed BUG-MLFEM in the discrete $L^2$ norm, where $k$ denotes the polynomial degree of the finite element space, together with significant computational speedups over existing low-rank integrators.

Neural Spline Flow Diffeomorphisms for Pullback Finite Element Methods on Complex Domains

Ioannis Kalogeris, Anaksagoras Spitas, Konstantinos Atzarakis, George Stavroulakis, Vissarion Papadopoulos

We propose a method for solving partial differential equations on complex domains by learning smooth, invertible mappings from a fixed reference domain using Neural Spline Flows (NSFs). Monotone rational-quadratic spline coupling layers ensure a strictly positive Jacobian determinant by construction, eliminating the need for Jacobian-sign penalties. A diffeomorphic pullback transfers the weak form of the PDE to the reference domain, enabling finite element analysis on reusable structured meshes. To generalize across shapes, a shared NSF is conditioned on low-dimensional latent codes learned through an auto-decoder framework; a subsequent encoder predicts these codes for unseen geometries in a single forward pass. For a given learned map, the pullback preserves the mathematical structure of the PDE and is exactly equivalent to solving on the domain represented by that map. Because the learned domain generally approximates the target geometry, geometric mapping error remains distinct from finite element discretization and quadrature errors. Numerical benchmarks show that sufficiently accurate learned mappings yield reliable finite element solutions. By avoiding repeated generation of unstructured meshes, the method addresses a bottleneck in simulation workflows. The fixed reference mesh also creates opportunities for efficient assembly, memory-aligned data access, and precomputed quadrature. The resulting framework assigns geometric complexity to the neural network while retaining conventional finite element discretization and convergence properties on the learned domain, with overall accuracy also governed by geometric fidelity.

LLM2R: From Large Language Models to 3D Reassemblers

Zhouhan Zhong, Qiuhong Shen, Xingyi Yang, Jinyi Xu, Xinchao Wang

3D part assembly aims to reconstruct a complete shape from a set of unassembled parts by predicting a rigid SE(3) transformation for each part. Because the placement of a part is rarely determined by its own geometry, the task demands reasoning over the entire part set, and existing methods, which rely on pairwise relations or iterative generation and verification, scale poorly to objects with many parts. We present LLM2R, which casts part assembly as sequence modeling with a pretrained decoder-only large language model (LLM): a Point-BERT encoder and a Q-Former compress each part into a block of geometric tokens, the blocks of all parts are concatenated, and a causal LLM adapted with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) processes the sequence in one forward pass. Causal attention, however, lets the hidden states of a part observe only the parts that precede it. LLM2R therefore appends a block of learnable summary tokens after the last part: these tokens follow every part, so their states aggregate the whole assembly at a constant token cost, and a part-aware pose head fuses each part's states with them before predicting its rotation and translation. One architecture thus handles semantic and geometric assembly and predicts all poses in a single non-autoregressive pass. LLM2R achieves the highest part accuracy and success rate on all three PartNet categories, the best rotation error, translation error, and part accuracy on PartNeXt, and the lowest translation error and fastest inference on Breaking Bad, where only a fracture-specialized method is more accurate. Its advantage grows with the number of parts: at 100 parts it exceeds GARF by 33.5 points in part accuracy on Breaking Bad and Assembler by 29.1 points on PartNet, and it transfers zero-shot to unseen categories with more than 16 points higher part accuracy than the strongest of three baselines.

FEM-Validated Real-Time Modelling of Soft Tactile Deformation

Silvia Terrile, Kaichen Shi, Taejun Kwon, Jiseok Lee, Nathan Lepora, Yeejin Lee

Accurate modelling of soft tactile sensors remains difficult because finite element methods (FEM) are computa- tionally expensive, while real-time simulators often lack fidelity for hyperelastic deformation. Large physical datasets are also impractical to collect because repeated testing can damage fragile sensors. We present a data-efficient framework that combines limited real-world validation, high-fidelity FEM, and graph neural networks (GNNs) for accurate real-time prediction of two-dimensional tactile deformation in a planar soft tactile structure. The key idea is to decouple validation from training: 82 camera-tracked contact configurations are used only to tune and validate an ANSYS FEM model, which then generates 768 synthetic contact scenarios for GNN training. We evaluate both forward prediction from marker deformation to contact localisation and inverse inference from contact configuration to marker deformation. The validated FEM achieves sub-0.5 mm error in more than 90% of validation cases, while the learned GNN surrogate reaches a mean absolute error of 0.32 mm with millisecond-scale inference, compared with minute-scale FEM simulation. Models trained entirely on synthetic FEM data retain sub-millimetre accuracy on real measurements from the 2D tactile structure, demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer within the tested planar setting. We further integrate the learned model into a real-time visualisation pipeline, enabling rapid deformation prediction for virtual prototyping and simulator-based design exploration without extensive physical testing.

Deep Learning-based Mechanical Characterization of Synthetically Generated Plant Fibers under Transverse Compression

Nikolaos Kegkeroglou, Bilal Sabillah, Myrto Inglezou, Vincent Placet, Céndric Clevy, Antonios Porichis

Plant fibers are increasingly used as sustainable reinforcements in composites, but the intrinsic variability of their microstructure causes substantial scatter in transverse mechanical behavior, complicating composite design. Characterizing this behavior relies on finite element (FE) simulations requiring several minutes of high-performance computing per fiber, making high-throughput characterization across realistic fiber populations impractical. In this study, we propose a deep learning framework replacing this FE bottleneck with a fast pipeline. It includes two coupled recursive U-Nets predicting the step-by-step evolution of fiber deformation and internal stress fields under transverse compression, followed by a regression CNN estimating the transverse Young's modulus from the final stress field. Unlike prior approaches predicting stress fields for fixed configurations or idealized geometries, our framework recursively resolves the full temporal stress evolution for synthetic fibers derived from real hemp cross-sections, with or without a lumen. Trained on 1,000 synthetic fiber geometries with FE ground truth, it reproduces the stress field sequence with a Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) of $0.9574 \pm 0.0074$ and a Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) of $0.0345 \pm 0.0079$. It estimates transverse Young's modulus with an $R^2$ of $0.938 \pm 0.024$, a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of $24.31 \pm 1.23$ MPa, and a Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) of $5.25 \pm 0.91\%$. Inference requires under one second per fiber versus five minutes per FE simulation, drastically reducing computational cost. This offers a fast, reliable tool for mechanical characterization of synthetic plant fibers, with promising applications in designing natural fiber-reinforced composites.

An algebraic multiscale framework for two-level overlapping Schwarz preconditioners

Filipe Cumaru, Hadi Hajibeygi, Cornelis Vuik, Alexander Heinlein

The robustness of two-level overlapping additive Schwarz (OAS) preconditioners for highly heterogeneous elliptic problems hinges on the choice of the coarse space. We propose an algebraic multiscale (AM) coarse space built from the system matrix alone through an algebraic formulation of the multiscale finite element method (MsFEM). The coarse basis functions are energy-minimizing extensions of the solutions of reduced boundary condition (RBC) problems on the subdomain interface, so that coefficient variations along the interface enter the coarse space. By posing the RBC problems on slabs that extend the interface, the construction also applies to general unstructured domain decompositions. Embedding the AM formulation in an energy-minimizing framework extends it to linear elasticity, and we show that existing condition number bounds for OAS with MsFEM coarse spaces carry over to the algebraic setting. For problems with high coefficient inclusions on the coarse nodes, the AM iteration count and condition number are unaffected by increasing the contrast. In 3D scalar diffusion with log-normal coefficients, AM needs about as many iterations as the generalized Dryja--Smith--Widlund (GDSW) coarse space, while solving a coarse problem ten times smaller. For linear elasticity, omitting the coarse basis functions associated with the rotational rigid body modes yields a fully algebraic coarse space that still scales well for homogeneous problems.

Two-level additive Schwarz preconditioners with GDSW coarse spaces for high-order discretizations in 2D

Tommaso Bevilacqua, Axel Klawonn, Martin Lanser, Adam Wasiak

Two-level overlapping Schwarz preconditioners with Generalized Dryja-Smith-Widlund (GDSW) and Reduced GDSW (RGDSW) coarse spaces for high-order finite element methods (FEM) and virtual element methods (VEM) are investigated and applied to the Poisson problem in 2D. The GDSW coarse space is a well-established robust choice for preconditioners in domain decomposition, while the RGDSW coarse space offers a competitive alternative by reducing the coarse problem dimension while maintaining robustness. On the theoretical side, a condition number bound for both preconditioners is proved for conforming $\mathbb{P}_k$ and $\mathbb{Q}_k$ finite element spaces with explicit dependence on the polynomial degree $k$. Through numerical experiments on structured triangular and quadrilateral meshes for FEM and on polygonal meshes for VEM, it is demonstrated that both GDSW and RGDSW maintain scalability properties across polynomial orders up to $k=9$ for selected degrees. The RGDSW approach achieves a reduction in the coarse problem dimension by a factor of approximately three compared to GDSW while maintaining competitive iteration counts.
