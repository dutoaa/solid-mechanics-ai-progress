Fuse-PINN: Combining Finite Element Methods and Physics-Informed Neural Networks to enable massively parallel simulations of non-linear PDEs

Stanislas Pamela, Nicolas Pailliez, Emmanuel Franck, Victor Michel-Dansac, Laurent Navoret, Vignesh Gopakumar

Research in the domain of Physics-Informed Neural Networks applied to PDE modelling has advanced significantly in recent years, providing a wide ecosystem of novel methods. However, one frequent limitation is the inability of PINNs to scale out on High-Performance Computing architectures to address more ambitious challenges, particularly non-linear physics models with turbulent dynamics in large spatial domains. Typical studies restrain demonstrations to toy models with simple physics equations, few variables, and rarely any complex non-linear dynamics evolved over long timescales. Part of the AI community has even adopted the idea that "PINNs don't scale". This work proves otherwise. We present a mathematically robust formulation of Finite-Element-Method for Neural Networks (FEM-NN) implemented into a comprehensive, modular framework: Fuse-PINN. This formulation provides the basis for a sclable parallelisation of Physics-Informed Neural Networks on GPU-based HPC systems. Furthermore, an Energy Natural Gradient Descent optimiser is implemented to allow high-precision convergence. Fuse-PINN is demonstrated on physics models relevant to Magnetic Confinement Fusion, including non-linear convective models with fine-scale dynamics. Near-perfect scaling is achieved on multi-GPU runs with increasing problem size.

Thermodynamically consistent neural constitutive models for elastoplasticity via eigen-representations

Harikrishnan Vijayakumaran, Shunyu Yin, Martin Abendroth, Miguel A. Bessa, Karl A. Kalina

Neural network material models can represent yield surfaces and hardening laws with far more flexibility than classical closed-form plasticity formulations, but training such a model end-to-end through an implicit return map introduces non-trivial challenges. This work presents a thermodynamically consistent neural constitutive model for rate-independent, isotropic elastoplasticity that jointly learns a flexible yield function together with its evolution laws. For non-associative flow, the yield function extends the convex flow potential by a jointly trained additive term, which renders the model thermodynamically consistent by construction. Isotropy and convexity are enforced simultaneously across all learnable components through a permutation-invariant, spectral (eigenvalue-based) representation. Training proceeds through a backward-Euler return map, with gradients propagated by the implicit function theorem together with an eigenvalue-degeneracy-aware scheme for safe differentiation. The robustness and generality of the approach are demonstrated by reproducing known constitutive models, including non-associative flow, learning material behavior from stress--strain data extracted from a full-field finite element simulation, and capturing unknown non-associative behavior from homogenized representative volume elements of a foam. Finally, the proposed neural plasticity model is evaluated within finite element simulations to solve boundary-value-problem under plane stress.

Making Plasticity Visible: A Case Study for High-Temperature Exposed Aluminum Alloy

Bakhtiyar Mammadli, Karol Frydrych, Casim Yazici, Azad Mammadli, Kazimierz Skrobas, Siddharth Suman

Understanding the onset of plastic deformation and the early development of strain localization is essential for characterizing material behavior and identifying failure-prone regions. However, these transitions are not always distinguishable from conventional full-field strain measurements. This study investigates a data- driven framework combining Digital Image Correlation (DIC) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to extract temporal and spatial deformation signatures during uniaxial tensile loading. The methodology is evaluated using AA6063-T5 aluminum alloy specimens in the as-received condition and following thermal exposure at 100~$^\circ$C, 200~$^\circ$C, and 300~$^\circ$C. PCA applied to sequential strain fields reveals a characteristic peak in the second principal component projection near the yielding transition, showing agreement with the conventional 0.2\% offset criterion across the investigated conditions. Furthermore, principal component maps extracted from high-temporal-resolution strain fields preceding this transition highlight localized deformation features associated with subsequent failure regions. These features are more clearly distinguishable than competing strain concentrations in the corresponding instantaneous strain maps. The results indicate that PCA-derived deformation signatures remain identifiable despite thermally induced variations in mechanical response. This approach demonstrates the potential of unsupervised dimensionality reduction for extracting physically interpretable information on plastic deformation and early strain localization from experimental full-field measurements.

Unified constitutive model of body-centered cubic metals informed by discrete dislocation dynamics

Jiarui Zhang, Yin Zhang

Predicting plastic flow in body-centered cubic metals across temperature and strain-rate regimes requires distinguishing structural resistance from kinetic contributions. Here, we develop a temperature dependent mobility law for screw dislocation, integrating thermal activation theory and relativistic effects. Through discrete dislocation dynamics simulations, uniaxial tension and compression simulations were conducted on configurations with different initial dislocation densities, elucidating the correlation between plastic strain rate and key parameters such as stress, temperature, and dislocation density. Subsequently, a unified constitutive model incorporating temperature and strain rate is derived based on microscopic mechanisms. The model naturally bridges microscopic mechanisms to continuum response, resolving the additivity dilemma by establishing explicit physical criteria for how structural resistances and competing kinetic mechanisms superimpose. Through rigorous bottom-up simulations, we quantitatively capture the crossover point where phonon drag overtakes thermal activation, providing a mechanism-based explanation of anomalous thermal hardening. This work enables physically consistent predictions of strength and creep behavior across broad temperature and strain-rate regimes, advancing beyond phenomenological descriptions toward transparent, mechanism-based alloy design.

A scaling law for numerical computation of fracture stress intensity factors

Ronghao Cui, Jan Martin Nordbotten, Ivar Stefansson, Inga Berre

Numerical simulations of fracture propagation require accurate computation of stress intensity factors (SIFs) to determine whether a pre-existing fracture will propagate. Even though displacement solutions from well-established numerical methods generally converge with mesh refinement, the convergence behavior of SIF estimates remains less understood. In this work, we propose a scaling law to improve the performance of SIF extraction using two-point displacement correlation methods. This scaling law relates sampling distance from the fracture tip to mesh resolution. It is developed by balancing the asymptotic truncation error against the numerical discretization error. Three numerical discretization methods--standard quadratic finite elements, quadratic finite elements with quarter-point elements, and a multi-point stress approximation finite volume method--are employed to validate the proposed scaling law on fracture benchmark problems under pure-mode and mixed-mode loading. We find that the conventional nearest-tip sampling strategy fails to compute convergent SIF estimates. In contrast, the proposed scaling-based approach yields decreasing errors in the SIF estimates as the mesh is refined. Across all three discretization methods, the proposed scaling law captures the sampling locations required for convergent SIF extraction, suggesting a general numerical characteristic of near-tip SIF extraction.

Machine learning of strength surfaces enables high regularization length phase-field fracture

S Chockalingam, Aditya Kumar, Adrian Buganza Tepole

Classical phase-field models of brittle fracture capture toughness-controlled crack growth but not the material's strength surface, which governs crack nucleation. The theory of Kumar et al. (2020) incorporates the strength surface through a nucleation driving force, and Chockalingam (2025) derived this driving force for arbitrary strength surfaces described by $n$ material parameters, with which the phase-field strength surface (PFSS) converges to the material strength surface as the regularization length $\varepsilon \to 0$ and matches it exactly at $n$ chosen states for any $\varepsilon$. In practice, however, the strength surface must be supplied as an analytic form chosen and calibrated case by case, and for the widely used analytic surfaces ($n=2$) accurate strength reproduction requires $\varepsilon$ commensurate with the Irwin characteristic length $l_{ch}$, imposing prohibitive mesh requirements for large structures. We introduce a neural-network (NN) strength function that is learned directly from strength data, is physics-constrained by construction, and provides far more calibration parameters than analytic surfaces; the calibration is generalized to any number of strength states and to enforce openness in chosen directions. A single architecture captures synthetic data from the Mohr-Coulomb, Drucker-Prager, Mogi-Coulomb and 3D Hoek-Brown surfaces and experimental data for graphite, natural rubber and styrene-butadiene rubber. The resulting PFSS is accurate up to $\varepsilon = 10\,l_{ch}$, whereas that of the exact analytic surfaces performs comparatively poorly even at $\varepsilon = 0.5\,l_{ch}$. Finite-element simulations validate the theory across fracture regimes. By decoupling strength fidelity from the regularization length, the approach substantially reduces the mesh resolution required for phase-field fracture simulations of large structures.

A mesh-free deep energy method for phase-field modeling of thermally driven brittle fracture

Han Zhang, Mehrisadat Makki Alamdari, Babak Shahbodagh, Mohammad Vahab, Cosmin Anitescu, Timon Rabczuk

Phase-field simulation of thermally driven fracture requires resolving localized crack bands whose paths are unknown in advance and thermal boundary layers whose thickness evolves during the transient. Neural energy-based computations of thermal fracture have so far started from defects placed in advance. This paper proposes a mesh-free deep energy method based on multiresolution neural representations that predicts spontaneous crack nucleation, crack spacing, and selective crack propagation and arrest under transient thermal loading. Two networks, each with its own multiresolution encoding, represent the displacement and phase fields and the transient temperature, and the mechanical energy and the incremental functional of the backward Euler heat equation are estimated on integration points redrawn at every iteration. Restricting irreversibility to the crack set, carrying the optimizer state across load steps and decaying the learning rates within each step help prevent spurious damage accumulation. On two notched plates the initiation peak loads agree closely with an independent finite element reference. On a quenched glass strip under an analytical temperature field the crack path changes from straight to oscillatory and then to branched, with an oscillation threshold close to the linear stability prediction. Under a prescribed boundary temperature a quenched ceramic slab forms a crack array of the type observed in experiment, with a spacing close to that of a published benchmark. On the slab and a quenched annulus the arrays are denser than those of a finite element computation on the same material field. No crack paths, nucleation sites or crack-adapted meshes are prescribed.

Dynamic Prandtl-Reuss Thermoplasticity with Degenerating Yield Constraints

Sebastian Owczarek

We study a three-dimensional dynamic thermoplastic system with a temperature-dependent convex yield set whose deviatoric section may collapse completely at a finite temperature. The underlying constitutive model is rate-independent Prandtl-Reuss perfect plasticity, without Kelvin-Voigt viscosity, hardening, or a safe-load condition. We construct global approximation-generated relaxed entropy-energy states by a one-parameter Temam-Norton-Hoff-Galerkin scheme with an undiscretized heat equation and untruncated plastic heat production. The logarithmic temperature is strongly compact in $L^2(0,T;L^q(Ω))$ for every $q<6$ and in $L^p((0,T)\timesΩ)$ for every $p<8/3$. An entropy-weighted work estimate yields convergence of the overstress at rate $O(\sqrt{\log n/n})$ in every fixed finite Lebesgue space and identifies stress admissibility. The constitutive limit comprises a distributional plastic balance, finite support-weighted measures, and compact-fibre lifts carrying exact normality and maximum plastic work. A locally finite lift on the positive-radius fibres unifies the level-restricted limits at every continuity level. At the macroscopic level, however, the available compactness does not identify a canonical stress-plastic-flow pairing. Moreover, an explicit smooth state satisfying the intrinsic relaxed balance conditions shows that these conditions do not imply macroscopic Prandtl-Reuss normality. In the fully collapsed regime, an energy-entropy rigidity argument determines the evolution and yields approximation-generated examples in which the plastic flow need not be a finite measure or the displacement need not retain its full boundary trace. Thermal concentration and plastic entropy production remain explicit throughout.

A Unified Framework for First- and Second-Order Energy-Stable Parametric Finite Element Methods for Planar Willmore Flow

Xiaoli Li, Yujing Yan

The construction of structure-preserving schemes for planar Willmore flow that are second-order accurate in time and avoid large coupled nonlinear solves remains challenging. In this work, we develop a unified framework for first- and second-order fully discrete energy-stable parametric finite element schemes for planar Willmore flow with a length-penalization term. The framework is based on a new equivalent reformulation involving a global auxiliary variable. The main novelty lies in the second-order construction, which combines midpoint curvature quantities weighted by elementwise arc-length Jacobian ratios with a compatible discrete tangent to obtain exact discrete chain rules for the bending energy and curve length. A further contribution is to show that both schemes satisfy discrete modified energy dissipation laws that yield the same type of a priori bounds on the discrete solution as the original energy law. They also retain Barrett--Garcke--N{ü}rnberg tangential motion to promote favorable mesh distribution. Each stage reduces to two linear solves with exactly the same coefficient matrix and one scalar nonlinear solve. We rigorously prove unique solvability of the linear subproblems under suitable assumptions and derive computable sufficient conditions for local existence and uniqueness of the scalar solution near unity. Numerical experiments demonstrate the expected temporal accuracy, energy dissipation, good mesh quality and computational efficiency.

Ultrasound Elastography: Excitation Methods and Low-Cost Alternative

Corin F. Otesteanu

Ultrasound elastography (or elasticity imaging) is a method that characterizes the tissue mechanical properties with the purpose of aiding the diagnosis of certain pathologies. Many novel clinical applications use the assumption that these tissue properties are changed in the presence of pathological processes. Characterization of tissue mechanical properties is achieved by imaging the response of the tissue to an induced stress or excitation. A plethora of approaches have been used in the scientific community to induce excitation in tissue, which usually require additional tools or devices than are costly. Furthermore these are usually integrated in the imaging pipeline using additional software or hardware for control. The induced displacements are used to solve the elasticity inverse problem (IP) using a finite element method (FEM) approach. Preliminary elasticity reconstructions of a phantom that mimics tissue are similar to when using a dedicated actuator as means of inducing harmonic displacements, illustrating the feasibility of our low-cost ultrasound elasography approach.

Physics-constrained differentiable causal discovery of constitutive mechanisms through boundary value problems

Michael A. Kraus

Data-driven constitutive model discovery is recast as causal discovery of mechanism parent sets using time-unrolled structural causal models. Hard-coding balance laws and constitutive skeletons, candidate graphs score environment--mechanism and state--state edges via weak-form equilibrium gaps evaluated from full-field displacements, avoiding the need to measure stresses or internal variables. An adaptive lasso and a NOTEARS-type constraint ensure graph acyclicity and an explicit evaluation order, treating climate-chamber protocols as interventions. Automatic differentiation yields the score's Gauss--Newton matrix as a local identifiability certificate, driving greedy D-optimal test design. In a synthetic study on a hygro-thermo-viscoelastic damaging plate (8 true edges among 13 candidates), interventional data recover the exact true graph in 7 of 10 runs (0.01px displacement noise) with zero attribution error. Conversely, observational data with a temperature--moisture correlation of $-0.998$ fail entirely because confounded scores are 250--380 times flatter. Observationally discovered models reproduce training climates (3.6% error) but mispredict hot and humid climates by up to 41.5% (16.9% average), whereas interventionally discovered models stay within 3.2%. A correlation sweep shows the certificate successfully predicts attribution errors, though weak-form score noise bias remains a primary limitation. Computed without knowledge of the truth, the D-optimal design selects test sets ranked second or third out of 126 candidates.

Mechanical behaviour of the intact brain-skull interface under tension, compression and shear

Sajjad Arzemanzadeh, Karol Miller, Ron Kikinis, Adam Wittek

The brain-skull interface governs the boundary/contact conditions of the brain in computational head models, yet its mechanical behaviour remains poorly characterised experimentally. We characterised the brain-skull interface by testing brain-skull complex samples (skull bone, meninges and brain tissue in their native arrangement) excised from 19 sheep cadaver heads under uniaxial tension and compression at strain rates of 0.002, 0.02 and 0.2 s$^{-1}$ and simple shear at 0.02 s$^{-1}$. Histology confirmed that the meningeal layers remained intact and attached to the brain and skull after excision. We acquired magnetic resonance images of every sample, reconstructed their geometry and developed a sample-specific finite element model of each experiment. We represented the brain tissue using a second-order Ogden hyperelastic model and the brain-skull interface using a cohesive zone model. Using inverse finite element analysis, we first calibrated the brain tissue parameters for each subject. Keeping these parameters fixed, we then identified the interface parameters from the corresponding brain-skull complex specimen. The brain tissue shear modulus increased with strain rate from 0.94 $\pm$ 0.16 kPa at 0.002 s$^{-1}$ to 1.60 $\pm$ 0.30 kPa at 0.2 s$^{-1}$. In contrast, the interface properties (maximum normal traction 2.35 $\pm$ 0.81 kPa, maximum tangential traction 4.14 $\pm$ 0.97 kPa, and fracture energy 0.35 $\pm$ 0.14 N m$^{-1}$) showed no significant dependence on strain rate or loading mode. In all samples, failure occurred within the subarachnoid space. Neither a rigid brain-skull connection nor frictionless sliding contact with separation reproduced the measured mechanical response or failure mode of the brain-skull interface. Our experimentally derived cohesive zone model provides a realistic representation of the brain-skull interface for computational head models.

Adapting a Vision Foundation Model for Fracture Characterization in Acoustic Image Logs

Guangyu Wang, Xiaodong Ma, Xinming Wu

Natural fractures (NFs) and drilling-induced tensile fractures (DITFs) in acoustic image logs provide insights into rock mass structure and in situ stress, but manual characterization is time-consuming. Deep learning can automate this task, although expert-labeled data are scarce. We investigate whether adapting a pretrained vision foundation model (VFM) outperforms task-specific models trained from scratch with limited annotations. Using 80 annotated acoustic image samples, we fine-tuned DINOv3, pretrained on satellite imagery, and evaluated it on four boreholes excluded from fine-tuning. The adapted model estimated NF and DITF parameters comparably to human interpretation. Relative to U-Net, YOLOv8, and Swin Transformer models trained from scratch, it yielded fewer false positives and negatives and more accurate fracture counts and orientations. However, its performance advantage generally narrowed as training sample size decreased. We also assessed decoder architecture, fine-tuning strategy, and image enhancement. A progressive upsampling decoder with full fine-tuning performed best among the DINOv3 configurations tested, while fracture-focused image enhancement did not consistently improve accuracy. These results demonstrate the potential of VFM adaptation for fracture characterization in acoustic image logs with limited labeled data and suggest a transferable workflow for other annotation-scarce geoscientific imaging tasks.
