Microstructural Generative Inverse Models for Additive Manufacturing Digital Twins

Pablo Luna Falcon, Jason Mayeur, Amirkoushyar Ziabari, Vincent Paquit, Patxi Fernandez-Zelaia

Metal additive manufacturing (AM) offers substantial advantages to the fabrication of complex structural components, but its widespread adoption is slowed due to complexities in the process-structure-property relations which is the cause for uncertainty and drives risk. Accurately quantifying these complex relations solely via experimental workflows is time consuming and costly. Integrated computational materials and engineering (ICME) frameworks address this challenge by leveraging advanced computational tools, such as crystal plasticity finite element models (CPFEM), to predict critical mechanical properties directly from microstructural data. However, these frameworks require a large datasets that are often difficult to obtain, and their predictive accuracy depends on the availability of realistic AM microstructure representations. Generative deep learning models are a promising approach towards synthesizing AM microstructures which contain complex morphological and crystallographic features. Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs), in particular, show a strong potential to capture the inherent complexities of AM structures. In this work, we demonstrate that DDPMs can reliably synthesize digital-twin orientation-imaging micrographs, and we support this claim with quantitative evidence of the fidelity of the generated samples. Additionally, we establish a classifier-free guidance framework for solving complex inverse problems such as property-to-structure and process-to-structure mappings, and we showcase a potential application on a qualification-relevant uncertainty quantification problem. Our work highlights the potential of AI-based generative modeling as a key component of computational frameworks for accelerating AM materials qualification and certification.

Scalable Stress-Constrained Topology Optimization: A Super-resolution and Simultaneous Approach Based on Machine Learning

Xiangyu Sun, Shirin Hosseinmardi, Ramin Bostanabad

Large-scale topology optimization (TO) remains computationally demanding, especially for stress-constrained problems. To address this challenge, we propose the simultaneous neural topology optimization (SNTO) framework where we represent state, adjoint, and design variables with custom neural networks (NNs) whose parameters are jointly optimized within a single optimization loop that leverages continuous adjoint analysis and automatic differentiation. This optimization minimizes a multi-component loss function that combines the design objective, an aggregation of the local stress constraints, and the potential energy functionals. Our NNs have a unique architecture that accelerates convergence while enabling the networks to capture sharp gradients and boundaries which are critical to stress-constrained TO. To achieve scalability to millions of degrees of freedom (DoF), we develop a hardware-aware training scheme in which the design domain is partitioned into subdomains that are distributed across multiple GPUs. To control peak GPU memory usage, we further slice each subdomain into smaller regions whose loss gradients are evaluated sequentially and accumulated to update the NN parameters. Unlike domain decomposition approaches, our partitioning naturally ensures field continuity across the subdomains and does not require the introduction of additional terms into the loss function. For validation, we first evaluate the proposed framework on various 2D stress-constrained benchmarks and compare our results to a conventional finite element-based approach, showing that SNTO produces super-resolution designs with sharper boundaries, smaller gray areas, and lower volume fractions. We then consider large-scale 3D problems with up to eight million collocation points and distribute the training over up to eight GPUs.

Synthetic Dataset Generation, Physical Consistency and Active Learning for Shape-Informed Surrogate Modeling of Hemodynamics

Francesco Romor, Richard Schussnig, Charles A. Taylor, Gerhard A. Holzapfel, Alfonso Caiazzo

Model order reduction has the potential to enable real-time patient-specific assessment of cardiovascular flows. However, it still faces significant challenges stemming from the geometric variability of vascular anatomies. Building upon the SynthAorta dataset (Bosnjak et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imag. 45(1), 2026) of physiological aortic geometries, we generate an extensive dataset of physiologically consistent computational fluid dynamics solutions through an automatic boundary-condition adjustment procedure. A common reference geometry allows all patient-specific anatomies to be represented by displacement fields, providing point-to-point correspondence without the need for geometric registration. Based on this dataset, we develop Graph U-Net and Transformer U-Net surrogate models to predict velocity and pressure fields at peak systole. We further investigate two strategies to enhance data efficiency and prediction accuracy: active learning and differentiable physical consistency constraints based on the finite element residual. The results show that active learning is strongly regime-dependent: while it offers little benefit over random sampling during early training, it improves performance during fine-tuning through gradient- and subspace-based acquisition strategies. Similarly, physical consistency constraints prove to be substantially more effective as a fine-tuning objective than as a regularizer applied throughout training. Finally, out-of-distribution studies demonstrate that the primary limitation of surrogate models lies in the geometric support of the underlying parametrization rather than in the neural network architectures themselves. The proposed framework combines synthetic dataset generation, geometry-aware surrogate modeling, physics-informed optimization, and active learning into a scalable workflow for inter-patient hemodynamic prediction.

Robust topology optimization of isogeometric Kirchhoff--Love shells by the Speed Method

Philipp Eisenhardt, Fernando Hübner Scherer, Barbara Wohlmuth, Andrei Constantinescu

This paper presents a framework for robust topology optimization of Kirchhoff-Love (KL) multi-patch shells using isogeometric analysis and non-matching meshes and provides a CAD-compatible method. The material distribution is optimized via a NURBS-based level set method, enabling accurate boundary representation and seamless export to standard CAD/CAM workflows. The shape derivative is computed using the speed method, yielding a volume-based formulation that avoids boundary approximations and aligns with the isogeometric principle of coarse, high-order discretizations. This eliminates the need for local refinement, adaptive quadrature, or trimming as the descent direction for the compliance and volume objectives are obtained through a biharmonic equation. The framework is extended to robust optimization under uncertainty, accounting for stochastic loading and material properties via Monte Carlo sampling and Gaussian Random Fields. Numerical examples demonstrate the method's efficiency and robustness across single- and multi-patch shells, reproducing literature results with fewer elements than traditional FEM or refined IGA approaches. The developed methods and tools can be directly extended to problems involving stress-based optimization and risk-averse metrics. The numerical implementations are open-source and available on GitHub.

Hyperreduction accelerates finite element model updating for the discovery of neural materials

Hagen Holthusen, Matthias Knipper, Kevin Linka

Finite element model updating is among the most general approaches to discover constitutive models from structural experiments: since the displacement field is computed rather than measured, it accommodates arbitrary geometries, boundary conditions, and sparse or noisy data. However, every loss evaluation requires a nonlinear finite element solve per load step, and training neural materials requires many such evaluations. Intrusive model order reduction lowers this cost while retaining boundary conditions and variational structure. How can a reduced model remain accurate while the network weights change at every training step? We combine proper orthogonal decomposition and energy-conserving sampling and weighting with adjoint sensitivities of the reduced model, and adapt both the trial space and the sampled elements along the optimization path. The constitutive law is a polyconvex neural network: a logarithmic normalization term, together with a zero-anchored input convex architecture and a growth term, renders the energy polyconvex, coercive, and non-negative by construction, independent of the trained weights. We demonstrate the approach on two experimental datasets and one synthetic three-dimensional example. For a two-phase specimen, training time decreases from nine hours to 41 minutes compared with a full-order identification, without loss of accuracy. In the three-dimensional example, six modes replace 48 672 degrees of freedom, 116 of 15 625 elements suffice to evaluate the internal forces, and training takes 13 minutes. In all cases, the identified material reproduces the measured reaction force and predicts the displacement field from the force alone. Hyperreduction makes the discovery of neural materials from structural data affordable while retaining the advantages of finite element model updating.

Elastic theory of ideal liquid crystal elastomers

Zumrat Usmanova, Ruobing Bai

A liquid crystal elastomer (LCE) exhibits a two-way coupling between its mesogen order and network elasticity: the mesogen order sets the undeformed network conformation, and the network deformation reorients the mesogens. This coupling gives rise to large, reversible stimuli-responsive actuation, as well as to soft elasticity, a finite deformation at nearly zero stress. This article reviews the elastic theory of ideal LCEs, bridging single-chain statistics and continuum constitutive models. The "ideal" elastic behavior rests on the assumption of anisotropic Gaussian chains that retain no memory of the crosslinking state, such that rotating the director costs no free energy. The corresponding neo-classical free energy of a monodomain LCE is non-quasi-convex, with a continuous family of stress-free ground states, leading to thermally induced spontaneous deformation and soft elasticity accommodated by coexisting stripe domains. The quasi-convex relaxation of the free energy classifies the behavior of polydomain LCEs into three phases, each with a distinct macroscopic mechanical response depending on the stretch state. The ideal elastic theory can be further extended to incorporate additional behaviors and physical processes, including thermo- and photomechanical coupling, Frank elasticity from director distortion, semi-soft or non-soft behavior arising from network nonideality, and temperature- and rate-dependent viscoelasticity.

Rinkle Juneja, Pablo Seleson, Samuel Temple Reeve, Justin Weinmeister, Gary Staebler

Plasma-facing materials (PFMs) are the primary armor materials in fusion reactors, protecting reactor wall components from extreme heat fluxes, energetic particles, and intense neutron irradiation. These harsh conditions induce thermo-mechanical stresses, irradiation damage, and overall degradation in PFMs. Among these, thermo-mechanical damage induced by extreme heat flux is one of the key factors limiting the lifetime of PFMs. We present a modeling and simulation approach for predicting thermo-mechanical cracking and damage in tungsten, a leading PFM candidate, under fusion-reactor-relevant heat fluxes. Specifically, we study damage induced by cyclic electron beam (e-beam) exposure under different combinations of base temperature and heat flux. We carry out component-scale thermo-mechanical simulations using CabanaPD, a peridynamics fracture mechanics code, informed by experimental data for recrystallized pure tungsten from the JUDITH e-beam facility. The simulations compare well with the experimental classification and enable identification of cracking thresholds across a range of heat fluxes and base temperatures. These predictions can provide detailed insights into the spatial distribution and temporal accumulation of cracks and damage, while offering a pathway for screening candidate PFMs and quantifying their lifespan under fusion-relevant conditions.

Can Language Models Get "Aphasia" From Neuronal Ablation?

Pratik Worah, Randall Bateman

Progressive neuronal loss is the hallmark of neurodegenerative disease, yet how loss of neurons shapes the pattern of cognitive decline remains poorly understood. Here we systematically ablate neurons (computational units) in language models spanning recurrent long short-term memory (LSTM) and Transformer architectures and apply limited fine-tuning as a simple proxy for compensatory plasticity. With whole-unit ablation, LSTMs degrade gradually with syntactic and semantic faculties declining in parallel across mild-to-moderate damage. By contrast, Transformers exhibit late threshold-like collapse under feedforward multi-layer perceptron (MLP) ablation and smoother sigmoid-like decline under attention-head ablation. Qualitatively, MLP-ablated models continue to make within-category semantic substitutions (semantic paraphasias) alongside rising non-responses, paralleling Wernicke-type aphasia, while attention-head ablation produces a trend toward loss of semantic specificity similar to the semantic variant of primary progressive aphasia (svPPA). These findings indicate that feedforward and attentional pathways have functionally distinct failure modes, suggesting that architecture-dependent failure profiles may offer new computational perspectives on aphasic syndromes and neurodegenerative diseases as an in silico model. Notably, early accuracy retention under Transformer attention-head ablation qualitatively parallels pre-symptomatic reserve before the 40--60% neuronal loss reported by Gomez-Isla et al. (1996) when clinical symptoms emerge in Alzheimer's disease, whereas the steadier LSTM decline parallels the symptomatic progression described by DeKosky and Scheff (1990) and Terry et al. (1991).

Xun Sun, Thanh Thi Nguyen, Thuy-Trang Vu

Continual learning requires a model to keep acquiring new skills and knowledge without compromising its existing capabilities. Parametric methods suffer from catastrophic forgetting and loss of plasticity. To effectively combine their complementary strengths, we propose HERD (Harness-Evolving Recursive Distillation), a hybrid continual learning framework that learns efficiently in the harness and internalizes experience into cumulative capabilities via distillation. On the harness side, HERD continually refines its skills to capture reusable patterns, while retaining high-residual examples that the skills fail to capture in a bounded memory. On the parameter side, HERD performs drift-constrained distillation with dual anchors, substantially reducing forgetting over long recursive runs. On the 25-stage task stream of TRACE+17, under fixed memory and compute budgets, HERD matches the strongest parametric baseline in new-task acquisition while exhibiting the least forgetting and the strongest retention of general capabilities.

A high-order multi-scale method and its error analysis for nonlocal-in-time fractionally damped wave problems in composite materials

Gehao Wang, Hao Dong, Yufeng Nie, Junzhi Cui

Significant spatial heterogeneity and nonlocal history-dependent attenuation jointly govern wave propagation in composite materials, making full-scale direct numerical simulation (DNS) computationally prohibitive. This study proposed a high-order multi-scale (HOMS) method for nonlocal-in-time fractionally damped wave problems with highly oscillatory and discontinuous material coefficients. Using a multi-scale asymptotic expansion approach, the macroscopic homogenized equation, and first- and second-order correction terms are derived to construct the high-accuracy HOMS solution. Moreover, a local pointwise error analysis identifies the leading residual eliminated by this HOMS solution and validates its local physical balance preservation. Then, a global energy-norm error estimate establishes the $O(\varepsilon)$-order convergence rate for the HOMS solution. Subsequently, an efficient spatiotemporal multi-scale algorithm integrating the finite element and finite difference methods is designed, which employs a novel fully discrete $L2\text{-}1_σ$-Newmark scheme, and corresponding unconditional shifted-energy stability and explicit convergence rates are also obtained for the macroscopic homogenized displacement and velocity, respectively. Both two- and three-dimensional examples are presented to demonstrate the accuracy, efficiency, reduced cost, and robustness of the proposed HOMS method. This work provides an effective multi-scale tool for nonlocal-in-time wave simulation in engineering applications.

Conservative Finite Element Methods for Kirchhoff-Type Equations

Benjamin Dörich, Felix Krumbiegel, Jemal Rogava, Andreas Rupp, Zurab Vashakidze

We construct and analyse a fully discrete scheme for a Kirchhoff-type nonlinear integro-differential equation in two spatial dimensions with time-dependent coefficients. The scheme combines a symmetric three-layer time discretization of Crank--Nicolson type with a conforming finite element method of arbitrary polynomial degree $p$ and a Picard iteration for the nonlinearity. We prove that the Picard iteration is well defined and, that the iterates converge to the Ritz projection of the exact solution measured in fractional-power norms of the discrete Laplacian. Running the same energy argument in the unshifted norms, and under the same hypotheses, we then obtain the optimal order $\mathcal{O} ( h^{p+1} + τ^2 )$ in $L^2$ and $\mathcal{O} ( h^{p} + τ^2 )$ in $H^1$ for the full error. For the homogeneous problem with constant coefficients, the exact solution of the scheme moreover preserves a discrete energy identically. Numerical experiments with manufactured solutions reproduce the predicted second order in time and the full spatial order $h^{p+1}$ in the $L^2$-norm.

Mixed finite element methods for a heat-assisted model of sedimentation

Julio Careaga, Cristian Inzunza

A model for the consolidation-sedimentation process incorporating thermal effects is analysed. The model is motivated by novel applications to heat-assisted sedimentation of mineral suspensions. A system of convection-diffusion equations for the concentration and temperature are coupled to flow equations for the volume-average velocity and pressure. The model is supplemented with Dirichlet and mixed boundary conditions. A quasi-stationary approach of the system is analysed by means of a mixed variational formulation, where additional variables such as the pseudostress tensor and variables for the gradient of the temperature and concentration are introduced. The existence and uniqueness of the system is shown making use of the Babu{\v s}ka-Brezzi theory and Banach's fixed-point theorem, under hypotheses of smallness of the data and additional regularity on the solutions. A discrete formulation based on finite element spaces is proposed and analysed, and the existence of solutions is achieved thanks to Brouwer's fixed-point theorem. Additionally, Céa and a priori estimates are derived for the mixed formulation. Finally, numerical examples are provided to demonstrate the performance of the model and to confirm the theoretical rates of convergence.

Concurrent two-scale topology optimization of printable bone scaffolds via rectified-flow generation and exact latent-space refinement

Reza T Batley, Sourav Saha

Bone scaffolds must reconcile a macroscale mechanical response with microscale geometric feasibility, yet conventional approaches to their design restrict attainable constitutive properties. This work presents a concurrent two-scale topology optimization framework; a conditional rectified flow generates periodic lattice microstructures, which are then refined in latent space by exact finite-element homogenization sensitivities. The resulting unit-cell geometries and their spatial assignments are jointly optimized under mechanical and feature-size constraints. In a distal rabbit femur benchmark, all eight flow-started refinements produce printable cells with maximum relative errors under 1\% across six prescribed engineering moduli. Analysis of the learned representations reveals distinct architectural families in latent space. Further, the attention heads of the rectified-flow transformer specialize for spatial shifts -- learning the analogue of a finite-difference stencil -- and coordinate symmetries, with ablations showing their influence on generated topologies. These results establish a route to printable, mechanically constrained scaffold designs. The framework is general enough for extending to many other multiscale structural design applications including topology-microstructure co-design in metal additive manufacturing.
