Universal neural network approximation of symmetric polyconvex energies and envelopes

Florian Gammer

Symmetric polyconvexity provides a sufficient condition for weak lower semicontinuity in small-strain elasticity and a tractable lower bound for the relaxation of non-(quasi)convex energies. Since classical algorithms for computing such functions can be computationally expensive, neural network approximations have recently emerged as an alternative. A central difficulty with establishing such neural network based approaches for symmetric polyconvexity is the additional monotonicity property. As we show, the corresponding weight restrictions on ReLU-based input-convex networks do not yield universal approximation of the lifted representers. Here, we introduce symmetric polyconvex neural networks (SPCNNs) for constitutive modeling and approximation of symmetric polyconvex envelopes. The deep architecture combines convexity with the monotonicity required of a representer in the space of minors. We prove a universal approximation theorem for symmetric polyconvex energies, without requiring isotropy, and quantify the number of affine pieces attainable by the lifted network and derive reduced architectures for isotropic energies. Numerical examples illustrate stress-based regression and approximation of the symmetric polyconvex envelope of a double-well potential. Symmetric polyconvexity is guaranteed by construction, while the accuracy of the fitted energies and envelopes is assessed numerically.

Microstructural Generative Inverse Models for Additive Manufacturing Digital Twins

Pablo Luna Falcon, Jason Mayeur, Amirkoushyar Ziabari, Vincent Paquit, Patxi Fernandez-Zelaia

Metal additive manufacturing (AM) offers substantial advantages to the fabrication of complex structural components, but its widespread adoption is slowed due to complexities in the process-structure-property relations which is the cause for uncertainty and drives risk. Accurately quantifying these complex relations solely via experimental workflows is time consuming and costly. Integrated computational materials and engineering (ICME) frameworks address this challenge by leveraging advanced computational tools, such as crystal plasticity finite element models (CPFEM), to predict critical mechanical properties directly from microstructural data. However, these frameworks require a large datasets that are often difficult to obtain, and their predictive accuracy depends on the availability of realistic AM microstructure representations. Generative deep learning models are a promising approach towards synthesizing AM microstructures which contain complex morphological and crystallographic features. Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs), in particular, show a strong potential to capture the inherent complexities of AM structures. In this work, we demonstrate that DDPMs can reliably synthesize digital-twin orientation-imaging micrographs, and we support this claim with quantitative evidence of the fidelity of the generated samples. Additionally, we establish a classifier-free guidance framework for solving complex inverse problems such as property-to-structure and process-to-structure mappings, and we showcase a potential application on a qualification-relevant uncertainty quantification problem. Our work highlights the potential of AI-based generative modeling as a key component of computational frameworks for accelerating AM materials qualification and certification.

Scalable Stress-Constrained Topology Optimization: A Super-resolution and Simultaneous Approach Based on Machine Learning

Xiangyu Sun, Shirin Hosseinmardi, Ramin Bostanabad

Large-scale topology optimization (TO) remains computationally demanding, especially for stress-constrained problems. To address this challenge, we propose the simultaneous neural topology optimization (SNTO) framework where we represent state, adjoint, and design variables with custom neural networks (NNs) whose parameters are jointly optimized within a single optimization loop that leverages continuous adjoint analysis and automatic differentiation. This optimization minimizes a multi-component loss function that combines the design objective, an aggregation of the local stress constraints, and the potential energy functionals. Our NNs have a unique architecture that accelerates convergence while enabling the networks to capture sharp gradients and boundaries which are critical to stress-constrained TO. To achieve scalability to millions of degrees of freedom (DoF), we develop a hardware-aware training scheme in which the design domain is partitioned into subdomains that are distributed across multiple GPUs. To control peak GPU memory usage, we further slice each subdomain into smaller regions whose loss gradients are evaluated sequentially and accumulated to update the NN parameters. Unlike domain decomposition approaches, our partitioning naturally ensures field continuity across the subdomains and does not require the introduction of additional terms into the loss function. For validation, we first evaluate the proposed framework on various 2D stress-constrained benchmarks and compare our results to a conventional finite element-based approach, showing that SNTO produces super-resolution designs with sharper boundaries, smaller gray areas, and lower volume fractions. We then consider large-scale 3D problems with up to eight million collocation points and distribute the training over up to eight GPUs.

Francesco Romor, Richard Schussnig, Charles A. Taylor, Gerhard A. Holzapfel, Alfonso Caiazzo

However, it still faces significant challenges stemming from the geometric variability of vascular anatomies. Building upon the SynthAorta dataset (Bosnjak et al., IEEE Trans. Med. The proposed framework combines synthetic dataset generation, geometry-aware surrogate modeling, physics-informed optimization, and active learning into a scalable workflow for inter-patient hemodynamic prediction.

Multi-Material Topology Optimization of Phononic Crystals for Targeted Elastic Wave Bandgap

Bir Bahadur Chaudhary, Wenfeng Li, Ting Chen, Xinmai Yang, Candan Tamerler, Bin Ouyang

Phononic crystals are periodic elastic structures that exhibit frequency bandgaps within which elastic waves cannot propagate. The formation and width of these bandgaps depend on the spatial distribution of material properties within periodic phononic unit cells. In this work, we propose novel topology optimization formulations to achieve multi-material design of phononic crystals for bandgap optimization at targeted frequencies, where we discover the influence of spatially varying properties of base materials on the band structures that block and permit elastic wave propagation. Specifically, we model wave propagation by Floquet-Bloch theory and solve the resulting eigenvalue problem by the finite element method along the boundary of the irreducible Brillouin zone. We first propose a multivariate weight-based method via a novel interpolation scheme that maps the design variables of an arbitrary number of candidate materials into effective density and stiffness for elastic wave-propagation calculations, where bandgaps arise from the contrast in the stiffness and inertia among material constituents. We then develop two formulations for bandgap optimization. The first formulation maximizes the gap between two adjacent frequency bands using a bound-based approach, where we introduce two auxiliary scalar variables as additional design variables and incorporate them directly into the design objective, yielding a linear, differentiable design objective, with the eigenfrequencies entering the formulation through associated constraints. The second formulation places a bandgap over a user-defined target frequency range through a dynamic band-pairing strategy, where we adaptively identify the relevant pair of adjacent bands amid optimization iterations. Towards the end, we validate the bandgap performance of our designs against the transmission loss spectra computed from COMSOL Multiphysics.

Robust topology optimization of isogeometric Kirchhoff--Love shells by the Speed Method

Philipp Eisenhardt, Fernando Hübner Scherer, Barbara Wohlmuth, Andrei Constantinescu

This paper presents a framework for robust topology optimization of Kirchhoff-Love (KL) multi-patch shells using isogeometric analysis and non-matching meshes and provides a CAD-compatible method. The material distribution is optimized via a NURBS-based level set method, enabling accurate boundary representation and seamless export to standard CAD/CAM workflows. The shape derivative is computed using the speed method, yielding a volume-based formulation that avoids boundary approximations and aligns with the isogeometric principle of coarse, high-order discretizations. This eliminates the need for local refinement, adaptive quadrature, or trimming as the descent direction for the compliance and volume objectives are obtained through a biharmonic equation. The framework is extended to robust optimization under uncertainty, accounting for stochastic loading and material properties via Monte Carlo sampling and Gaussian Random Fields. Numerical examples demonstrate the method's efficiency and robustness across single- and multi-patch shells, reproducing literature results with fewer elements than traditional FEM or refined IGA approaches. The developed methods and tools can be directly extended to problems involving stress-based optimization and risk-averse metrics. The numerical implementations are open-source and available on GitHub.

Nested Sparsity for Scalable Pretraining and Continual Learning

Tianzhe Chu, Druv Pai, Yi Ma

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) transformers, the standard backbone for modern large-scale deep learning, employ conditional computation: only a sparse subset of model weights is used in each forward pass. In MoE transformers, this sparsity arises in the MoE operator, which generalizes the multi-layer perceptron in the classical transformer. In this work, we explore an alternative mechanism for conditional computation that analogously generalizes the attention operator. The sparsity induced by this mechanism naturally nests (i.e., hierarchically composes) with the sparsity induced by the MoE operator. We show that a transformer employing this nested sparsity mechanism, which we call Nestor, performs better than a conventional MoE transformer at equal compute and equal sparsity in language model pretraining. We also show that Nestor is well-suited for continual pretraining: it can be adapted to longer contexts more effectively than conventional MoEs, as well as adapt more efficiently to new domains while forgetting less on old domains. Furthermore, we demonstrate that these benefits persist as model size, dataset size, and context length grow. Our results demonstrate the potential of alternative conditional computation mechanisms for deep neural network architectures. Code and checkpoints are available on GitHub at https://github.com/Ma-Lab-Berkeley/Nestor .

Relativistic Plasticity

Joshua, Cadogan, Nils Andersson, Ian Hawke, Thomas Celora

The elastic crust of a neutron star has long been established as a key player in relevant aspects of neutron star physics. However, the state-of-the-art theory of elasticity is restricted to the perturbative domain and does not account for permanent plastic deformations which should be relevant for pulsar glitches, magnetar flares, and the continuous emission of gravitational waves. Plasticity, however, is a historically challenging phenomenon to model - even in the Newtonian regime. This is owed mainly to its dissipative character and the multiscale methods required to consistently reproduce observed phenomena. In this work, we address this first challenge by making use of recent developments in the variational modelling of dissipative fluids to extend the theory of relativistic elasticity to include plastic phenomenology. The resulting theory possesses the same freedoms as the modern Newtonian formalism, while now being compatible with general relativity. The second law of thermodynamics is then used to introduce the yield criterion of plasticity theory in the relativistic setting.

An Arbitrary-Order Augmented Lagrangian Discrete De Rham Scheme for Contact Mechanics with Tresca Friction

Jérôme Droniou, Raman Kumar, Roland Masson

We design and analyse an arbitrary-order augmented Lagrangian Discrete de Rham polytopal scheme for contact mechanics in fractured media. The fractures are represented as objects of codimension one in the matrix, a linear elastic medium, and a Tresca friction model is adopted when contact between fracture walls occurs. Both the normal Signorini-type/traction constraint and the Tresca friction law are recast as projection identities, and are combined into a single augmented Lagrangian bilinear form. The displacement is discretised by a DDR space of arbitrary polynomial degree $k\ge 0,$ enriched on the fracture faces by bubble degrees of freedom that ensure stability via the existence of a discrete lifting between the Lagrange multiplier space and the discrete jump of the displacement along the fracture. We establish an abstract error estimate yielding optimal order convergence for the displacement and the Lagrange multiplier variables. Extensive numerical experiments confirm the theoretical analysis and demonstrate the practical accuracy and robustness of the scheme.

REDAR: Reinforcement-Driven Adaptive Regularisation for Class Incremental Learning

Akila Ayanthi, Darshana Priyasad, Tharindu Fernando, Sridha Sridharan, Clinton Fookes, Peyman Moghadam

Regularisation-based class-incremental learning (CIL) methods control the stability-plasticity trade-off through fixed coefficients tuned offline, independently of the continual learning objective and blind to phase-specific variations in data volume, task difficulty, and class similarity. We argue this is a fundamental mismatch and propose REDAR: Reinforcement-Driven Adaptive Regularisation for Class Incremental Learning, which treats regularisation strength selection as a sequential decision problem. At each incremental phase, a lightweight reinforcement learning (RL) agent observes a compact state of current performance metrics and incoming data statistics, and outputs the distillation coefficients for that phase. To respect CIL's constraint on historical data access, the policy is trained entirely on pseudo-CIL tasks constructed from the initial phase, then deployed on the incremental sequence without further adaptation. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet demonstrate that REDAR reduces forgetting and improves forward transfer over fixed and heuristic baselines, and that policies trained on short pseudo-task horizons transfer effectively to longer incremental runs.

Hyperreduction accelerates finite element model updating for the discovery of neural materials

Hagen Holthusen, Matthias Knipper, Kevin Linka

Finite element model updating is among the most general approaches to discover constitutive models from structural experiments: since the displacement field is computed rather than measured, it accommodates arbitrary geometries, boundary conditions, and sparse or noisy data. However, every loss evaluation requires a nonlinear finite element solve per load step, and training neural materials requires many such evaluations. Intrusive model order reduction lowers this cost while retaining boundary conditions and variational structure. How can a reduced model remain accurate while the network weights change at every training step? We combine proper orthogonal decomposition and energy-conserving sampling and weighting with adjoint sensitivities of the reduced model, and adapt both the trial space and the sampled elements along the optimization path. The constitutive law is a polyconvex neural network: a logarithmic normalization term, together with a zero-anchored input convex architecture and a growth term, renders the energy polyconvex, coercive, and non-negative by construction, independent of the trained weights. We demonstrate the approach on two experimental datasets and one synthetic three-dimensional example. For a two-phase specimen, training time decreases from nine hours to 41 minutes compared with a full-order identification, without loss of accuracy. In the three-dimensional example, six modes replace 48 672 degrees of freedom, 116 of 15 625 elements suffice to evaluate the internal forces, and training takes 13 minutes. In all cases, the identified material reproduces the measured reaction force and predicts the displacement field from the force alone. Hyperreduction makes the discovery of neural materials from structural data affordable while retaining the advantages of finite element model updating.

Elastic theory of ideal liquid crystal elastomers

Zumrat Usmanova, Ruobing Bai

A liquid crystal elastomer (LCE) exhibits a two-way coupling between its mesogen order and network elasticity: the mesogen order sets the undeformed network conformation, and the network deformation reorients the mesogens. This coupling gives rise to large, reversible stimuli-responsive actuation, as well as to soft elasticity, a finite deformation at nearly zero stress. This article reviews the elastic theory of ideal LCEs, bridging single-chain statistics and continuum constitutive models. The "ideal" elastic behavior rests on the assumption of anisotropic Gaussian chains that retain no memory of the crosslinking state, such that rotating the director costs no free energy. The corresponding neo-classical free energy of a monodomain LCE is non-quasi-convex, with a continuous family of stress-free ground states, leading to thermally induced spontaneous deformation and soft elasticity accommodated by coexisting stripe domains. The quasi-convex relaxation of the free energy classifies the behavior of polydomain LCEs into three phases, each with a distinct macroscopic mechanical response depending on the stretch state. The ideal elastic theory can be further extended to incorporate additional behaviors and physical processes, including thermo- and photomechanical coupling, Frank elasticity from director distortion, semi-soft or non-soft behavior arising from network nonideality, and temperature- and rate-dependent viscoelasticity.

Multi-material topology optimization exploits the high-dimensional design space of spatially varying material constituents by simultaneously optimizing material selection and spatial distribution over the design domain, offering great flexibility in controlling natural frequencies. Numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness and applicability of our proposed method.

WiMoT: Replay-Free Continual Wi-Fi Sensing via Moment Transport

Chunchun Yang, Yang Cao, Zhitang Chen

Continual Wi-Fi sensing must balance plasticity, learning new rooms or users, against stability, keeping earlier ones, yet nodes often cannot retain channel state information (CSI) for privacy and storage reasons. We propose WiMoT (Wi-Fi Moment Transport), which stores the per-class feature mean and covariance of every earlier domain instead of samples.
