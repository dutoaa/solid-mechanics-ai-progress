Thermodynamically consistent neural constitutive models for elastoplasticity via eigen-representations

Harikrishnan Vijayakumaran, Shunyu Yin, Martin Abendroth, Miguel A. Bessa, Karl A. Kalina

Neural network material models can represent yield surfaces and hardening laws with far more flexibility than classical closed-form plasticity formulations, but training such a model end-to-end through an implicit return map introduces non-trivial challenges. This work presents a thermodynamically consistent neural constitutive model for rate-independent, isotropic elastoplasticity that jointly learns a flexible yield function together with its evolution laws. For non-associative flow, the yield function extends the convex flow potential by a jointly trained additive term, which renders the model thermodynamically consistent by construction. Isotropy and convexity are enforced simultaneously across all learnable components through a permutation-invariant, spectral (eigenvalue-based) representation. Training proceeds through a backward-Euler return map, with gradients propagated by the implicit function theorem together with an eigenvalue-degeneracy-aware scheme for safe differentiation. The robustness and generality of the approach are demonstrated by reproducing known constitutive models, including non-associative flow, learning material behavior from stress--strain data extracted from a full-field finite element simulation, and capturing unknown non-associative behavior from homogenized representative volume elements of a foam. Finally, the proposed neural plasticity model is evaluated within finite element simulations to solve boundary-value-problem under plane stress.

Operator Learning of Microstructure-Dependent Internal-Variable Models for Inelastic Homogenization: Approximation and Complexity

Lianghao Cao, George Stepaniants, Andrew M. Stuart

We study the learning of homogenized constitutive laws for inelastic heterogeneous materials, aiming to replace costly concurrent unit-cell simulations with surrogates that generalize across loading histories and microstructures. We focus on the recurrent neural operator framework, motivated by the explicit form of homogenized Kelvin--Voigt linear viscoelasticity. In this framework, internal variables enable a Markovian approximation of loading-history dependence, and neural operators represent the microstructure-dependent constitutive response. For periodic Kelvin--Voigt viscoelasticity, we prove a universal approximation theorem showing that the model can approximate the homogenized constitutive law to any prescribed accuracy, uniformly in time and over a wide set of admissible strain trajectories and microstructural property fields, in two and three dimensions. This comprises a significant extension of the existing, somewhat limited, one-dimensional theory that uses explicit solutions for piecewise constant materials. Instead, in two and three dimensions, we adopt a more versatile approximation based on the spectral representation of the memory kernel that arises from homogenization. This also enables us to give an explicit bound on the internal-variable dimension, sufficient for approximation, exhibiting logarithmic growth in the inverse relative stress error and in material contrast. Numerical experiments on viscoelasticity and crystal plasticity complement the theory, illustrating the roles of internal-variable dimension and sample complexity in learning homogenized responses from unit-cell data. Theoretical results are formally verified in Lean 4.

Numerical analysis of coupled flow, deformation and frictional contact mechanics in a fractured porous medium

Marius Nevland, Kundan Kumar, Inga Berre, Jakub Wiktor Both, Eirik Keilegavlen

This paper studies the numerical approximation of a mathematical model of coupled flow and viscoelastic deformation in a fractured porous medium including fracture contact mechanics. We consider a mixed-dimensional model, where the fracture is modeled as a co-dimension one object within the porous medium. This model couples Darcy flow in the matrix and fracture, linear viscoelasticity of the matrix and frictional contact mechanics of the fracture surfaces; the contact conditions include normal compliance and a Coulomb friction law. We propose and analyze a fully discrete numerical scheme for this problem, using conformal finite elements in space and the implicit Euler method in time. Existence and uniqueness of the discrete solution is established, and stability and a priori error estimates are derived. A numerical experiment is performed in which numerical error estimates are computed and compared to the theoretical results.

Adapting a Vision Foundation Model for Fracture Characterization in Acoustic Image Logs

Guangyu Wang, Xiaodong Ma, Xinming Wu

Natural fractures (NFs) and drilling-induced tensile fractures (DITFs) in acoustic image logs provide insights into rock mass structure and in situ stress, but manual characterization is time-consuming. Deep learning can automate this task, although expert-labeled data are scarce. We investigate whether adapting a pretrained vision foundation model (VFM) outperforms task-specific models trained from scratch with limited annotations. Using 80 annotated acoustic image samples, we fine-tuned DINOv3, pretrained on satellite imagery, and evaluated it on four boreholes excluded from fine-tuning. The adapted model estimated NF and DITF parameters comparably to human interpretation. Relative to U-Net, YOLOv8, and Swin Transformer models trained from scratch, it yielded fewer false positives and negatives and more accurate fracture counts and orientations. However, its performance advantage generally narrowed as training sample size decreased. We also assessed decoder architecture, fine-tuning strategy, and image enhancement. A progressive upsampling decoder with full fine-tuning performed best among the DINOv3 configurations tested, while fracture-focused image enhancement did not consistently improve accuracy. These results demonstrate the potential of VFM adaptation for fracture characterization in acoustic image logs with limited labeled data and suggest a transferable workflow for other annotation-scarce geoscientific imaging tasks.

When Does the Real Wiring Win? Testing connectome structure-function claims against unmeasured physiology

Yeyuan Liu

Connectome-constrained models are often used to argue that a measured wiring diagram outperforms shuffled or rewired wiring, and hence that structure shapes function. Such comparisons can confound wiring with how the models are tuned: a connectome fixes who connects to whom, not the excitability, gains or plasticity rates the model also needs, and modellers often tune these until the real-wiring model works. We separate three estimands: physiology tuned on the real wiring and transferred to nulls (P1), each wiring tuned on its own (P2), and P2 restricted to physiology consistent with independent data (P3). The biases involved are known in machine learning (under-tuned baselines, inherited weights, winner's curse); we measure what they do to biological conclusions, using synthetic ground truth, placebo nulls and three Drosophila circuit models. Some claims survive every protocol we test: segregated reward and punishment dopamine improves learning, and head-direction topography is required for a persistent bump. Others do not. In the mushroom body, the real Kenyon-cell output wiring beats a cross-subtype identity shuffle by 0.09 under P1 and P2 but loses to it by 0.09 under P3 (95% CIs exclude zero; held-out runs confirm). The verdict is set by the share of alpha'beta' cells among active Kenyon cells, which P1 selects low; at the share implied by imaging (about 44%) the real-wiring model fails to learn, exposing a model inadequacy rather than a deficit of the fly's wiring. In flyvis, P1 penalises a mirrored connectome, which has a constructed solution within 0.07 (median) of the real error, more than a spatially shuffled one in 50 of 50 members; the ranking survives ten-fold longer retraining but depends on the checkpoint at fifty-fold. A credible structural claim needs matched optimisation, data-constrained physiology and a model that still works there.

Accelerating matrix-free fast diagonalization and preconditioned linear solvers for higher-order finite element discretized elliptic problems on distributed GPUs

Gourab Panigrahi, Phani Motamarri

The time to solution of an iterative solver depends on the number of iterations and the cost per iteration. This paper develops algorithms that reduce both for elliptic problems discretized with high-order continuous finite elements on distributed GPUs, implemented in a unified matrix-free code supporting CUDA, HIP and SYCL. A distributed direct fast-diagonalization (FD) Poisson solver provides exact solutions on tensor-product meshes with direction-wise grading and coefficients varying along one coordinate direction. Its tensor data stay distributed, moved between slab layouts by collective transposes or kept in place by a Schur-complement approach. It is also an exact coarse solver. A two-level hybrid Schwarz preconditioner with FD cell solves (HS-FD) is extended to 2:1 hanging-node octrees with exact local cell inverses, while a \(p\)-multigrid preconditioner with Chebyshev--Jacobi smoothing (CJ-\(p\)MG) resolves hanging-node constraints at every level within fused kernels. Both approaches exploit FP32, where cell shapes and coefficients allow, within an FP64 conjugate-gradient iteration, adaptively select replicated or distributed coarse levels, support block solves for multiple right-hand sides and determine all parameters from the operator without case-specific tuning. Comparisons with Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients (Jacobi PCG), MFEM, nekRS and deal.II span uniform, graded and octree meshes, layered coefficients, and four applications across NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs. The direct solver is two to three orders of magnitude faster than Jacobi PCG on uniform meshes. HS-FD converges in 11 iterations for an octree generalized Poisson problem across 8 to 1024 MI250X graphics compute dies. The results guide solver selection by mesh structure and coefficients

Design of Self-Folding Origami Architectures for Biomedical Implants: A Finite Element Simulation Framework

Suhas Chandrashekar, Thomas Stieglitz, Prasanth Velvaluri

Minimally invasive brain implants require compact deployment geometries and controlled shape transformation for delivery through confined vessels. Origami-inspired structures enable large shape changes from compact to tailored deployed configurations, but implementation at microscale dimensions remains challenging, as conventional designs rely on predefined mountain and valley folds that become increasingly difficult to fabricate and assemble as unit-cell size is reduced to meet clinical requirements. To address this, we propose a self-folding methodology based on thermally induced stresses in thin-film bimetallic composites. Pairing the shape memory alloy Nitinol (NiTi) with molybdenum or stainless steel (SS316L) exploits their differing thermal expansion coefficients to generate localized hinge bending, enabling deterministic, autonomous folding into prescribed mountain and valley configurations. Using finite element simulations, we systematically optimize hinge length, layer thickness, width, and shape, identifying a hinge geometry achieving a ~50% reduction in unit-cell dimensions relative to previously reported designs. Integration into a complete waterbomb origami unit cell confirms that the strip-level folding mechanism scales to produce coordinated, self-folding deformation across the tessellated structure. These results demonstrate the feasibility of stress-induced, self-folding thin-film origami architectures built from clinically established biomaterials, providing a scalable design framework for next-generation, active neurovascular implants with integrated sensing and monitoring functionality.

Henrique Oliveira

Few-shot abstract reasoning on ARC-AGI requires inducing algorithmic transformations from 2 to 5 demonstration pairs without task-specific pretraining. Because few-shot demonstrations under-constrain the hypothesis space, program synthesis suffers from the Rashomon effect (predictive multiplicity): heuristic search finds spurious programs with low training error that fail on test inputs, while unconstrained library growth degrades latency. We introduce F3-AGI v4.0, a CPU-native neuro-symbolic and active inference architecture comprising three mechanisms: (1) F3-Metabolic-Optim, combining mistake-gated plasticity, Fisher-EMA preconditioning, and virtual ATP gating; (2) Adaptive Complexity-Guided Program Synthesis (AACR) with a Cycle-3 ResidualPatchSynthesizer and smallest-pair early rejection; and (3) a 512D L2-normalized random projection driving Expected Free Energy (EFE) exploration with dead-signature pruning. Anchor Behavior, Not Parameters: Rehearsal-Free Continual Learning with a Single Shared Prompt

Duy-Cuong Bui, Trung-Anh Dang, Vincent Nguyen, Duc V. Le, Ngoc-Son Vu

Prompt-based continual learning adapts a frozen pre-trained vision transformer by tuning a small set of prompt tokens. Recently, one-prompt methods which share one single prompt across all tasks make this paradigm maximally parameter efficient. The open question is how a shared prompt can keep the knowledge of old classes while being updated for new ones. Existing methods protect old knowledge in parameter space, where they freeze task-specific prompts, expand new components, or sparsely gate which tokens receive gradients. However, this method sacrifices plasticity, yet the drift it still permits in shared tokens can corrupt the prompt's function on past classes. What must be preserved is the prompt's behavior. We propose TEA-Prompt, which couples (i) a token evaluation module that assigns each prompt token a continuous, input-conditional relevance score injected into the attention logits, softly specializing tokens across tasks without discarding any of them, and (ii) an attention-behavior anchoring loss that constrains each token's attention output around class-specific query regions using only compact Gaussian query statistics without stored old images, weighted by the token's past relevance. Anchoring the attention value allows the parameters to continue updating freely, as long as their interaction remains unchanged. TEA-Prompt achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with markedly lower forgetting on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, and CUB-200 with only 0.38M parameters. Code is publicy available at https://github.com/cuonga1cvp/TEA-Prompt.git

Multiscale LegONet: Learning Coefficient-Dependent Local Spaces for Composable Multiscale Finite Elements with Localized Error Bounds

Yueqi Wang, Cindy Xiangrui Kong, Guang Lin

We introduce multiscale LegONet (Ms-LegONet), a plug-and-play learned block for heterogeneous elliptic problems. One network per spatial dimension is trained once on local coefficient patches and reused, without retraining, across coefficient layouts, sources, boundary data and domain sizes within the trained coefficient class. On a fixed primitive patch the network maps the local coefficient to a zero-boundary space, the only learned object. The multiscale basis functions are the harmonic extensions of a fixed set of boundary traces, each a Galerkin solve in that space. Shared interface traces enforce continuity, larger blocks are assembled by recursive static condensation without any parent-scale network, and a localized Céa bound attributes the error of the learned spaces primitive by primitive. The training labels are harmonic responses of single patches, so no global solve enters the training. Against MsFEM, GMsFEM, the neural-operator element method and an end-to-end Fourier neural operator, Ms-LegONet is the most accurate at equal unknowns: its $L^2$ error is $4$ to $150$ times below the baselines on two-dimensional mosaics of $256$ high-contrast primitives, and its median error stays within $0.1$ percentage points of that of exact local spaces in three-dimensional layered media. For each new coefficient it is $1.6$ to $2.3$ times faster than a fine-grid direct solve in two dimensions. In three dimensions it computes the many-query port response of a chain $30$ to $900$ times faster than finite element solves on the same or a finer mesh, at a relative error of $10^{-4}$ against their $6\%$ and $1.6\%$.

Are UFOs Real? Introducing Unified Factored Organizers

Thomas X. Sheppard, Alon Albalak, Joel Lehman, Kenneth O. Stanley

Implicit in much research in deep learning is the assumption that if a network achieves low loss, then it must have learned a good representation of its training data. Recent work by Kumar et al. (2025, arXiv:2505.11581) questions this assumption, arguing that networks trained with conventional gradient descent achieve low loss despite learning fractured entangled representations (FER), leaving related concepts disconnected and unrelated concepts entangled inappropriately. Kumar et al. further show that dramatically better representations are possible: a network evolved through an open-ended, human-in-the-loop process learned unified factored representations (UFR) that capture the regularities of the domain far better than a counterpart with nearly identical outputs trained with conventional gradient descent. Inspired by UFR, this paper introduces unified factored organizers (UFOs), networks trained to model regularities in the representations of other networks. A network can then be trained both to achieve low loss on its task objective and to align its representations with the model learned by the UFO. Networks trained with UFOs are shown to learn qualitatively superior representations in an image-regression domain where representations are easily interpretable. To provide quantitative evidence for the impact of UFOs on representation learning, a transformer is trained with and without UFO regularization to grok a modular arithmetic problem set. When combined with weight decay, UFOs can reduce the number of transformer training steps needed to grok by more than 10x relative to weight decay alone. Without weight decay, UFOs can reduce the number of transformer training steps needed to grok by more than 100x relative to an unregularized baseline. If UFOs are real, they suggest a new paradigm where loss functions themselves are trained to learn an effective model of good representation.

A transversely isotropic constitutive model for semisoft elasticity in main-chain liquid crystal elastomers

Donghao Li, Xin Yi, Fan Feng

Monodomain main-chain liquid crystal elastomers exhibit pronounced stiffness anisotropy and broad semisoft plateaus following finite onset strains. At fixed chain anisotropy, the neo-classical model predicts equal principal Young's moduli, while its transversely isotropic extension with a constant semisoft modulus constrains the relation between reorientation onset and subsequent stress growth. We develop a finite-strain, isothermal elastic model combining transversely isotropic network elasticity with a deformation-dependent quadratic semisoft penalty. Within a weak-fluctuation approximation, a reduced statistical closure based on assumed linear contraction of effective local anisotropy fluctuations at fixed mean anisotropy yields an exponential transition from an initial to a residual semisoft modulus. The network energy preserves ideal soft modes, and the formulation recovers the de Gennes strain-director coupling in the infinitesimal limit. Analytical relations for perpendicular tension show that the rotating-stage stress depends on both the current semisoft modulus and its derivative with respect to the first deformation invariant. This permits a finite onset followed by a substantially flatter rotating response than in the constant-modulus limit. Oblique tension couples director rotation, shear and lateral contraction. Comparisons with orientation-dependent measurements using a common parameter set capture the principal stiffness contrast and broad perpendicular plateau, with discrepancies remaining in oblique stresses and intermediate-angle initial moduli.
