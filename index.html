CloakFEM: Agentic FEM Code Generation for Red-Teaming Computational Resource Exhaustion

Li Zheng, Simin Chen

Finite element method (FEM) solvers are foundational to scientific and engineering simulation, yet their susceptibility to \emph{efficiency attacks} remains largely unexplored. An adversary can craft syntactically valid and runnable FEM programs that complete successfully while forcing excessive computational work, enabling computational denial-of-service (DoS) attacks. Finding such inputs is challenging because FEM programs occupy a vast, structured space constrained by numerical, geometric, and constitutive requirements. We introduce CloakFEM, a domain-guided LLM-agent framework for automatically generating runnable adversarial FEM programs that amplify solver computation. CloakFEM combines LLM-guided search with an FEM-specific intermediate representation and a deterministic validity gate that enforces runnable constraints. Across three FEM stacks, 17 problem classes, and 1,068 seeds, CloakFEM discovers adversarial inputs that substantially amplify solver work while remaining valid and solvable. In the most extreme case, solver iterations increase from 43 to 247,367, a \(5{,}752.72\times\) amplification. CloakFEM outperforms five generic black-box baselines and retains an advantage over evolutionary search under a controlled bounded action space. Cross-solver analysis further reveals both problem-intrinsic and solver-specific sources of vulnerability. Our results uncover an underexplored efficiency-attack surface in FEM solvers and show that runnable FEM programs can serve as adversarial inputs.

Two-level additive Schwarz preconditioners with GDSW coarse spaces for high-order discretizations in 3D

Tommaso Bevilacqua, Axel Klawonn, Martin Lanser, Adam Wasiak

Overlapping Schwarz methods are among the most successful domain decomposition preconditioners for large-scale finite element simulations. In this work, the Generalized Dryja-Smith-Widlund (GDSW), GDSW$^*$, and Reduced GDSW (RGDSW) coarse spaces are studied for three-dimensional high-order finite element method (FEM) and virtual element method (VEM) for the Poisson equation and the equations of linear elasticity. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study of GDSW-type coarse spaces for polynomial degrees $k\ge 3$ in three dimensions, for either discretization. In 3D VEM, the face degrees of freedom (DOFs) are moments instead of point values. The kernel of the differential operator, which defines the interface values of the GDSW coarse basis functions, must be represented through these moments. The construction is therefore no longer purely algebraic and, without this correction, the coarse level loses its effect for $k\ge 3$. FEM and VEM are tested on hexahedral meshes, and VEM is additionally tested on polyhedral ones. Polynomial degrees up to $k=5$ and runs on up to $4\,096$ subdomains and parallel cores are included in the numerical study. Numerical experiments show scalable iteration counts for all coarse space variants for the Poisson problem, illustrating the expected trade-off between the coarse space size and robustness. Further experiments for linear elasticity confirms that the moment-based construction extends naturally from the scalar to the vector-valued problem.

Derivation of Novel Isotropic Metamaterials at the Hashin Shtrikman Stiffness Limit Enabled by Physics Informed Neural Networks

Manish Kumar Singh, Dominic Kang Jueh Lim, Anson Wen Han Cheong, Adams Wai Kin Kong, Chang Quan Lai

Current methods of deriving ultrastiff isotropic metamaterials require heuristics or topology optimization, which are highly inefficient and limited by human input. To circumvent this, Physics Informed Neural Networks are applied to analytical equations to systematically solve for plate orientations in closed cell lattices. Following a standardized procedure, the plate orientations were modelled and assembled into geometries resembling quasi random foams for validation via finite element simulations. Closed cell 6-plate geometries were also additively manufactured using LAPIS and experimentally characterized. The results indicate that a minimum of 6 plates is required to constitute an ultrastiff isotropic lattice, 7 plates would return the established plate Cubic Octet structure while 8 to 10 plate geometries did not show significant differences in properties. Despite the introduction of some plate bending due to sub-optimal plate placement strategy, relative stiffness of the PINNs lattices adheres closely to the Hashin Shtrikman upper bound, 90 percent of Hashin Shtrikman upper bound at a relative density of 0.4, and the lattices maintain near isotropic behaviour, achieving a Zener ratio of 0.95 at a relative density of 0.1. Benchmarking results show that specific stiffness of PINNs lattices is higher than the majority of isotropic architectures reported in the literature.

Remap for ALE Hydrodynamics via Direct Interpolation and Constraint Optimization

Brendan Keith, Dohyun Kim, Boyan Lazarov, Ketan Mittal, Cosmin G. Petra, Mathias R. Schmidt

This work presents an optimization-based remap approach for arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) hydrodynamics that preserves sharp features and physical bounds while conserving global quantities such as volume, mass, momentum, and total energy. Achieving these properties is the main challenge in remap methods. The presented approach is well-suited for high-order finite element (FE) discretizations and ALE methods that allow for large, purely Lagrangian displacements between successive remeshing and remapping steps. Recent advances in parallel, GPU-capable interpolation routines enable the initial transfer of all fields via direct physical-space interpolation, avoiding computationally costly and diffusive pseudo-transient PDE-based advection. The interpolation produces bounded fields with minimal numerical diffusion. However, it may violate the desired conservation of global properties. Thus, a subsequent optimization step restores conservation while preserving physical bounds. The presented method provides minimally diffusive remapping of finite element fields of arbitrary order. Moreover, it directly remaps quantities defined at integration points, eliminating the need for projection onto finite element spaces. The approach also mitigates typical challenges associated with synchronizing primal variables and conserved quantities in advection-based remap methods. The proposed optimization-based remap method is evaluated using standard two- and three-dimensional remap benchmarks and full ALE hydrodynamics simulations.

Identifying the Shear Piezoelectric Coefficient using Physics-Informed Neural Networks under Exact Boundary and Initial Constraints

Suhas Suresh Bharadwaj

The shear piezoelectric coefficient $e_{15}$ governs the shear-mode electromechanical coupling in piezoelectric micromachined ultrasonic transducers (PMUTs) and surface acoustic wave (SAW) devices, but measuring it typically requires destructive resonance testing on dedicated specimens. On simulated data, $e_{15}$ can instead be recovered non-destructively from sparse, noisy subsurface wavefield observations using a constrained physics-informed neural network (PINN). By building exact spatial distance functions and temporal multipliers directly into the network output, the clamped, grounded boundaries and resting initial state hold automatically, leaving the loss with only the interior equations of coupled electro-elastodynamics and the data misfit. Non-dimensional scaling brings all governing coefficients within one decade of O(1), and the setup removes the boundary and initial-condition penalty weights that soft-constraint methods must tune. In a six-way comparison (plane-strain PZT-5H, manufactured wavefield, 30 dB noise), a soft-penalty baseline with adaptive weights converges to an erroneous $e_{15}$ in four of the five runs (51.7% mean error), and fixed penalties reach an error of 0.85% only after a second-order optimization stage. The constrained formulations reach mean $e_{15}$ errors between 0.15% and 0.51% with held-out displacement errors below 1%. Within these variants, the spatial multiplier gives rapid first-order convergence, and the quadratic temporal multiplier lowers the early-time electrostatic residual by a factor of 6.8 versus a linear one. On finite-element data along one subsurface line, the full formulation recovers $e_{15}$ with 1.19% mean error at 30 dB and keeps it between 0.65% and 2.71% across a 40 to 0 dB noise sweep. Finally, training on displacements alone still recovers $e_{15}$ with mean errors of 0.45% on finite-element and 1.64% on manufactured data.

Process-zone condensation: a discretization-exact reduced cell model for FE$^2$ analysis of composites with fibre-matrix debonding and matrix plasticity

Leonilson Toffoli Pedrini, Maycoln Conci, Rafael Marin Ferro

Periodic composites with cohesive interfaces and matrix plasticity require nonlinear cell solutions in multiscale analysis. Process-zone condensation eliminates the linear elastic remainder of the cell by a Schur complement, retaining all interfacial and process-zone internal variables. Influence matrices recover macroscopic stress and consistent tangent. One back-substitution per increment checks that the matrix outside the retained zone remains elastic. While this holds, the reduction is exact relative to the full discretisation, also with a plastic matrix, and requires no training. In an E-glass/epoxy cell, eight of nine two-dimensional paths agree with the full cell to 1e-11 in stress and tangent; the check flags the ninth. Three paths on each of two three-dimensional meshes need zone expansion. Excluding setup and checks, two-dimensional path timings show an aggregate speed-up of 3.3; certified three-dimensional zones show none. In adaptive finite element squared (FE$^2$) simulations of a perforated plate, the reduced solver reproduces the full-cell response within solver tolerances, 1.51 times faster for glass/polypropylene and slower for glass/epoxy. Independent Ansys models agree to below 1e-3 in cell stress and about 0.6% in plate reactions, although local damage and plasticity onsets differ. The advantage depends on zone size, active set and adaptation costs.

ENGINE: Endogenous Variational MultiScale Optimization for Zeroth-Order LLM Fine-Tuning

Zhuoli Ouyang, Changxi Chi, Siyuan Li, Tailin Wu

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization enables memory-efficient large language model (LLM) fine-tuning, and recent methods incorporate curvature-aware strategies. However, in the high-dimensional parameter space of LLMs, these methods inherently suffer from exploding gradient variance and prohibitive costs. Conversely, confining updates to a low-dimensional subspace reduces variance but discards critical high-dimensional information, degrading performance. This creates a fundamental dilemma: full-space optimization is informative but has high variance, while subspace optimization has low variance but loses information. To break this trade-off, we propose ENGINE (Endogenous Variational MultiScale). Inspired by computational mechanics, we adapt static Variational MultiScale (VMS) theory to dynamic optimization, treating low- and high-dimensional spaces as resolved coarse and unresolved fine scales. Through a multigrid-style V-cycle, ENGINE alternates between spaces, dynamically probing fine-grained information in the high-dimensional space and injecting it to correct the efficient low-dimensional sampling process. Under appropriate assumptions, statistical analysis characterizes variance reduction and correction of curvature-induced bias, and convergence analysis establishes a stationarity bound for the corrected coarse updates. Evaluations on standard LLM benchmarks show that ENGINE achieves higher accuracy and faster convergence than existing ZO methods, achieving up to a $2.61\times$ speed-up in forward passes with zero additional peak memory overhead. Code is available at https://github.com/AI4Science-WestlakeU/ENGINE.

BISECT: Bilevel Spectral Decomposition for Low-Rank Continual Learning

Tom Saliencro, Wen-Hsuan Liu, Rohan Desai, Amara Okonkwo, Maya Lindqvist

Exemplar-free continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers increasingly relies on Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), yet prevailing methods govern the low-rank update space through strict subspace isolation. By projecting each incoming task into the approximate null space of historical token activations, isolation-based learners equate any cross-task feature overlap with catastrophic interference. We show that this orthogonality premise induces a structural bottleneck we term \emph{spectral starvation}: when down-projection bases are locked before task optimization, the gradient norm reaching the trainable up-projection is upper-bounded by the energy of current-task features captured by the chosen basis. Because natural visual tasks share low- and mid-level covariance structure, historical null tails contain barely $5.5\%$ of incoming task variance, starving new-class plasticity while blocking positive consolidation along shared directions. We propose BISECT (Bilevel Spectral Decomposition), a continual LoRA framework that partitions a fixed rank-$r$ budget into two orthogonal functional blocks: an invariant carrier spanned by the dominant modes of the joint historical-and-current Gram matrix, and a novelty complement extracted via generalized Rayleigh whitening followed by orthogonal complement projection. After single-task training, BISECT folds both blocks back into the frozen backbone using a closed-form precision gate that scales each rank-1 invariant atom by its current-to-joint Rayleigh ratio while preserving novelty atoms at unit gain. Across eight incremental settings on ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and DomainNet, BISECT outperforms prompt, adapter, and LoRA baselines with zero extra inference parameters or FLOPs, and remains complementary to sparse analytic expansion.

RASP: Read-All, Sparse-Plasticity Continual Learning in a Stationary-Size LoRA

Wen-Hsuan Liu, Amara Okonkwo, Tom Saliencro, Rohan Desai, Maya Lindqvist

Parameter-efficient continual learning on pre-trained Vision Transformers largely relies on a costly premise: preventing catastrophic forgetting requires either spawning a dedicated LoRA branch per session---causing adapter parameters to scale linearly with task horizon---or applying sparse Mixture-of-Experts gating symmetrically to both forward and backward passes, which sacrifices representational rank at test time. We argue that reading consolidated knowledge and writing new task credit are fundamentally asymmetric operations. Because visual features in a shared low-rank space distribute across rank-1 atoms rather than localizing in disjoint task silos, forward inference should read all atoms at full rank capacity, whereas backward optimization should restrict plasticity to a sparse subset of input-aligned channels. We introduce RASP (Read-All, Sparse-Plasticity LoRA), which adapts a single stationary-size adapter across sequential tasks. At each step, a scene-modulated cosine router selects the top-$q$ unlocked atoms to receive gradients, blocks updates on the remaining $r-q$ channels, and penalizes Frobenius Gram coherence between active and non-updated output directions. Across sessions, the router's empirical activation trace and each atom's exact spectral norm form a zero-overhead geometric consolidation index $ω_j = \sqrt{\tilde{c}_j \tildeσ_j}$ that drives Cauchy step attenuation and boundary anchor locking. Across CIFAR-100, CUB-200, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-A, RASP matches or surpasses branch-expanding LoRA learners under a strictly constant parameter footprint.

Estimating semantic correspondences between different object instances of similar categories in real-world images is a fundamental challenge in computer vision. While the recent progress in foundation models has significantly advanced solutions for such difficult image correspondence problems, respective methods are often insufficiently regularised. For example, common nearest-neighbour matching with foundation model features often results in noisy correspondences, which is particularly prominent when solving for dense correspondences. In this work, we tackle image matching via functional maps, which have been popularised for 3D shape matching due to their powerful spectral formalism utilising an efficient low-dimensional linear representation. However, functional maps require that domains (in our cases the rectangular image grids) are equipped with an informative and non-trivial geometry. To define a meaningful manifold structure for images, we use Galerkin's method from finite elements to derive a discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator (LBO) based on a manifold embedding of deep image features. Further, we leverage a neural adjoint map that allows to represent non-linear mappings. We demonstrate that our method sets the new state of the art among zero-shot image correspondence methods on multiple dense correspondence benchmarks of real-world images. Our code is publicly available: https://github.com/NafieAmrani/NAMI

Coupled Soft-Clamped Polygon Resonators with Tunable Mechanical Coupling

Zhihao Niu, Peng Chen, Shenggen Zheng, Yuan-Yuan Zhao, Yuanyuan Zhao

Coupler geometry controls both the frequency splitting and the dissipation of coupled polygon resonators. We develop a continuum model for two identical square-shaped resonators coupled by a shared tether and compare the analytical predictions with finite element simulations. The model relates the coupler length and width to the frequency splitting and dissipation dilution of the two coupled modes. In the short-coupler limit, $k_cl_c\ll1$, the symmetric-mode coupler undergoes nearly uniform rotation, and its torsional strain energy vanishes asymptotically. The remaining loss contributions arise from polygon-side bending and supporting-tether torsion. The reduced supporting-tether contribution allows the symmetric-mode dissipation dilution to exceed that of an isolated polygon with the same side and supporting-tether dimensions. The antisymmetric mode retains torsional strain in the coupler and has an additional loss contribution. We also derive an expression for $Q\cdot f$ in the short-coupler and short-tether limits, where $Q$ is the mechanical quality factor and $f$ is the resonance frequency. This expression gives the dependence of $Q\cdot f$ on tensile stress, film thickness, and supporting-tether geometry. For a coupler length of $100~μ\mathrm{m}$ and width of $2~μ\mathrm{m}$, finite element calculations give a frequency splitting of $17.7~\mathrm{Hz}$ and a symmetric-mode dilution factor approximately $13\%$ above the analytical isolated-polygon value.

Variational sensitivity analysis reveals a symmetric-plus-rank-one Jacobian that yields matrix-free sensitivities without additional adjoint PDE solves, reducing residual sensitivity overhead to only 0.11% of the state elasticity solve time in 3D. Simultaneously, single-node hinges and strut fragmentation are substantially suppressed compared to unregularized baselines. Three-dimensional benchmarks further demonstrate practical spatial viability for complex structures.

Three-dimensional computational rheometry of viscoplastic fluids in vane-in-cup fixtures

James Malcolm, Clare McColgan, Emma Mullarkey, Emad Chaparian

We develop a computational framework to investigate the flow kinematics of viscoplastic materials, described by the Herschel-Bulkley model, in vane-in-cup rheometers. Numerical simulations are performed using augmented Lagrangian finite element and regularised finite volume methods for two- and three-dimensional configurations, respectively, to characterise the hydrodynamics and yielding behaviour over a broad range of effective parameters. Three types of vanes are investigated: conventional 4- and 6-arm bespoke vanes and a 24-arm fractal vane (Owens et al., J. Rheol., vol. 64(3), 2020, 643-662). Our previous two-dimensional study (Chaparian et al., J. Non-Newtonian Fluid Mech., vol. 307, 2022, 104857) demonstrated the superiority of the fractal vane over conventional N-arm designs. The bespoke vanes produce highly non-uniform yielding, leading to significant deviations from the circular Couette analogy, whereas the 24-arm fractal vane generates nearly axisymmetric yield surfaces that effectively "cloak" the vane geometry. Consequently, the resulting flow closely resembles the azimuthal shear in a conventional "bob & cup" rheometer, providing substantially more accurate rheological measurements. Building on these findings, the present study revisits the torque-to-stress and rotation-to-strain rate conversions used in vane-in-cup rheometry. In particular, we re-examine the circular Couette analogy that underpins both the physical interpretation of the flow and the extraction of flow curves from raw rheometric data. Based on our extensive two- and three-dimensional simulations, we identify limitations of the existing conversion relations and propose improved closure expressions that enable more accurate determination of material's rheological properties. Furthermore, the three-dimensional flow structures and yielding patterns within the vane-in-cup fixtures are characterised.
