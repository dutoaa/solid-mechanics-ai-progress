Frame Based Inelasticity for Strength and Fracture in Anisotropic Media

Robert Lipton, Prakash K. C.

A field theory is presented for predicting damage and fracture in brittle materials. Four concepts are used: a new nonlocal deformation gradient for dynamic stress analysis in anisotropic media, a new type of phase field coming from a constitutive law, the evolution of material displacement from Newton's second law, and a new incorporation of scaled strength surfaces into constitutive laws for predicting dynamic and quasi-static fracture patterns. The elastic softening is governed by the strain dynamics through the constitutive law and in this way we provide the two way coupling between strain and fracture. Simulations validating the approach for both dynamic and quasi-static fracture analysis are compared to experimental results and other simulation methods. The model is capable of predicting material strength in titania and the competition between strength and energy in soda lime glass and titana and captures dynamic crack branching in soda lime glass and concrete.

Variationally correct physics-informed operator learning for nonlinear partial differential equations

Yuan Qiu, Wolfgang Dahmen, Peng Chen

Physics-informed operator learning trains neural operators on the residual of a parametric partial differential equation (PDE) instead of labeled solution data, but its reliability depends on a variationally correct residual: one that is equivalent to the error in a norm dictated by the equation, the PDE-compliant norm. For nonlinear equations written as first-order systems, we prove that the first-order system least-squares residual is equivalent to the error in this norm near a solution branch if the linearized residual is stable and the residual derivative is Lipschitz continuous, possibly in a norm stronger than the error norm. We verify these conditions for monotone reaction, solution- and gradient-dependent diffusion, and compressible Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity. The stability mechanisms vary across these classes, as do the regions where the equivalence holds, from arbitrary bounded sets to a neighborhood of the branch. In a conforming finite element space, we show that the loss excess of a state near the finite element solution is equivalent to its squared distance from that solution. We use this loss to train a reduced basis neural operator with values in this space, with solution snapshots needed only for the basis and the loss and its symbolically derived gradient assembled by the finite element library. At inference, this loss estimates the error. Numerically, the square root of the loss excess tracks the error within factors comparable to those of the linear case, and the operator achieves higher accuracy in the PDE-compliant norm than grid-based baselines evaluated in the same finite element space.

Nonlocal regularization of sharp-crack energetics: a variational bridge from Griffith geometry to emergent constitutive degradation

Qing Cheng, Yuqi Sun, Xuejun Xu

We develop a variational diffuse-crack model based on nonlocal averaging of tensile strain energy. A bounded potential acts on the scaled average, while the compressive energy remains local. The initial slope preserves the averaged tensile strain-energy density to leading order. The first variation yields a constitutive stress in which the tensile contribution is multiplied by the adjoint average of the derivative of the saturation potential. The resulting degradation is therefore determined by the stored-energy functional, without introducing an independent fracture field or prescribing a separate degradation law. For fixed smooth displacement fields with bounded strain, the energy density converges to the classical elastic strain-energy density as the averaging radius tends to zero. The Griffith surface-energy coefficient is recovered for planar opening transitions. A structure-preserving scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) scheme satisfies an exact discrete balance between the modified-energy increment and external work. We compare SAV and velocity-Verlet solutions for dynamic crack branching and Kalthoff--Winkler impact.

Mesoscale Neural Operators: Learning Nonlinear Constitutive Laws from Molecular Dynamics for Scalable Fracture Predictions

Yue Yu, Jihong Wang, Stewart Silling, Ziwei Yang

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations can capture the nanoscale physics of fracture at the atomistic level, but their computational cost prohibits predictions at the length scales and time scales needed for many applications. Machine learning approaches, on the other hand, have been proposed as heuristic surrogates for material fracture, yet they cannot guarantee the physical consistency of the predicted response. To address this shortcoming, we propose the mesoscale neural operator (MNO): a data-driven nonlocal constitutive model that is learned directly from MD loading and displacement data. After training, it can be used in continuum fracture simulations on much larger domains and grid sizes. The key idea of the MNO is to exploit the built-in characteristic length of a nonlocal model, called the horizon, to travel across scales. Physical consistency across scales is obtained by building two structural ingredients into the MNO: a normalized kernel that renders the strain energy density invariant under horizon rescaling, and a rescaled critical bond stretch that keeps the energy release rate consistent across scales. Through tests on synthetic data, we show that the MNO and its learning workflow indeed discover the nonlocal continuum multiscale model in the form of the nonlocal kernel and the nonlinear constitutive relation. We then apply the framework to coarse-grained MD data and find that in a test problem, the learned model matches the MD strain energy density and critical energy release rate within 3%. It also predicts the peak transmitted forces within 6% in out-of-distribution tension tests. As a further demonstration, the model is applied to a plate with 4000 times the area of the training specimens and containing an initial defect. In this problem, the model reproduces the main features of dynamic brittle fracture, including branch and cascading branch networks.

Locking-free numerical methods for poroelasticity optimal control problems with minimal regularities

Jeonghun J. Lee

We study distributed optimal control of a linear poroelastic system in a total pressure formulation, with displacement and fluid-pressure tracking and box constraints on the source control. A mechanical Schur complement gives well-posedness at zero storage for each fixed finite Lamé parameter, without estimates that grow like the inverse storage coefficient. We discretize the state and adjoint with conforming finite elements and dG(0) time stepping, and use piecewise constant controls. Under the stated regularity assumptions and a positive lower bound on storage, we prove first-order control and state error estimates uniform as the Lamé parameter increases. Numerical experiments confirm the predicted convergence rates, examine the dependence on the Lamé and storage coefficients, and demonstrate localized pressure control with active bounds.

Scientific Coding with Large Language Models: Evidence from Finite Element Analysis

Erfan Hamdi, Joel Shor, Emma Lejeune

Large language models (LLMs) offer a potential route to automating scientific programming, but their usefulness depends on consistently producing numerically correct code. We extend FEM-Bench to 17 finite element tasks involving nonlinear and history-dependent mechanics and structural dynamics, and evaluate 15 models on implementation and test generation without execution feedback. The best model succeeds in 87.1% of single-pass implementation trials, but rankings differ between tracks. The leading implementer achieves 49.9% joint test success, while the leading tester reaches 82.6%. For six models, we compare single-pass generation with two agentic harnesses that allow code execution and revision. Agentic execution solves some tasks that never succeeded in single-pass trials and improves consistency on others, but gains vary across models and harnesses, and regressions also occur. Claude Opus 5 with SimpleAgent reaches 96.5% Pass@3, the probability of at least one success in three attempts, but 77.1% Pass$^3$, the probability that all three succeed. No model-harness combination exceeds 78.8% Pass$^3$. To understand why agents submit incorrect code after passing their own checks, we analyze 249 failed agentic trajectories and identify six categories of self-verification failure. The most common are inadequate test coverage (73.1%) and reliance on generic consistency checks (70.3%), and in 28.5% of cases the tests share an error with the implementation. Motivated by these findings, an exploratory configuration with separate coder and tester agents achieves the highest observed scores, with 97.6% Pass@3 and 88.8% Pass$^3$. These results identify consistency across attempts and independent verification as the main obstacles to trusting LLM-generated scientific code.

Differentiable High-Order Mesh $r$-Adaptivity

Ketan Mittal, Julian Andrej, John Camier, Veselin Dobrev, Tzanio Kolev, Leonardo Molinari

High-order mesh r-adaptivity typically entails minimizing a nonlinear functional using its gradients and, for Newton-based methods, its Hessian. These derivatives can be expensive to derive, must be updated with any changes to the functional, and errors in the derivatives directly impact the convergence of r-adaptivity. Additionally, derivatives of the r-adaptivity operator are required for consistent sensitivity analysis for inverse problems. This work develops a framework for differentiable r-adaptivity based on the Target-Matrix Optimization Paradigm. Given a pointwise functional defining the local mesh-adaptation objective, the proposed framework uses $\partial$FEM to automatically generate pointwise first- and second-order derivatives. These local derivatives are then assembled into a global gradient vector and a Hessian operator through the finite element operator decomposition. The derivative kernels support portable CPU and GPU execution, with partial assembly and tensor sum factorization enabling matrix-free application of the Hessian operator. The framework also provides consistent sensitivities of the r-adaptivity operator through automatically generated mixed derivatives and implicit differentiation of the mesh stationarity equations. Numerical experiments show GPU solve times comparable to those of hand-derived TMOP implementations on a benchmark problem, improvement in r-adaptivity convergence due to accurate computation of the derivatives, and demonstrate the use of mesh sensitivities for material-interface identification in an inverse heat-conduction problem.

A Staggered Discontinuous Galerkin Neural Network Method with a Computable Error Decomposition

Gung-Min Gie, Chang-Yeol Jung, Bongsuk Kwon, Kiwoong Kwon

We develop a staggered discontinuous Galerkin neural network (SDGNN) method for scalar elliptic problems with a computable error decomposition. The staggered discontinuous Galerkin (SDG) method requires only local continuity of its trial functions and gives a square, inf-sup stable system without stabilization parameters; we carry this structure over to neural networks. Networks defined on the patches of a staggered mesh satisfy the SDG continuity conditions for all values of the network parameters, and the loss is the squared dual norm of the SDG weak residual. We show that the loss is equivalent, with computable constants, to the square of the training error, the distance between the SDG projection of the network and the SDG finite element approximation, so that the full error is bounded by the discretization error, a multiple of the square root of the loss, and the network projection error. The weak residual requires no derivatives of the networks, the boundary and convection terms carry no penalty or stabilization weights, and the scalar trial functions may jump across the midlines of the primal cells. Numerical experiments show that the square root of the loss tracks the error during the training, that prescribed jumps across a square and a circular interface are captured consistently over the initializations, and that the method is more accurate than physics-informed neural networks at the same parameter counts.

A mesoscale damage model for void nucleation and growth in shock loaded microstructure informed by computer vision

Abhijith Thoopul Anantharanga, Brandon Runnels

Spallation in ductile materials under high strain rates occurs through the process of void nucleation, growth, and coalescence. Void nucleation is known to depend on factors ranging from microstructural to stress state. The criteria for void nucleation in damage models are stochastic, and microstructural awareness is often neglected. Recently, it was shown that nucleation propensity can be estimated using computer vision. In the present work, this is integrated with a continuum damage model framework for comprehensive simulation of spallation in tantalum under plate impact. Void nucleation probability is governed by a susceptibility field, predicted from grain orientations and grain boundary (GB) energy, with a mechanical barrier set by the local maximum principal stress. This is coupled to a crystal plasticity model within a Lagrangian shock simulation, with microstructure selectively resolved to reduce computational cost. The model is calibrated at a flyer velocity of 196 m/s, where the simulated free surface velocity (FSV) matches experimental measurements of shock arrival, peak state, release and the pullback signal measured in flyer impact experiments, with some deviations confined to the late time ringing. With no further parameter changes, the model transfers to a 214 m/s case and accurately reproduces the FSV. To demonstrate the microstructure-awareness of the nucleation model, a selection of synthetic microstructures is presented for comparison. In the resolved microstructure, voids nucleate predominantly (though not exclusively) at GBs and triple junctions. It is shown that the FSV differs significantly based on the incorporated microstructure, yielding the correct FSV prediction only in the case of representative microstructures.

PANO: Parallel Latent Space Neural Operator for Solving PDEs

Zijiang Yang, Xiaomeng Wu, Chen Kuang, Dongmei Fu

Aggregating observations into a limited number of latent tokens and learning physical interactions in the latent space has emerged as an effective approach for Transformer-based neural operators to solve Partial Differential Equation (PDE) problems involving large-scale observations. However, existing methods typically compress observations using a single aggregation pattern, making physical information not retained by that pattern invisible to the latent space and thereby constraining physical interactions within it. To address this limitation, we propose PArallel Latent Space Neural Operator (PANO), a novel neural operator for solving PDEs that formulates physical interactions among observations as an additive combination of interaction results from parallel latent spaces with shared parameters. Specifically, PANO transforms observations into multiple distinct compressed representations through independent mappings, learns physical interactions in parallel based on these representations, and finally fuses the decoded results to predict the target physical field. Within PANO, we further propose Coupled Assignment Projection (CAP) to construct bidirectionally consistent encoding and decoding mappings, and Pairwise Latent Space Attention Layer (PaLA) to jointly update latent representations of each latent space through adaptive cross-space interactions. Extensive experiments on nine benchmarks spanning diverse physical systems, geometries, and observation scales demonstrate that PANO achieves state-of-the-art performance. Notably, PANO reduces equivalent plastic strain prediction errors on Unilateral Stamping by 28.3% (or more) and lift-coefficient prediction errors on AirCraft by 21.4% (or more) compared with existing methods. Code is available at https://github.com/ZijiangY1116/PANO.

Pharmaco-mechanical computational simulation of atherosclerotic arteries: effects of calcium channel blockers on local stress distributions

Sharan Nurani Ramesh, Lea Saßmannshausen, Axel Klawonn, Daniel Balzani

This paper presents a computational model integrating transmural drug transport, smooth muscle cell contraction, nonlinear anisotropic material behavior, and residual stresses to investigate the effects of calcium channel blockers~(CCBs) on local stress distributions in atherosclerotic arteries. The balance of linear momentum is coupled with a three-dimensional reaction-diffusion equation for the drug transport. Smooth muscle contraction is modeled phenomenologically based on a chemo-mechanical model that incorporates the effects of stretch on intracellular calcium dynamics and calcium sensitization. A novel drug-tissue interaction model is proposed to capture the effects of CCBs on intracellular calcium and thus on smooth muscle contraction. Material parameters are identified by fitting to experimental data from the literature and finite element simulations are conducted on five exemplary atherosclerotic arterial geometries reconstructed from histological cross-sections. Simulation results show that although CCBs reduce the stresses in the media through reduced smooth muscle contraction, they shift load to the fibrous cap, whose stress increases in all five geometries (median of up to 28.9~kPa). This increase varies strongly between plaque morphologies and becomes smaller with lower smooth muscle tone before treatment, lower drug concentrations in the arterial wall and increasing axial pre-stretch. A blood pressure reduction of 10~mmHg already lowers it to a median of at most 3.8~kPa in four of the five geometries. Our findings suggest that CCBs may increase the rupture risk of vulnerable plaques with highly loaded fibrous caps under insufficient blood pressure reduction, which remains to be confirmed experimentally and clinically.

A Divergence-Free Finite Element Discretization of a Convection-Cooling Optimal Control Problem

Tongxin Wang, Zhaojie Zhou

In this paper, we develop and analyze a finite element discretization for a bilinear optimal control problem governed by a stationary convection-diffusion equation, in which an incompressible velocity field acts as the control through the convective term. Continuous piecewise linear finite elements are employed to approximate the state and adjoint variables, while the Stokes-type optimality equation for the velocity control is discretized by a low-order, pointwise divergence-free ($P_1^c\oplus RT_0-P_0$) finite element. To accommodate the nonconforming velocity space, we construct a discrete cost functional by replacing the continuous $H^1$-seminorm regularization with a suitable discrete velocity bilinear form and derive the corresponding discrete first-order optimality system. Since the bilinear coupling between the velocity control and the state variable renders the underlying optimization problem nonconvex, we establish local strong convexity of the discrete reduced cost functional under a second-order sufficient optimality condition at an isolated strict local minimizer of the continuous problem. Furthermore, by exploiting a divergence-preserving interpolation operator, auxiliary state and adjoint error estimates, and a stability relation between the discrete reduced-gradient residual and the control error, we derive a priori error estimates for the state, adjoint state and velocity control. Numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the optimized incompressible velocity in redistributing the temperature field and to verify the predicted convergence rates.

Fully Spiking Neural Implementation of Lambda-Calculus Beta-Reduction via Biologically Documented Mechanisms

Vladimir A. Lazovskiy, Victor B. Kazantsev, Sergey V. Stasenko

We present the first complete implementation of $β$-reduction in the untyped $λ$-calculus using exclusively spiking neural dynamics. Every step of the reduction---redex detection, subterm segmentation, variable-binding detection, De~Bruijn index arithmetic, token copying, and inter-step role maintenance---is performed by leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons with explicit membrane dynamics, exponential synaptic currents, axonal delays, and pair-based spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP). Each neural component maps onto a specific, experimentally documented mechanism: Jeffress-type delay-line coincidence for redex detection; a line-attractor balance counter for subterm segmentation; $θ$--$γ$ cross-frequency coupling for De~Bruijn variable binding; a phase-ring traveling wave for depth-index arithmetic; theta-cycle STDP for token copying; and recurrent persistent firing for role maintenance between reduction steps. All synaptic weights are derived analytically from measured EPSP amplitudes; no hand tuning is performed. The closed-loop system achieves 100\,\% agreement with a symbolic oracle across ten validation classes, including Church numeral arithmetic (\textsc{plus}, \textsc{mult}, \textsc{pred}), Boolean logic with divergent branches, Y-combinator steps, terms with up to six bound-variable occurrences, De~Bruijn indices up to~12, noise robustness, and determinism. To our knowledge this is the first Turing-complete symbolic computation performed entirely within spike dynamics using biologically grounded circuit primitives. We discuss implications for neuromorphic proof assistants and the neural basis of symbolic reasoning.

These properties make SBFEM an attractive candidate for porous and highly heterogeneous microstructures, where existing voxel-based FFT discretizations offer no generally reliable choice: Reduced-integration hexahedral elements, equivalently the rotated staggered grid discretization, may suffer from hourglass instabilities in the presence of voids, whereas fully integrated hexahedral elements are often too stiff. This result enables a fully matrix-free implementation of the SBFEM-discretized problem, without assembling or storing the global stiffness matrix. Numerical experiments on a short-fibre reinforced composite, a polyurethane foam and a sand-based binder jet microstructure demonstrate robust convergence and competitive accuracy compared with established voxel discretizations.
