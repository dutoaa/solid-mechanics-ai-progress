Variationally correct physics-informed operator learning for nonlinear partial differential equations

Yuan Qiu, Wolfgang Dahmen, Peng Chen

Physics-informed operator learning trains neural operators on the residual of a parametric partial differential equation (PDE) instead of labeled solution data, but its reliability depends on a variationally correct residual: one that is equivalent to the error in a norm dictated by the equation, the PDE-compliant norm. For nonlinear equations written as first-order systems, we prove that the first-order system least-squares residual is equivalent to the error in this norm near a solution branch if the linearized residual is stable and the residual derivative is Lipschitz continuous, possibly in a norm stronger than the error norm. We verify these conditions for monotone reaction, solution- and gradient-dependent diffusion, and compressible Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity. The stability mechanisms vary across these classes, as do the regions where the equivalence holds, from arbitrary bounded sets to a neighborhood of the branch. In a conforming finite element space, we show that the loss excess of a state near the finite element solution is equivalent to its squared distance from that solution. We use this loss to train a reduced basis neural operator with values in this space, with solution snapshots needed only for the basis and the loss and its symbolically derived gradient assembled by the finite element library. At inference, this loss estimates the error. Numerically, the square root of the loss excess tracks the error within factors comparable to those of the linear case, and the operator achieves higher accuracy in the PDE-compliant norm than grid-based baselines evaluated in the same finite element space.

Fuse-PINN: Combining Finite Element Methods and Physics-Informed Neural Networks to enable massively parallel simulations of non-linear PDEs

Stanislas Pamela, Nicolas Pailliez, Emmanuel Franck, Victor Michel-Dansac, Laurent Navoret, Vignesh Gopakumar

Research in the domain of Physics-Informed Neural Networks applied to PDE modelling has advanced significantly in recent years, providing a wide ecosystem of novel methods. However, one frequent limitation is the inability of PINNs to scale out on High-Performance Computing architectures to address more ambitious challenges, particularly non-linear physics models with turbulent dynamics in large spatial domains. Typical studies restrain demonstrations to toy models with simple physics equations, few variables, and rarely any complex non-linear dynamics evolved over long timescales. Part of the AI community has even adopted the idea that "PINNs don't scale". This work proves otherwise. We present a mathematically robust formulation of Finite-Element-Method for Neural Networks (FEM-NN) implemented into a comprehensive, modular framework: Fuse-PINN. This formulation provides the basis for a sclable parallelisation of Physics-Informed Neural Networks on GPU-based HPC systems. Furthermore, an Energy Natural Gradient Descent optimiser is implemented to allow high-precision convergence. Fuse-PINN is demonstrated on physics models relevant to Magnetic Confinement Fusion, including non-linear convective models with fine-scale dynamics. Near-perfect scaling is achieved on multi-GPU runs with increasing problem size.

Mesoscale Neural Operators: Learning Nonlinear Constitutive Laws from Molecular Dynamics for Scalable Fracture Predictions

Yue Yu, Jihong Wang, Stewart Silling, Ziwei Yang

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations can capture the nanoscale physics of fracture at the atomistic level, but their computational cost prohibits predictions at the length scales and time scales needed for many applications. Machine learning approaches, on the other hand, have been proposed as heuristic surrogates for material fracture, yet they cannot guarantee the physical consistency of the predicted response. To address this shortcoming, we propose the mesoscale neural operator (MNO): a data-driven nonlocal constitutive model that is learned directly from MD loading and displacement data. After training, it can be used in continuum fracture simulations on much larger domains and grid sizes. The key idea of the MNO is to exploit the built-in characteristic length of a nonlocal model, called the horizon, to travel across scales. Physical consistency across scales is obtained by building two structural ingredients into the MNO: a normalized kernel that renders the strain energy density invariant under horizon rescaling, and a rescaled critical bond stretch that keeps the energy release rate consistent across scales. Through tests on synthetic data, we show that the MNO and its learning workflow indeed discover the nonlocal continuum multiscale model in the form of the nonlocal kernel and the nonlinear constitutive relation. We then apply the framework to coarse-grained MD data and find that in a test problem, the learned model matches the MD strain energy density and critical energy release rate within 3%. It also predicts the peak transmitted forces within 6% in out-of-distribution tension tests. As a further demonstration, the model is applied to a plate with 4000 times the area of the training specimens and containing an initial defect. In this problem, the model reproduces the main features of dynamic brittle fracture, including branch and cascading branch networks.

Scientific Coding with Large Language Models: Evidence from Finite Element Analysis

Erfan Hamdi, Joel Shor, Emma Lejeune

Large language models (LLMs) offer a potential route to automating scientific programming, but their usefulness depends on consistently producing numerically correct code. We extend FEM-Bench to 17 finite element tasks involving nonlinear and history-dependent mechanics and structural dynamics, and evaluate 15 models on implementation and test generation without execution feedback. The best model succeeds in 87.1% of single-pass implementation trials, but rankings differ between tracks. The leading implementer achieves 49.9% joint test success, while the leading tester reaches 82.6%. For six models, we compare single-pass generation with two agentic harnesses that allow code execution and revision. Agentic execution solves some tasks that never succeeded in single-pass trials and improves consistency on others, but gains vary across models and harnesses, and regressions also occur. Claude Opus 5 with SimpleAgent reaches 96.5% Pass@3, the probability of at least one success in three attempts, but 77.1% Pass$^3$, the probability that all three succeed. No model-harness combination exceeds 78.8% Pass$^3$. To understand why agents submit incorrect code after passing their own checks, we analyze 249 failed agentic trajectories and identify six categories of self-verification failure. The most common are inadequate test coverage (73.1%) and reliance on generic consistency checks (70.3%), and in 28.5% of cases the tests share an error with the implementation. Motivated by these findings, an exploratory configuration with separate coder and tester agents achieves the highest observed scores, with 97.6% Pass@3 and 88.8% Pass$^3$. These results identify consistency across attempts and independent verification as the main obstacles to trusting LLM-generated scientific code.

A Staggered Discontinuous Galerkin Neural Network Method with a Computable Error Decomposition

Gung-Min Gie, Chang-Yeol Jung, Bongsuk Kwon, Kiwoong Kwon

We develop a staggered discontinuous Galerkin neural network (SDGNN) method for scalar elliptic problems with a computable error decomposition. The staggered discontinuous Galerkin (SDG) method requires only local continuity of its trial functions and gives a square, inf-sup stable system without stabilization parameters; we carry this structure over to neural networks. Networks defined on the patches of a staggered mesh satisfy the SDG continuity conditions for all values of the network parameters, and the loss is the squared dual norm of the SDG weak residual. We show that the loss is equivalent, with computable constants, to the square of the training error, the distance between the SDG projection of the network and the SDG finite element approximation, so that the full error is bounded by the discretization error, a multiple of the square root of the loss, and the network projection error. The weak residual requires no derivatives of the networks, the boundary and convection terms carry no penalty or stabilization weights, and the scalar trial functions may jump across the midlines of the primal cells. Numerical experiments show that the square root of the loss tracks the error during the training, that prescribed jumps across a square and a circular interface are captured consistently over the initializations, and that the method is more accurate than physics-informed neural networks at the same parameter counts.

A mesoscale damage model for void nucleation and growth in shock loaded microstructure informed by computer vision

Abhijith Thoopul Anantharanga, Brandon Runnels

Spallation in ductile materials under high strain rates occurs through the process of void nucleation, growth, and coalescence. Void nucleation is known to depend on factors ranging from microstructural to stress state. The criteria for void nucleation in damage models are stochastic, and microstructural awareness is often neglected. Recently, it was shown that nucleation propensity can be estimated using computer vision. In the present work, this is integrated with a continuum damage model framework for comprehensive simulation of spallation in tantalum under plate impact. Void nucleation probability is governed by a susceptibility field, predicted from grain orientations and grain boundary (GB) energy, with a mechanical barrier set by the local maximum principal stress. This is coupled to a crystal plasticity model within a Lagrangian shock simulation, with microstructure selectively resolved to reduce computational cost. The model is calibrated at a flyer velocity of 196 m/s, where the simulated free surface velocity (FSV) matches experimental measurements of shock arrival, peak state, release and the pullback signal measured in flyer impact experiments, with some deviations confined to the late time ringing. With no further parameter changes, the model transfers to a 214 m/s case and accurately reproduces the FSV. To demonstrate the microstructure-awareness of the nucleation model, a selection of synthetic microstructures is presented for comparison. In the resolved microstructure, voids nucleate predominantly (though not exclusively) at GBs and triple junctions. It is shown that the FSV differs significantly based on the incorporated microstructure, yielding the correct FSV prediction only in the case of representative microstructures.

Pharmaco-mechanical computational simulation of atherosclerotic arteries: effects of calcium channel blockers on local stress distributions

Sharan Nurani Ramesh, Lea Saßmannshausen, Axel Klawonn, Daniel Balzani

This paper presents a computational model integrating transmural drug transport, smooth muscle cell contraction, nonlinear anisotropic material behavior, and residual stresses to investigate the effects of calcium channel blockers~(CCBs) on local stress distributions in atherosclerotic arteries. The balance of linear momentum is coupled with a three-dimensional reaction-diffusion equation for the drug transport. Smooth muscle contraction is modeled phenomenologically based on a chemo-mechanical model that incorporates the effects of stretch on intracellular calcium dynamics and calcium sensitization. A novel drug-tissue interaction model is proposed to capture the effects of CCBs on intracellular calcium and thus on smooth muscle contraction. Material parameters are identified by fitting to experimental data from the literature and finite element simulations are conducted on five exemplary atherosclerotic arterial geometries reconstructed from histological cross-sections. Simulation results show that although CCBs reduce the stresses in the media through reduced smooth muscle contraction, they shift load to the fibrous cap, whose stress increases in all five geometries (median of up to 28.9~kPa). This increase varies strongly between plaque morphologies and becomes smaller with lower smooth muscle tone before treatment, lower drug concentrations in the arterial wall and increasing axial pre-stretch. A blood pressure reduction of 10~mmHg already lowers it to a median of at most 3.8~kPa in four of the five geometries. Our findings suggest that CCBs may increase the rupture risk of vulnerable plaques with highly loaded fibrous caps under insufficient blood pressure reduction, which remains to be confirmed experimentally and clinically.

Scaled boundary finite elements for FFT-based computational micromechanics

Flavia Gehrig, Carolin Birk, Matti Schneider

We introduce a matrix-free FFT-based solver for three-dimensional linear elastic homogenization problems discretized by the scaled boundary finite element method (SBFEM) on a voxel grid. Owing to its analytical solution in the radial direction, the SBFEM discretization is inherently softer than fully integrated voxel finite elements, whereas its displacement-based formulation remains symmetric, conforming, and supports arbitrary stiffness anisotropy. These properties make SBFEM an attractive candidate for porous and highly heterogeneous microstructures, where existing voxel-based FFT discretizations offer no generally reliable choice: Reduced-integration hexahedral elements, equivalently the rotated staggered grid discretization, may suffer from hourglass instabilities in the presence of voids, whereas fully integrated hexahedral elements are often too stiff. The proposed discretization uses hexahedral SBFEM cells on the full voxel mesh of a regular periodic grid, retaining the simplicity of a fixed-resolution voxel representation without requiring adaptive mesh coarsening. The main contribution is a preconditioner based on the homogeneous SBFEM stiffness operator. We show that this preconditioner may be applied locally in Fourier space and that the condition number of the preconditioned system is bounded by the material contrast, independently of the grid resolution. This result enables a fully matrix-free implementation of the SBFEM-discretized problem, without assembling or storing the global stiffness matrix. Numerical experiments on a short-fibre reinforced composite, a polyurethane foam and a sand-based binder jet microstructure demonstrate robust convergence and competitive accuracy compared with established voxel discretizations. In particular, SBFEM remains stable for porous microstructures, for which reduced-integration hexahedral elements become unstable.

Understanding the onset of plastic deformation and the early development of strain localization is essential for characterizing material behavior and identifying failure-prone regions. However, these transitions are not always distinguishable from conventional full-field strain measurements. This study investigates a data- driven framework combining Digital Image Correlation (DIC) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to extract temporal and spatial deformation signatures during uniaxial tensile loading. The methodology is evaluated using AA6063-T5 aluminum alloy specimens in the as-received condition and following thermal exposure at 100~$^\circ$C, 200~$^\circ$C, and 300~$^\circ$C. PCA applied to sequential strain fields reveals a characteristic peak in the second principal component projection near the yielding transition, showing agreement with the conventional 0.2\% offset criterion across the investigated conditions. Furthermore, principal component maps extracted from high-temporal-resolution strain fields preceding this transition highlight localized deformation features associated with subsequent failure regions. These features are more clearly distinguishable than competing strain concentrations in the corresponding instantaneous strain maps. The results indicate that PCA-derived deformation signatures remain identifiable despite thermally induced variations in mechanical response. This approach demonstrates the potential of unsupervised dimensionality reduction for extracting physically interpretable information on plastic deformation and early strain localization from experimental full-field measurements.

Unified constitutive model of body-centered cubic metals informed by discrete dislocation dynamics

Jiarui Zhang, Yin Zhang

Predicting plastic flow in body-centered cubic metals across temperature and strain-rate regimes requires distinguishing structural resistance from kinetic contributions. Here, we develop a temperature dependent mobility law for screw dislocation, integrating thermal activation theory and relativistic effects. Through discrete dislocation dynamics simulations, uniaxial tension and compression simulations were conducted on configurations with different initial dislocation densities, elucidating the correlation between plastic strain rate and key parameters such as stress, temperature, and dislocation density. Subsequently, a unified constitutive model incorporating temperature and strain rate is derived based on microscopic mechanisms. The model naturally bridges microscopic mechanisms to continuum response, resolving the additivity dilemma by establishing explicit physical criteria for how structural resistances and competing kinetic mechanisms superimpose. Through rigorous bottom-up simulations, we quantitatively capture the crossover point where phonon drag overtakes thermal activation, providing a mechanism-based explanation of anomalous thermal hardening. This work enables physically consistent predictions of strength and creep behavior across broad temperature and strain-rate regimes, advancing beyond phenomenological descriptions toward transparent, mechanism-based alloy design.

Maximum principle preserving approximation of scalar conservation equations with a fractional Laplacian

Andrea Bonito, Jean-Luc Guermond, Murtazo Nazarov

In this paper, we propose a maximum principle preserving continuous finite element method for scalar conservation laws augmented with a fractional diffusion operator of order $s\in (0,1)$. Conservation laws with fractional diffusion operators arise in a variety of physical models involving anomalous transport and nonlocal regularization, including geophysical flows, plasma physics, and nonlinear acoustics. The fractional Laplacian is approximated using a sinc-quadrature representation, yielding a resolvent-based formulation compatible with continuous finite elements and explicit strong stability preserving Runge--Kutta time integration. A first-order monotone scheme is constructed using graph viscosity and is shown to satisfy a discrete maximum principle under an explicit CFL condition. A high-order extension is obtained via entropy-consistent corrections and convex limiting, achieving second-order accuracy while preserving the maximum principle, and hence positivity, as well as mass conservation in the periodic setting. Numerical experiments, including applications to the Surface Quasi-Geostrophic equation, confirm the second-order accuracy and the robustness of the proposed method on unstructured meshes.

An all-steps-at-once algorithm for time-domain wave propagation in heterogeneous and unbounded media

V. Domínguez, M. Ganesh

We propose, analyze, and implement a convolution quadrature (CQ) algorithm for transient acoustic waves in an unbounded medium consisting of a heterogeneous domain embedded in free space, where the wave speed may be spatially variable and discontinuous. Multistep CQ, applied to the time-domain solution operator, reduces the evolution problem to a family of Helmholtz problems with damped complex frequencies, which can be solved independently in parallel, all time steps at once. The frequency problems are discretized by an overlapping coupling of finite elements (FEM) on a polygonal/polyhedral domain containing the inhomogeneity, and boundary elements (BEM) on a smooth curve enclosed by that domain, in the region where the medium is homogeneous, which accounts exactly for the unbounded exterior. Neither discretization needs to conform to the geometry of the heterogeneous domain, nor to each other. For the resulting FEM--BEM coupling we prove bounds for the solution operators of the transformed problems that are explicit in the Laplace parameter and the wave speed. These bounds yield convergence of the time discretization for A-stable multistep schemes. We also prove explicit estimates on the exponential decay of the transformed solutions away from the heterogeneity, which show that the correction provided by the BEM part is small but relevant, and cheap to compute: a few GMRES iterations suffice, and the correction is needed only over a fixed range of weakly damped frequencies, beyond which it becomes negligible and can be dropped. Numerical experiments confirm the predicted convergence rates in time and space and explore iterative (multigrid) methods for the interior FEM problems.

Learning inadequacy of Bayesian neural operators for PDE-constrained inference and design

Pratyush Kumar Singh, Lianghao Cao, E. Bruce Pitman, Danial Faghihi

Partial differential equations (PDEs) underpin predictive modeling across science, engineering and medicine. Their use in digital twins, parameter inference and optimal design requires repeated solutions across parameters and scenarios. For nonlinear, transient and multiphysics systems, these calculations can make numerical simulation prohibitively expensive. Here we introduce the residual-informed Bayesian neural operator (RI-BNO), which learns operator inadequacy using a posteriori error estimates from the governing PDEs and incorporates its correction and uncertainty into Bayesian training and prediction. We establish local error bounds for the residual-informed estimates under stability and smoothness assumptions. We demonstrate RI-BNO on a non-linear reaction-diffusion benchmark with DeepONet and latent-subspace architectures, Markov chain Monte Carlo inference of glioma parameter fields from magnetic resonance imaging, and Monte Carlo risk-averse optimization of three-dimensional thermal breaks in building envelopes under manufacturing uncertainty. Across the evaluated examples, RI-BNO improves agreement with finite element (FE) reference predictions, parameter posteriors and optimized designs while using half as many full nonlinear or transient training simulations as the vanilla Bayesian neural operator (vBNO). For thermal break design, online optimization time decreases from 178 hours with the FE model to 18 minutes with RI-BNO. These results demonstrate the value of explicit inadequacy modeling in neural operators for reliable PDE-based inference and design.

Long-time asymptotics of advection-diffusion of finite element differential forms

Kaibo Hu, Jindong Wang, Umberto Zerbinati

This paper establishes global numerical analysis for the long-time behavior of Lie advection--diffusion of differential forms discretized by finite element exterior calculus (FEEC).
