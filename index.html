A variational phase-field theory of cohesive fracture at finite deformation

Ye Feng

This paper formulates a kinematically and variationally consistent phase-field theory for cohesive fracture at finite deformation. The theory introduces an independent compliance-damage field, which accommodates both diffuse distributions and singular concentrations. The phase-field is thereby freed from the dual burden of describing crack geometry and material degradation. A rank-one additive split of the deformation gradient makes separation explicit through independent crack-strain and reference-normal fields. All fields are governed by a single principle of minimum potential energy, which consistently couples nonlinear bulk deformation, crack separation, and crack-plane rotation. One-dimensional energy comparisons show that minimization favors Dirac-type concentration of compliance damage and crack strain. Strong displacement discontinuities thus emerge naturally at a finite phase-field length without being prescribed a priori, while the phase-field remains continuous. The concentrated energy recovers the exact bulk--interface structure of a sharp-crack cohesive zone model. General objective hyperelastic bulk potentials and admissible mixed-mode cohesive laws can therefore be prescribed independently, with the nominal traction--separation law unaffected by the bulk law or phase-field length. Diffuse damage can coexist with concentrated cracks, retaining deformation modes for bulk-energy relaxation during nucleation. Two- and three-dimensional examples validate the theory across large stretches, finite rotations, and complex crack paths. Notably, the computed near-tip Cauchy stress closely matches the analytical 1/r crack-tip asymptotic solution of Long et al. (2011) outside the cohesive zone.

Lifelong Learning of Scheduling Rules with Surrogate-assisted Genetic Programming for Dynamic Flexible Job Shop Scheduling

Luyao Zhu, Fangfang Zhang, Yi Mei, Ke Chen, Mengjie Zhang

Genetic programming (GP) has been widely used to learn scheduling rules for dynamic flexible job shop scheduling (DFJSS). However, trained rules tend to only generalise to tasks of similar scales and distributions, and when task characteristics change, learned rules often suffer significant performance degradation. Moreover, fine-tuning pre-trained rules on new tasks often leads to catastrophic forgetting, where prior knowledge is lost. This motivates the need for GP methods that can adapt to new tasks while preserving past knowledge, leading to a lifelong learning paradigm. However, lifelong learning in GP for DFJSS remains largely under-explored. To address this gap, this paper proposes a surrogate-assisted GP method for lifelong learning to handle sequential heterogeneous DFJSS tasks with job or batch arrivals. The framework includes three key components: an adaptive two-stage training strategy to balance plasticity and stability, a task-similarity-based weighting mechanism to regulate knowledge reuse, and an improved surrogate model with distance constraints to enhance estimation accuracy. Experimental results show that the proposed method effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting while maintaining strong adaptability, outperforming several compared methods and demonstrating the potential of lifelong GP in dynamic scheduling environments.

Finite Element Modeling of Fracture in Brittle Foods with Physically Consistent Parameter Calibration: A Practical Case Study on Cooled Dark Chocolate

Patrick Kurzeja, Felix Rörentrop, Kai Langenfeld, Jörn Mosler

Fracture in food can be undesired during production and transportation but also intended during cutting, grinding or mastication. The associated peak load, the deformation at failure and the resulting fragment geometry are all product properties. This work condenses the modeling of such fracture into a physically rigorous recipe that considers the practical constraints imposed by finite element simulations where necessary and applies idealized theoretical assessments where applicable. Cooled dark chocolate serves as a reproducible model material. Two well-established modeling frameworks for Griffith-type brittle fracture are assessed at an affordable spatial discretization: a sharp-interface and a diffuse phase-field approach. The assessment covers the choice of numerical framework, its discretization and a staged identification of the model parameters. Each parameter is identified from the loading stage that governs it: elastic parameters from pre-crack tension and compression, critical energy release rate from three-point-bending peak force, and the phase-field width last. The phase-field width interferes with the structural response, crack curvature and stress fields, depending on its lower bound set by the affordable spatial resolution. Elastic slope, peak force and crack path therefore cannot be met practically by a single parameter set. The objective must be fixed for the calibration of the model parameters and reported together with the numerical parameters and the boundary value problem.

Differentiable Schwarz-Coupled Solvers for Thin Boundary Layer Transport: Inverse Modeling via Agentic AI Optimization

Siva Viknesh, Amirhossein Arzani

High-Péclet-number transport problems give rise to thin boundary layers with steep near-wall gradients whose accurate solution requires high-resolution meshes and could challenge inverse modeling. To address this challenge, we develop an overlapping Schwarz domain-decomposition framework grounded in perturbation theory, in which an inner equation governs near-wall dynamics and an outer equation governs bulk transport, and both subproblems are discretized using the finite element method and assembled into a fully differentiable multidomain physics solver. The formulation permits coarser mesh resolutions, reducing computational cost relative to a single-domain formulation. Exploiting the differentiability of the coupled solver, we formulate inverse problems in which the governing equations and boundary conditions are imposed as hard constraints, with the Schwarz coupling enforcing interface continuity. To accelerate the resulting adjoint-based optimization, we integrate large language models (LLMs) into the inverse workflow in two distinct configurations: a standalone optimizer that proposes successive parameter updates from the adjoint-computed sensitivities and an agentic artificial intelligence (AI) approach with access to the complete solver workflow, enabling it to perform multistage optimization procedures. The agentic AI configuration is shown to be most advantageous when the spatially varying unknown is represented in a functional basis rather than a high-dimensional nodal field representation. In this case, the AI agent grows the library of candidate basis functions during optimization under a prescribed admission policy, admitting predefined families in stages and initializing their parameters. In short, our proposed framework integrates Schwarz-coupled differentiable physics with agentic AI optimization for the inverse modeling of multiscale transport problems.

A two-stage diffusion-driven morphogenesis model with evolving connectivity

Alexander Safonov

Morphogenesis of diatom silica shells involves a sequence of topological transitions: initially separate ribs grow and branch, then merge through bridges, leaving a regular network of pores. Existing models based on diffusion-limited aggregation or reaction-diffusion reproduce either branching or pore formation, but not both within a single framework. Here we propose a two-stage model of diffusion-driven morphogenesis with evolving connectivity. The model is formulated on a fixed finite element mesh with a density field on an active layer. Stage 1 reproduces dendritic growth, where a stimulus based on the local diffusive flux exceeds a threshold. Stage 2 uses an inverted stimulus, where high local flux suppresses precipitation, stabilizing pores between the dendrites. The update rules are analogous to sensitivity in density-based topology optimization and to strain energy density in bone remodeling. The model predicts distinct morphological regimes on each stage, controlled by the respective thresholds. For a reference calculation, the predicted pore network is regular, with approximately uniform spacing, and pores are absent near the domain edges, consistent with observations on diatom valves. The model provides a unified framework for describing both stages of silica morphogenesis and suggests a route for comparing computational predictions with cryo-electron tomography data.

$W^{1,p}$ error estimates of an IPDG and a class of HDG methods for the Poisson problem on convex polyhedral domains

Haitao Leng, Weifeng Qiu

This paper analyzes a symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) method, a hybrid mixed DG method, and two representative hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) methods for the Poisson problem on convex polyhedral domains. For any fixed $ε\in (0,\frac{1}{2})$, we first derive optimal discrete $W^{1,p}$ error estimates for $2\leq p<\frac{1}ε$ by combining the interface Green's functions developed in \cite{lq2026} with the Fubini-type weighted estimates established in this paper. These error estimates lead to discrete $W^{1,q}$-$W^{1,p}$ inf-sup stability for $2\leq q<\frac{1}ε$, where $q$ is the conjugate number of $p$ such that $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1$. Using this stability result and a suitably constructed dual problem, we extend the optimal error estimates to $\frac{1}{1-ε}<p<2$ and subsequently establish the corresponding inf-sup stability for $\frac{1}{1-ε}< q<2$. Thus, both optimal discrete $W^{1,p}$ error estimates and discrete $W^{1,q}$-$W^{1,p}$ inf-sup stability hold throughout the range $\frac{1}{1-ε}<p<\frac{1}ε$, with $q=p/(p-1)$. Note that all of the aforementioned results are free of logarithmic factors and also hold on polyhedral meshes (except for the hybrid mixed DG method). Compared with the conforming finite element method (FEM), establishing such error estimates and inf-sup stability results for DG methods on convex polyhedral domains is more delicate because their analysis involves mesh-dependent norms. Consequently, the operator interpolation theory employed for conforming FEMs cannot be directly applied to DG methods. Finally, a numerical example is presented to illustrate the theoretical results.

Nonlinear visco-elasto-plastic model for the stress-strain and stress-relaxation in metallic glass

Ankit Singh, Alessio Zaccone, Oleg Gendelman, Valeriy V. Ginzburg

We develop a nonlinear visco-elasto-plastic (VEP) model for the stress-strain response of metallic glasses by connecting the deformation dynamics with the material relaxation time obtained from independent stress-relaxation measurements. The material is modeled as a Maxwell-type element, with a nonlinear elastic spring and a stretched-exponential dashpot whose behavior is determined from the separate stress-relaxation data. The model captures the effects of temperature and strain rate on the initial elastic response, stress overshoot, post-yield softening, and steady flow. We independently determine the reference material-time function $X_0(\varepsilon)$ from the stress-relaxation response and the nonlinear material-time variable $X(\varepsilon)$ from the elastic-viscoplastic strain decomposition based on the stress-strain response. We introduce an Eyring-type function or material-time ratio $φ(\varepsilon)=X(\varepsilon)/X_0(\varepsilon)$ which quantifies the change of relaxation dynamics during nonlinear deformation. Our model provides a simple connection between structural relaxation and yielding in metallic glasses.

$H^2$-based equilibrated residual error estimates

Kexin Ding, Yuwen Li

We develop a framework for equilibrated residual a posteriori error analysis of finite element methods, based on decomposing vector- and tensor-valued Sobolev spaces into $H^2$ and $H^1$ spaces. To estimate the residual functional acting on $H^2$ component, the method reconstructs symmetric and piecewise polynomial tensors on vertex patches in broken $H(\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div})$ spaces. We apply the proposed framework to conforming finite element discretizations of biharmonic equation, Poisson's equation, and linear elasticity in mixed form. As a result, we obtain several novel equilibrated residual a posteriori error estimates, providing guaranteed error control for energy error of biharmonic equation, $L^2$ norm error of Poisson's equation, and stress error of mixed linear elasticity. They also serve as the first polynomial-degree-robust a posteriori lower bounds of discretization errors. Numerical experiments illustrate the effectiveness of the $H^2$-based error estimators.

Scaling of Interface Variability in Aerospace Composite Structures: A Multi-scale Stochastic Approach from Local Identification to Panel Response

Elton Donfack-Siewe, Sylvain Dubreuil, Christian Fagiano, Jérôme Morio, Jean-Philippe Navarro

Quantifying random spatial variations in composite interface properties is an essential prerequisite for the high-fidelity propagation of material properties variabilities through structural numerical models. This study proposes a multi-scale stochastic framework designed to bridge the gap between local interfacial characterization at the coupon scale and the global response of industrial aerospace panels, focusing specifically on the skin-stringer bond line. Building upon previous stochastic modeling and identification methodologies for Mode I fracture, we first extend the likelihood-free Bayesian calibration to Mode II configurations to characterize interfacial stochastic parameters using experimental coupon data. To address the scale-up challenge, we employ a simulation-based Stochastic Finite Element Method (SFEM) to contrast two distinct probabilistic strategies -- spatially uniform variations (Paradigm 2) and spatially correlated random fields (Paradigm 3) -- against the traditional industrial B-basis standard (Paradigm 1). The numerical results reveal a dual mechanism in uncertainty propagation depending on the targeted airframe design criterion. For global performance, such as the ultimate load capacity, a significant scale effect leads to an uncertainty attenuation phenomenon; statistical averaging smooths out local heterogeneities, demonstrating that a computationally efficient spatially uniform approach is sufficient. Conversely, for local integrity, such as the threshold and spatial localization of early damage initiation, the inclusion of spatially correlated random fields becomes indispensable to capture premature degradation driven by local material weak spots. This balanced probabilistic treatment offers a route toward test derisking and predictive uncertainty management in the design of damage-tolerant aerospace composite airframes.

Towards Understanding the Impact of Plasticity Loss on Reinforcement Learning in Stochastic Environments

Philipp Bordne, André Biedenkapp

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) is a powerful framework for sequential decision-making. Despite many high-profile successes in recent years, deep RL is known to be brittle. An emerging line of research attributes this brittleness to a loss of plasticity in neural networks. However, plasticity loss has so far been understudied in stochastic environments. We study this interplay systematically, training SAC agents on three DeepMind Control tasks augmented with controlled levels of observation and action noise. We find that plasticity-preserving methods increase learning robustness on the two harder tasks, with their advantage growing as observation noise increases, while combining resets with layer normalization underperforms resets alone at every observation noise level tested. Further, stochasticity can accelerate the loss of plasticity in harder environments, whereas in easier settings noise can act as a regularizer that sustains plasticity. Lastly, plasticity loss is most pronounced in the early stages of training. In the most challenging stochastic settings, TD instability rather than gradual plasticity loss becomes the dominant failure mode.

Continual adaptation requires deciding not only how to update a model, but whether to commit an update without exceeding a declared budget for changes to protected behavior. We introduce Certified Anchor-Based Adaptation (CABA), a candidate-agnostic post-update gate that reserves historical observations from candidate optimization, compares old and candidate policies on frozen representations, and selects the largest tested interpolation coefficient whose worst-anchor forward KL divergence remains within budget. The finite check requires neither action labels nor complete trajectories. We develop a theory-led certification framework that separates measured anchor retention from conditional off-anchor guarantees. The experiment illustrates a retention-plasticity trade-off and does not establish foundation-model or robotic performance. CABA provides an auditable retention check for candidate generators exposing comparable policy distributions and a declared update path, while deployment also requires separate evidence of current-task utility.

The presented work proposes a multiscale framework for the numerical and analytical determination of the elastic constants of the intermetallic nickel aluminide (NiAl) at the microscopic scale. Molecular dynamics (MD) calculations have been employed to evaluate the stiffness tensors of a single NiAl monocrystalline body with cubic Pm$\bar{3}$m symmetry. The atomistic results were transferred to the upper scale as an input to micromechanical models. Here, finite element modeling (FEM) and analytical homogenization methods were used to simulate the elastic behavior of polycrystalline NiAl, with special attention to grain anisotropy and its morphology. The microstructural features of the heterogeneous body were evaluated experimentally using electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) and subsequently used to generate a 3D polycrystalline geometric model that preserves realistic distributions of grain orientations and grain sizes. The reconstructed NiAl polycrystal was subjected to uniaxial tensile test simulations using FEM to evaluate the effective Young's modulus ($E$) and Poisson's ratio ($ν$). The numerical results were confronted with the experimental values of $E$ and~$ν$. With these model assumptions, a very good agreement between numerical and experimental results was achieved. Finally, the experimental results were compared with classical analytical estimates. Among the analytical approaches, the Hashin-Shtrikman upper bound and self-consistent approximation reproduced the experimental Young's modulus with minor deviations.

A Preconditioned Quantum Linear Solver for Periodic Two-Phase Heat Conduction with Mesh-Independent Condition Number

Krishnan Suresh

We consider here computational homogenization of a periodic two-phase conduction problem. Quantum linear-system algorithms for its finite element discretization are limited by the condition number of the stiffness matrix. This condition number grows quadratically with the number of elements per side. Classically, preconditioning by the homogeneous (single-phase) operator bounds the condition number by the conductivity contrast, for every mesh size. On a quantum computer, however, a preconditioner encoded as a separate factor multiplies the subnormalizations, and the quadratic dependency returns. In this paper, we absorb the closed-form inverse square root of the homogeneous operator into the finite element factors. The preconditioned operator then becomes the product of a norm-preserving gradient operator $\mat{V}$, a diagonal matrix of scaled conductivities, and $\mat{V}^T$. Each factor has norm at most one. The block encoding therefore has subnormalization one, and its condition number is bounded by just the conductivity contrast, for every mesh size and in every dimension. The operator $\mat{V}$ is applied as a quantum Fourier transform, a small rotation at each wavenumber, and an inverse transform. Quantum singular value transformation then block-encodes the inverse of the preconditioned operator. We test the method numerically on three periodic cells with up to $10^6$ unknowns. The results confirm that the condition number does not depend on the mesh, and the energy-norm error of the inverse is below the polynomial tolerance at every mesh. On meshes of $4 \times 4$ and $8 \times 8$ elements, we also build the entire inverse as one Qiskit circuit and simulate it by state vector, with the rotation at each wavenumber built from uniformly controlled rotations. The output state agrees with the finite element solution to within the polynomial tolerance.
