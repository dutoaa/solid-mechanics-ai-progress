Retention-constrained fine-tuning of a pre-trained aluminium MACE potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity

Yue Wu, Yuan Tian, Deng Pan, Mingwei Chen

Adapting a pre-trained machine-learning interatomic potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity requires improving defect accuracy without degrading the near-equilibrium elastic and vibrational response. Here we achieve this balance for the aluminium MACE potential QP17 by integrating conditional readout uncertainty, iterative full-parameter updates and protocol-compatible relative-energy anchoring. Sensitivities over 144 linear readout weights guide acquisition, with within-batch covariance updates and evolving neural representations. After four active-learning rounds (32 acquired configurations and 400 gradient updates), force-uncertainty acquisition reduces mean development force error from 22.43 to 21.52 meV/Angstrom, outperforming random sampling (22.70), geometry-diversity selection (22.46) and uniform training across all 104 candidate labels (22.71) under the same update budget. All three force-uncertainty runs satisfy bulk-mechanical retention criteria: historical relative-energy error decreases from 0.375 to 0.207 meV/atom, while harmonic phonon root-mean-square drift remains below 0.05 THz. Matched objective ablations reveal that relative-energy anchoring suppresses harmonic drift. Out-of-sample evaluations across planar faults, twin boundaries and grain boundaries demonstrate transferable gains in defect energies and deviatoric stresses, even though virial stresses are never fitted. A closed-loop molecular dynamics and density functional theory (MD-DFT) pilot confirms that automated acceptance gating intercepts updates that violate historical elastic-group energy or vibrational tolerances. Together, these components establish a retention-constrained fine-tuning protocol for improving plastic-deformation observables while preserving the tested bulk mechanical response.

Constrained neural softening functions for the Mullins effect in parameter-dependent materials

Dominik K. Klein, Jonas Fey, Maximilian Kannapinn, Oliver Weeger

Accurately capturing the Mullins effect across varying material compositions remains challenging, as constitutive models must unify flexible representations of stress softening with physical consistency and robust parameter calibration. In this work, we investigate parameter-dependent physics-augmented neural network constitutive models within the pseudo-elastic modeling framework, focusing on structural constraints that ensure physically admissible neural softening functions. This paper has three key contributions: (1) We formulate different neural softening functions within the pseudo-elastic framework, including a multiplicative decomposition of the softening function that enables parameter-dependent ansatz functions satisfying common mechanical constitutive conditions by construction. (2) We derive a sufficient condition for ellipticity of pseudo-elastic constitutive models, which can be enforced in the constitutive models by construction. (3) We systematically investigate how flexibility and structural constraints influence the performance of neural softening functions in terms of prediction accuracy and calibration robustness. For this, we consider multiaxial experimental data of carbon black-filled EPDM rubber synthesized with varying amounts of curatives. The findings of our work suggest that neural softening functions are a promising extension of the classical pseudo-elastic framework and, when subject to suitable constraints, can improve predictive accuracy for highly nonlinear, parameter-dependent materials.

Universal neural network approximation of symmetric polyconvex energies and envelopes

Florian Gammer

Symmetric polyconvexity provides a sufficient condition for weak lower semicontinuity in small-strain elasticity and a tractable lower bound for the relaxation of non-(quasi)convex energies. Since classical algorithms for computing such functions can be computationally expensive, neural network approximations have recently emerged as an alternative. A central difficulty with establishing such neural network based approaches for symmetric polyconvexity is the additional monotonicity property. As we show, the corresponding weight restrictions on ReLU-based input-convex networks do not yield universal approximation of the lifted representers. Here, we introduce symmetric polyconvex neural networks (SPCNNs) for constitutive modeling and approximation of symmetric polyconvex envelopes. The deep architecture combines convexity with the monotonicity required of a representer in the space of minors. We prove a universal approximation theorem for symmetric polyconvex energies, without requiring isotropy, and quantify the number of affine pieces attainable by the lifted network and derive reduced architectures for isotropic energies. Numerical examples illustrate stress-based regression and approximation of the symmetric polyconvex envelope of a double-well potential. Symmetric polyconvexity is guaranteed by construction, while the accuracy of the fitted energies and envelopes is assessed numerically.

Our work highlights the potential of AI-based generative modeling as a key component of computational frameworks for accelerating AM materials qualification and certification.

Curvature-induced fracture mode coupling in doubly curved shells

Riccardo Brambilla, Siddhant Kumar, Bianca Giovanardi

Classical shell fracture theories predominantly consider cracks aligned with principal curvature directions in cylindrical and spherical shells under tensile mode or pressure loading, leaving the fracture behavior of arbitrarily oriented cracks in doubly curved shells under general loading poorly understood. Here, we introduce a semi-analytical framework based on Reissner shallow-shell theory to investigate isotropic shells with positive, zero, and negative Gaussian curvature. The formulation computes stress intensity factors, energy release rates, and crack-lip displacements for arbitrarily inclined through-thickness cracks in spheres, ellipsoids, cylinders, hyperboloids, and minimal surfaces under general mechanical loading. We show that shell curvature couples opening, sliding, and tearing, allowing a single loading component to activate multiple fracture modes, unlike in flat plates. This coupling depends strongly on crack orientation and the normal, parallel, and torsional components of the shell curvature. Ellipsoids exhibit weak coupling, hyperboloids generally exhibit the strongest coupling, and cylinders display the most complex orientation-dependent responses. Moreover, in-plane shear can induce crack closure in hyperboloidal shells, suggesting a potential geometric mechanism for enhanced damage resistance. These findings elucidate the role of local curvature in fracture-mode coupling and provide a mechanistic foundation for the analysis of shellular (meta-)structures with engineered curvature distributions.

Evolution of microstructure and crystallographic texture in a novel Zr-4Nb-2Al alloy during hot rotary swaging

Adam Strnad, Jana Šmilauerová, Josef Stráský, Lukáš Horák, Jan Čapek, Alžběta Endrychová

A multi-step induction-assisted hot rotary swaging route was developed for the novel dual-phase Zr-4Nb-2Al alloy to investigate the evolution of microstructure, crystallographic texture, and mechanical properties during sequential deformation. Phase-transformation temperatures determined by electrical resistivity measurements were used to select processing conditions in the single-phase $β$ field at 880°C and the $α+β$ field at 780°C. Induction heating with thermographic temperature control enabled reduction of the initial 12 mm rod to 6.3 mm, corresponding to a total area reduction of 72%. Swaging in the $β$ field refined millimetre-sized prior-$β$ grains to approximately 100 $μ$m, while subsequent cooling produced a fine lamellar $α+β$ microstructure. Further deformation in the $α+β$ field disrupted the lamellar structure, promoted globularisation and recrystallisation of the $α$ phase, and refined the $β$ phase. The microstructural evolution was accompanied by a pronounced $\langle 11\bar{2}0\rangle_α\parallel\mathrm{SD}$ fibre texture in the $α$ phase, whereas the $β$ phase exhibited predominantly $\langle 110\rangle_β\parallel\mathrm{SD}$ together with a $\langle 111\rangle_β\parallel\mathrm{SD}$ component. The phase constitution remained comparatively stable, with approximately 12-15 wt.% retained $β$ throughout processing. Subsequent $α+β$ swaging decreased the compressive yield strength from 1103 to 839 MPa while increasing plastic deformability, with a maximum fracture strain of 32%. Radial hardness heterogeneity was progressively eliminated. These results demonstrate that induction-assisted rotary swaging in selected phase fields provides an effective route for tailoring microstructure, texture, and mechanical response in dual-phase zirconium alloys.

An unconditionally convergent BDF2-type finite element integrator for the inertial Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert equation

Michele Aldé, Hywel Normington, Dirk Praetorius, Michele Ruggeri

We propose and analyze a fully discrete numerical scheme to approximate solutions to the inertial Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (iLLG) equation, a state-of-the-art model to describe the dynamics of the magnetization at subpicosecond time scales. The method employs first-order finite elements for the spatial discretization and the two-step backward differentiation formula (BDF2) for the time discretization, where the inertial term is treated by a convex combination of BDF2 and the backward Euler method. We prove well-posedness and unconditional stability of the scheme, and obtain explicit bounds for the error in the realization of the iLLG-inherent unit length constraint satisfied by the magnetization. Moreover, we derive the discrete energy law satisfied by the approximations, which resembles the inherent dissipative energy law of the model. Finally, with a constructive approach, we prove unconditional convergence of the approximations toward a weak solution of the problem. Numerical experiments assess the performance of the scheme and validate the theoretical findings.

Robust augmented Block Preconditioning for two-phase Incompressible Inductionless MHD

Bijie Song, Lingxiao Li, Jie Zhang

In this paper, we develop a robust augmented block preconditioner for three-dimensional, two-phase, incompressible inductionless MHD equations. We employ a high-order monolithic and charge-conservative arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian (ALE) finite element method as base scheme, for which the solution variables are current density, velocity, electric potential and pressure. Grad-div stabilization is added in both the current density equation and discrete momentum equation. Backward Euler method is used for the time discretization. At each time step, fully coupled solution strategy is adopted. This leads to large-scale, ill-conditioned, nonsymmetric, and indefinite dual saddle-point system. To solve this system efficiently, we construct an augmented block preconditioner within the constraint preconditioning framework. The ultimate preconditioner combines the augmented Lagrangian technique with approximate block factorization for current density sub-system and fluid sub-system. The fully coupled four-field system is then solved using preconditioned FGMRES iteration. Numerical experiments confirm the high-order accuracy and discrete charge conservation of the ALE-FEM. Drop of heavy fluid in light fluid is taken as benchmark to demonstrate the robustness of the preconditioner with respect to the discontinuity of physical parameters and the mesh size.

Structure-preserving discretisations for the quasi-incompressible, thermal Maxwell-Stefan equations

Boris D. Andrews, Aaron Baier-Reinio, Aaron Brunk, Patrick E. Farrell, Mária Lukáčová-Medviďová

We study a thermal Maxwell-Stefan-Navier-Stokes system modelling miscible mixtures under a quasi-incompressibility constraint. The system consists of the conservation laws for mass and momentum, and the balance law for entropy, which is preferred over the balance law for energy because it facilitates the development of two structure-preserving discretisation methods. The first method applies a semi-implicit Euler scheme in time, together with suitable finite elements in space, which guarantees generation of entropy and enforces the quasi-incompressibility constraint pointwise, but numerically dissipates total energy. The second method employs a continuous Petrov-Galerkin discretisation in time, which also generates entropy and satisfies the constraint pointwise, and in addition conserves the total energy and allows high-order time approximations. The structure-preserving properties and behaviour of both numerical methods are demonstrated by a series of numerical experiments.

Reliability Assessment of FRP-RC Columns Using AK-MCS with a Piecewise Two-Branch Approach

Arndt Goldack, Richard Koehler, Ahmed Usama Syed

Slender concrete columns reinforced with fiber-reinforced polymer (FRP) bars fail at medium to high axial load levels either by concrete crushing or by loss of stability, while the tensile strains in the reinforcement remain far below the rupture strain. Each non-linear finite element (FE) analysis yields only the capacity of the governing failure mode, and the governing mode changes over a broad zone in which both capacities are nearly equal. Both characteristics conflict with active-learning Kriging-assisted Monte Carlo simulation (AK-MCS), which assumes a single, smooth limit-state function. This paper proposes a piecewise, two-branch AK-MCS framework in which each failure mode is represented by its own Kriging model, trained only on the FE analyses in which that mode governs, and the governing prediction is the minimum of both. Safeguards against data starvation keep rarely governing branches usable. After convergence, additional FE analyses are placed based on a leave-one-out calibration check and on the width of the confidence interval on the reliability index. A design-point search on the surrogate and importance sampling at the design points provide the reliability index even where direct Monte Carlo simulation finds no failure. For an FRP-reinforced concrete column example with mixed failure, 79% and 62% of the training points lie near the mode boundary. The reliability index from the design-point search agrees with the Monte Carlo estimate to within 0.05, and an FE analysis at the design point confirms the surrogate to within 0.6%.

A Two-Grid Finite Element Method for the Nonlinear Poisson-Boltzmann Equation Based on a Solution Decomposition

Zhen Chao, Jingchun Chen, Cong He

The nonlinear Poisson--Boltzmann equation (PBE) is a widely used implicit-solvent model for the electrostatics of biomolecules in ionic solvent. Its numerical solution is complicated by the point charges, the dielectric jump across the molecular surface, and the exponential nonlinearity. We study a two-grid finite element method for the PBE built on the solution decomposition $u=G+Ψ+\Phit$ of Xie [J.~Comput.~Phys.~275 (2014) 294--309], in which $G$ carries the singularities analytically, $Ψ$ solves a linear interface problem, and $\Phit$ solves a semilinear interface problem for an arbitrary number of ionic species. The nonlinear problem for $\Phit$ is solved on a coarse mesh only, followed by a single Newton correction on the fine mesh, so that only symmetric positive definite linear systems are solved on the fine mesh.
