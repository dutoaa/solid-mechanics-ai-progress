Self-Supervised Learning of Universal Latent Spaces for Operator Learning

Sepehr Mousavi, Levi Lingsch, Siddhartha Mishra, Laura De Lorenzis

Neural operators fit their representation of functions to the task they are trained on, so every new task rebuilds a reduced space from labeled data. Following the self-supervised pre-training that made representations shareable in natural language processing and computer vision, we decouple representation learning from operator learning. A functional autoencoder is pre-trained on analytic functions synthesized on the fly, with no solver and no labels, to represent functions on arbitrary point clouds, and the latent space it induces is frozen and reused across tasks. The representation is exactly equivariant to translation, dilation, and amplitude, and reconstructs solutions of partial differential equations (PDEs) and natural images on geometries it never saw. Being task-agnostic, the latent space can be interpreted in its own right. The whole space is used and decodes to valid fields, it preserves the ordering of distances between physical fields, its geodesics interpolate physical evolutions, and edit directions transfer across fields. With frozen representation maps, a dataset is encoded once and a neural operator is trained entirely on cached embeddings, which saves memory and training time. On 28 tasks in fluid dynamics, solid mechanics, acoustics, and the Helmholtz and Poisson equations, this paradigm can exceed the accuracy of state-of-the-art end-to-end architectures, with up to an order of magnitude higher throughput and memory savings that fit models nearly two orders of magnitude larger on consumer-grade GPUs. At inference, latent rollouts run up to an order of magnitude faster than regular rollouts, and chunked inference processes functions of over half a billion points on a single consumer-grade GPU.

A Tensor-Network Chebyshev-Galerkin Method for Boundary-Value Problems in Computational Mechanics

Juan José Rodríguez-Aldavero, Ángel de la Rosa Velasco, Juan José García-Ripoll

Quantum-inspired numerical methods for partial differential equations represent discretized fields and operators as quantics tensor trains (QTT), so that their cost depends on how compressible the problem is rather than on the number of unknowns. Global spectral formulations in this setting have so far relied on Fourier bases, restricted to periodic problems, or on collocation, which does not preserve the variational structure exploited by tensor-network solvers. We introduce a QTT Chebyshev-Galerkin method that assembles and solves global spectral systems directly in compressed form, without forming the underlying arrays. Starting from the weak formulation, the method handles coupled elliptic systems with variable coefficients, smoothly mapped geometries, and mixed Dirichlet-Robin boundary conditions, and yields symmetric positive-definite systems suited to variational tensor-network solvers. Parameters and random inputs enter as additional coordinates, so the same construction returns the solution over an entire parameter space in a single solve. In linear elasticity, the method retains spectral convergence and reaches errors below $10^{-8}$ on a three-dimensional benchmark, beyond those attained by QTT finite differences and Q2/Q4 FEniCSx finite elements over the tested range. A six-parameter cantilever design study covering $1.7\times10^{7}$ designs is solved in a single 92s run, and a stochastic problem with ten random coordinates is represented with $2.4\times10^{6}$ times less memory than its full coefficient array.

CloakFEM: Agentic FEM Code Generation for Red-Teaming Computational Resource Exhaustion

Li Zheng, Simin Chen

Finite element method (FEM) solvers are foundational to scientific and engineering simulation, yet their susceptibility to \emph{efficiency attacks} remains largely unexplored. An adversary can craft syntactically valid and runnable FEM programs that complete successfully while forcing excessive computational work, enabling computational denial-of-service (DoS) attacks. Finding such inputs is challenging because FEM programs occupy a vast, structured space constrained by numerical, geometric, and constitutive requirements. We introduce CloakFEM, a domain-guided LLM-agent framework for automatically generating runnable adversarial FEM programs that amplify solver computation. CloakFEM combines LLM-guided search with an FEM-specific intermediate representation and a deterministic validity gate that enforces runnable constraints. Across three FEM stacks, 17 problem classes, and 1,068 seeds, CloakFEM discovers adversarial inputs that substantially amplify solver work while remaining valid and solvable. In the most extreme case, solver iterations increase from 43 to 247,367, a \(5{,}752.72\times\) amplification. CloakFEM outperforms five generic black-box baselines and retains an advantage over evolutionary search under a controlled bounded action space. Cross-solver analysis further reveals both problem-intrinsic and solver-specific sources of vulnerability. Our results uncover an underexplored efficiency-attack surface in FEM solvers and show that runnable FEM programs can serve as adversarial inputs.

Two-level additive Schwarz preconditioners with GDSW coarse spaces for high-order discretizations in 3D

Tommaso Bevilacqua, Axel Klawonn, Martin Lanser, Adam Wasiak

Overlapping Schwarz methods are among the most successful domain decomposition preconditioners for large-scale finite element simulations. In this work, the Generalized Dryja-Smith-Widlund (GDSW), GDSW$^*$, and Reduced GDSW (RGDSW) coarse spaces are studied for three-dimensional high-order finite element method (FEM) and virtual element method (VEM) for the Poisson equation and the equations of linear elasticity. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study of GDSW-type coarse spaces for polynomial degrees $k\ge 3$ in three dimensions, for either discretization. In 3D VEM, the face degrees of freedom (DOFs) are moments instead of point values. The kernel of the differential operator, which defines the interface values of the GDSW coarse basis functions, must be represented through these moments. The construction is therefore no longer purely algebraic and, without this correction, the coarse level loses its effect for $k\ge 3$. FEM and VEM are tested on hexahedral meshes, and VEM is additionally tested on polyhedral ones. Polynomial degrees up to $k=5$ and runs on up to $4\,096$ subdomains and parallel cores are included in the numerical study. Numerical experiments show scalable iteration counts for all coarse space variants for the Poisson problem, illustrating the expected trade-off between the coarse space size and robustness. Further experiments for linear elasticity confirms that the moment-based construction extends naturally from the scalar to the vector-valued problem.

Denys Musiienko, Taras Kovaliuk, Petr Harcuba, Oleg Heczko, Ross H. Colman

Homogeneous single-crystal growth of Ni--Mn--Ga magnetic shape-memory alloys is limited by incongruent melting, Mn reactivity, and longitudinal composition segregation. We report a modified optical floating-zone method in which growth is performed inside a transparent sapphire crucible, stabilising the molten zone while preserving optical heating and visual monitoring. The resulting crystals show improved growth reproducibility and controlled macroscopic geometry. To interpret the measured segregation profiles, a transient finite-element heat-transfer model in COMSOL Multiphysics was coupled to an effective molten-zone mass-balance calculation. These results show that deviations from ideal Scheil--Pfann behaviour arise from the evolving molten-zone geometry and solidification conditions. The experimentally constrained model therefore provides a practical means of reconstructing otherwise inaccessible growth conditions and a basis for optimisation of sapphire-confined floating-zone growth of incongruently melting alloys.

Periodic composites with cohesive interfaces and matrix plasticity require nonlinear cell solutions in multiscale analysis. Influence matrices recover macroscopic stress and consistent tangent. One back-substitution per increment checks that the matrix outside the retained zone remains elastic. While this holds, the reduction is exact relative to the full discretisation, also with a plastic matrix, and requires no training. In an E-glass/epoxy cell, eight of nine two-dimensional paths agree with the full cell to 1e-11 in stress and tangent; the check flags the ninth. In adaptive finite element squared (FE$^2$) simulations of a perforated plate, the reduced solver reproduces the full-cell response within solver tolerances, 1.51 times faster for glass/polypropylene and slower for glass/epoxy. Independent Ansys models agree to below 1e-3 in cell stress and about 0.6% in plate reactions, although local damage and plasticity onsets differ. The advantage depends on zone size, active set and adaptation costs.

Geometric Multigrid Preconditioners for conforming Finite Element Methods on Arbitrarily Agglomerated Meshes

Santiago Badia, Jordi Manyer

Geometric multigrid methods for conforming finite element discretisations classically require a nested mesh hierarchy, forcing coarse meshes to be conforming, which is unsuitable for general domains. In order to eliminate this geometric constraint, we introduce and analyse a family of multilevel auxiliary space preconditioners. At the first level, a conforming Lagrangian discretisation on a fine simplicial mesh is preconditioned by a non-conforming polytopal discretisation posed on an arbitrarily agglomerated coarse mesh. At the subsequent levels, the coarse problem is solved by a geometric multigrid method for that polytopal discretisation on further agglomerated meshes, so that the whole hierarchy is generated from the fine mesh by agglomeration alone. We consider two polytopal discretisations, a symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method and a hybrid high-order method. The prolongation operator at the first level is an element-wise interpolation onto the fine mesh followed by nodal averaging, preceded in the hybrid high-order case by a local potential reconstruction. For point-Jacobi and vertex-star patch additive Schwarz smoothers, we prove that the condition number of the preconditioned system is bounded by $O((H/h)^3)$, where $H/h$ is the coarsening ratio, and by $O((H/h)^2)$ for the DG variant with penalty proportional to $H/h$. Numerical experiments in two and three dimensions, on structured and unstructured meshes with agglomerates generated by automatic clustering algorithms, confirm the robustness of the preconditioners.

Remap for ALE Hydrodynamics via Direct Interpolation and Constraint Optimization

Brendan Keith, Dohyun Kim, Boyan Lazarov, Ketan Mittal, Cosmin G. Petra, Mathias R. Schmidt

This work presents an optimization-based remap approach for arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian (ALE) hydrodynamics that preserves sharp features and physical bounds while conserving global quantities such as volume, mass, momentum, and total energy. Achieving these properties is the main challenge in remap methods. The presented approach is well-suited for high-order finite element (FE) discretizations and ALE methods that allow for large, purely Lagrangian displacements between successive remeshing and remapping steps. Recent advances in parallel, GPU-capable interpolation routines enable the initial transfer of all fields via direct physical-space interpolation, avoiding computationally costly and diffusive pseudo-transient PDE-based advection. The interpolation produces bounded fields with minimal numerical diffusion. However, it may violate the desired conservation of global properties. Thus, a subsequent optimization step restores conservation while preserving physical bounds. The presented method provides minimally diffusive remapping of finite element fields of arbitrary order. Moreover, it directly remaps quantities defined at integration points, eliminating the need for projection onto finite element spaces. The approach also mitigates typical challenges associated with synchronizing primal variables and conserved quantities in advection-based remap methods. The proposed optimization-based remap method is evaluated using standard two- and three-dimensional remap benchmarks and full ALE hydrodynamics simulations.

Trapping and Fracture of Particles in Three-Sphere Ball-Milling Collisions

We develop a reduced three-sphere collision description in which trapping is governed by impact geometry and friction, while fracture inside the trapping regime is evaluated from DEM output using stress-based and contact-force-based normalized criteria. A Second-Order Semi-Implicit Projection Time-Stepping Scheme for the Shallow-Water Equations on the Sphere Using Compatible Finite Elements

Karina Kowalczyk, Colin Cotter

We propose a second-order, semi-implicit time-stepping scheme for the shallow-water equations on the sphere discretised with compatible finite elements. The scheme splits the advection operator from the wave operator, applying a DG-upwind SSPRK3 method to the advection part and an $H(\text{div})$-conforming implicit discretisation to the wave part, solved efficiently via hybridisation. The scheme follows a three-step symmetric splitting: an explicit half-step, a full advection step, and an implicit half-step that simultaneously projects the velocity back into the $H(\text{div})$ space. This symmetry guarantees global second-order accuracy and confines the stability restriction to the CFL condition of the advection step alone. The time-step can therefore be extended beyond this limit by subcycling the advection step without additional implicit solves. The method is implemented in Firedrake and validated against a standard suite of spherical shallow-water benchmarks, demonstrating stable results for Courant numbers up to 1 (with two advection subcycles) with second-order finite elements, second-order convergence, and good parallel scaling.

This paper extends our prior work on interface conditions between two poroelastic domains to the case when a poroelastic domain interacts with a pure fluid. The porous medium is assumed to be deformable and modeled following mixture theory. When not in a mixture, each constituent of the porous domain is assumed to be incompressible. An arbitrary Lagrangian--Eulerian formulation is developed for both the fluid and porous domains. The fluid velocity and pressure fields are allowed to be discontinuous across the pure fluid/porous domain interface. The model implementation is verified using the method of manufactured solutions, and a sample problem is solved to compare an inert interface with a fully dissipative one. The results show satisfactory convergence rates, while the proposed model successfully reproduces the analytical solution of a benchmark problem using conventional jump conditions. Furthermore, the findings highlight the significant influence of the selected interface model on the behavior of the system.
