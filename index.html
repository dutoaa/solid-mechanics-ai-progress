Nondimensionalization, Variational Inequalities and Uncertainty Quantification in a Morphoelastic Model for Burn Injury Healing

Wilbert den Hertog, Fred Vermolen

Mathematical modelling of burn injury healing involves coupling biological processes with mechanical deformation, resulting in computationally stiff, nonlinear partial differential equations that severely limit the scalability of existing numerical solutions. We present a computational framework addressing these challenges. By nondimensionalizing the model and performing scaling analysis, we show that chemotaxis and inertia are negligible, permitting a quasi-static momentum approach. To enforce non-negativity constraints, we implement a Variational Inequality formulation using the Finite Element Method in FEniCSx, and verify it via numerical convergence studies and mass balances. We identify high-order parameter interactions, specifically equilibrium collagen density and signalling dynamics, as the primary drivers of solver stability. A sensitivity analysis of nearly four million simulations reveals that isolated parameter variations predominantly govern the maximum wound contraction. In contrast, higher-order interactions determine the timing of maximum contraction and the permanent contracture. However, no significant pairwise parameter interactions were found, suggesting that the model's behaviour is governed by higher-order interactions, highlighting the inherent complexity of the wound-healing process.

A GENERIC-Informed Neural Network for Generative Rheology

Imanol García de Beristain, David Nieto Simavilla, Marco Ellero

We present GINNGER (GENERIC-Informed Neural Network for GEnerative Rheology), a framework for discovering thermodynamically admissible constitutive equations from microstructural flow data. Unlike existing approaches that learn constitutive operators, GINNGER reformulates constitutive discovery as the learning of the underlying thermodynamic generators from which constitutive equations emerge. Embedding the GENERIC formalism into the learning process restricts the search to constitutive models that satisfy the first and second laws of thermodynamics by construction, eliminating the need to prescribe phenomenological constitutive equations a priori. A second conceptual advance is a spectral formulation of constitutive discovery in the intrinsic eigenspace of the polymer conformation tensor. This provides a kinematics-independent thermodynamic state space in which the thermodynamic generators depend only on the instantaneous microstructural state, while flow kinematics acts solely as an external driver transporting the system through this space. Consequently, data from shear, extensional, and mixed flows contribute to the discovery of a single constitutive model that generalizes across deformation protocols and flow geometries. By learning thermodynamic generators rather than high-dimensional constitutive operators, GINNGER substantially reduces the complexity of the discovery problem while preserving thermodynamic consistency, material objectivity, and physical interpretability by construction. The framework is validated using high-fidelity simulations, accurately reconstructing constitutive behavior and predicting complex flow fields across previously unseen geometries. GINNGER establishes a new paradigm for constitutive discovery by combining generative, spectral, and thermodynamically grounded learning within a unified framework for data-driven modeling of complex fluids.

A deep energy method formulation for phase-field modelling of ductile fracture

Nojus Plungė, Peter Brommer, Rachel S. Edwards, Emmanouil G. Kakouris

This work extends the Deep Energy Method to phase-field modelling of ductile fracture in elasto-plastic solids. Its key contribution is an incremental DEM-based formulation that couples history-dependent J2 plasticity and plasticity-dependent fracture toughness with separate energy-optimised displacement and phase-field representations. The formulation combines the second-order Ambrosio-Tortorelli phase-field fracture model with small-strain J2 plasticity and linear isotropic hardening. A volumetric-deviatoric split defines the elastic crack-driving energy, while accumulated equivalent plastic strain governs the degradation of local fracture toughness. Plastic internal variables are updated locally through radial return. The reduced energy is minimised alternately with respect to the displacement and phase fields, represented by a ReZero residual neural network and spatially localised Gaussian radial basis functions with trainable centres, respectively. This split addresses the diffuse phase-field profiles observed in preliminary calculations with a shared global network. Alternating optimisation is combined with transfer learning to reuse trainable parameters and optimiser states across loading increments, while the converged constitutive history is carried forward separately at quadrature points. The numerical results demonstrate close agreement with finite-element reference solutions in predicting force-displacement responses, energy evolution, plastic deformation and crack propagation. The framework captures the main features of ductile fracture, although accuracy becomes more sensitive to loading increments near abrupt fracture events.

In contrast, continual learning in large language models (LLMs) remains vulnerable to catastrophic forgetting. A promising direction is to identify functionally specialized subnetworks within LLMs that enable targeted and resource-efficient adaptation. Using gradient-attribution profiling, we localize cognition-associated functional subnetworks exhibiting extremely low mutual overlap. Building on this finding, we propose Function-isolated Memory-Efficient Optimization (CiMEO), a continual learning framework that routes each training example to its corresponding functional subnetwork, restricting parameter updates and optimizer states to selected regions. Implementing the EIT complete electrode model through Robin boundary conditions

Bing-Zhou Chen, Amelia Deakin, Andy Adler, Paul D. Ledger, William R. B. Lionheart

Objective. Medical electrical impedance tomography systems prescribe electrode currents. Accurate voltages, even on passive electrodes, require the complete electrode model (CEM), the most accurate model known for skin electrodes. Its current constraints specify total electrode currents, whereas standard finite element boundary interfaces impose local conditions. We describe how to implement the CEM through Robin boundary conditions, which are available in standard FEM packages. Approach. Prescribed electrode potentials give Robin problems whose unit-potential responses define an electrode transadmittance matrix. We prove equivalence to the conventional augmented CEM and formalise the discrete energy, equivalence and reference arguments in Lean 4. Numerical comparisons use two-dimensional P1 finite elements in DOLFINx, FreeFEM, GetFEM, MFEM and NGSolve, with the same nodal reference potential as EIDORS. Main results. For identical assembled blocks, the two procedures have the same solution set whenever the Robin matrix is invertible. Across the five packages in dimensionless test cases, maximum absolute electrode-potential differences from EIDORS were below $5\times10^{-13}$ (2692 nodes), and errors against an exact rational-arithmetic reference were below $1.1\times10^{-13}$ (2535 nodes). Significance. Standard Robin solves and consistent electrode-current integration provide a portable route to the current-driven CEM, with current constraints enforced through a small electrode system.

Modeling Transport and Mechanics in Anion Exchange Membranes to Optimize Continuous CO2 Separations

Samaneh Bayati, Jennifer Wade, Sara Sarbaz, Heidi Feigenbaum, Horacio Lopez-Marques, Winston Wang

Water transport in charged polymer membranes is strongly influenced by hydration-dependent transport properties and polymer-network deformation. Under water-activity gradients, spatial variations in water concentration induce swelling and mechanical stresses, while the resulting deformation can influence water transport. Capturing this bidirectional interaction is important for predicting membrane behavior under nonuniform hydration conditions. This study develops a transient finite element model of water transport and mechanical deformation in a Fumasep FAA-3-50 anion exchange membrane. The framework incorporates concentration-dependent water diffusivity, hygroscopic swelling, and nonlinear viscoelastic polymer response. Uncoupled, one-way coupled, and fully coupled formulations are compared to distinguish the effects of hydration-dependent diffusivity and mechanical coupling. In the fully coupled model, local water concentration governs swelling and mechanical response, while deformation, evolving membrane geometry, and mechanical pressure feed back into water transport. Simulations predict spatially and temporally nonuniform swelling and deformation that track the evolving water-concentration field. Relative to the uncoupled concentration-dependent diffusion model, mechanical feedback alters the predicted transport response, although transport remains primarily diffusion-driven. The fully coupled model is validated against steady-state water-transport measurements across water-activity gradients and reproduces the experimentally observed hydration dependence, with strongest agreement at intermediate hydration levels. These results highlight the importance of coupled hydration, swelling, deformation, and transport processes in models of hydrated anion exchange membranes and provide a framework for evaluating membrane behavior in moisture-facilitated transport applications.

Sparse Autoencoders Can Induce Local Geometry for Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting

Kamil Faber, Katarzyna Filus, Łukasz Pawela

Catastrophic forgetting remains a challenge in supervised continual learning, as adapting to new tasks causes representation drift and loss of previously learned information. This raises the question of which representational changes should be constrained while preserving plasticity. We therefore introduce Sparse Autoencoder Geometric Anchoring (SAGA), which converts a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) trained at each task boundary into local, low-rank constraints on representation drift. Replay examples specify where the constraints apply, while leading right singular directions of the encoder Jacobian specify which changes are penalized. The resulting geometry is input dependent and can be retained without keeping the SAE for subsequent tasks' training. We establish its local relation to latent code matching, define its spectral truncation error, and derive a conditional bound on frozen-readout drift. With replay and snapshot history, the method achieves the highest mean Class-IL accuracy and lowest mean forgetting among the evaluated ER, DER++, ER-ACE, and SGD baselines on three pretrained ViT-B/16 benchmarks, with accuracy gains of 12.15, 4.79, and 3.22 percentage points over the strongest conventional baseline on CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and ImageNet-R, respectively. From scratch results are more mixed, while rank ablations and geometry controls show that SAE-induced low-rank geometry offers useful stability-plasticity trade-offs.

Current industrial practice has largely relied on empirical fatigue models to predict damage progression and guide maintenance. However, an important limitation of existing models is the assumption on ideal impact conditions, including perfectly spherical droplet and normal incidence, which rarely occur in reality. This study develops a fluid-structure interaction (FSI) model that couples incompressible multiphase flow simulations with finite element (FE) analysis of blade materials. Using this framework, we investigate the impact dynamics of droplets with arbitrary shapes and orientations. This law provides a predictive framework for impact loading dynamics of non-ideal shapes, offering new insights into the mechanisms of LEE and improving the accuracy of erosion predictions for wind turbine blades.

Not All Change Is Damage: Update-Matched Unlearning for Large Language Models

Jie Li, Youming Tao, Bowei He, Meng Ding

Unlearning aims to remove selected knowledge from large language models to mitigate privacy and safety risks while preserving useful capabilities. For models that continue to be fine-tuned after release, this goal is insufficient: they must also resist relearning the deleted knowledge while preserving plasticity, the ability to learn new tasks. Existing defenses train against simulated relearning while trying to preserve useful capabilities, but low relearning alone cannot distinguish selective resistance from a general loss of plasticity. Evaluating preservation after an update requires deciding what the updated model should remain close to. A pre-update reference penalizes useful changes induced by the update simply because they depart from earlier outputs. We therefore formulate the reference-timing hypothesis that, at comparable target suppression, an update-matched reference better preserves plasticity than a pre-update one. To test and address this hypothesis, we propose Update-Matched Unlearning (UMU), which applies the same simulated relearning update to the defended model and to an undefended reference initialized from the same unlearned model, and uses new-task learning to guide model selection. Extensive experiments on TOFU and FaithUn with Llama and Qwen models show that UMU improves both relearning resistance and plasticity across all three base unlearning methods, lowering harmful and benign relearning by 30.4 and 9.9 points on average while raising new-task accuracy by 3.5 points. When the reference does not receive the update, relearning remains similarly low but new-task accuracy falls below the base method, supporting the reference-timing hypothesis and showing that robust unlearning should prevent recovery selectively without suppressing learning itself.

Orientation-Resolved Nanoindentation of Single Crystal Ti-15Mo: Inverted Elastic Anisotropy and Yield-Onset Behavior Beyond Simple CRSS Predictions

Kidus Abebe, David Gaetano, Aurelia Moriyama-Gurish, Jana Šmilauerová, Yanfei Gao, Udo D. Schwarz

Ti-15Mo (wt%) is a well-established $β$-stabilized alloy whose mechanical response is strongly governed by phase stability and deformation mechanisms that operate at the single-grain scale. Because these intragranular mechanisms are intrinsically anisotropic, understanding the orientation dependence of the elastic and plastic response is critical to the texture-engineered processing of wrought components. Here, we quantify the nanomechanical anisotropy of single-crystal Ti-15Mo by combining EBSD with nanoindentation to correlate hardness, indentation modulus, strain rate sensitivity, and indentation yield response with the crystallographic surface normal near $\langle111\rangle,\langle124\rangle,\langle011\rangle,\langle023\rangle$, and $\langle013\rangle$ directions. While the modulus anisotropy is small (~10%), contrary to the prediction for a cubic crystal with A>1, orientations near $\langle111\rangle$ exhibit the lowest moduli. Yield onset is likewise softest near $\langle111\rangle$ and spans 41%, while hardness spans only 6.6% and follows neither the modulus nor the yield ordering. The alloy also exhibits yield behavior not captured by the simple CRSS/Schmid-factor models evaluated here. Strain-rate-jump tests yield V*=18-31b$^3$, consistent with kink-pair-nucleation-controlled plasticity; based on V*, the orientations separate into two groups suggestive of glide-dominated versus twin/transformation-participating response. We reveal an inversion of the elastic anisotropy expected for a homogeneous cubic $β$-phase and an orientation-dependent yield-onset response that is not reproduced by the simple Schmid-factor models examined here. Retained athermal $ω$ is proposed as the contributor because $β\rightarrowω$ collapse occurs on {111}$_β$ planes and may provide anelastic compliance through the reversible $ω\rightarrowβ$ transformation.

Grain boundary strength and corrosion cracking susceptibility in TiN coatings under fission product segregation

Jia-Hong Ke, Rongjie Song

During fuel-cladding chemical interaction (FCCI), lanthanide fission products generated in the fuel can migrate into cladding grain boundaries (GBs), where segregation may reduce GB strength and promote corrosion cracking. We use first-principles segregation, decohesion, and bonding calculations to quantify Nd and Ce effects on six coincident-site lattice GBs in bcc Fe and TiN. For each material, segregation energies, work-of-separation values for complete interfacial cleavage, and traction-separation curves measuring ideal resistance to crack opening were evaluated within a consistent computational framework. Nd and Ce segregate favorably to both materials, and the median segregation energies are more negative in TiN, showing that TiN does not simply exclude these species from internal interfaces. The mechanical responses of Fe and TiN differ sharply. In Fe, Nd and Ce consistently reduce work of separation and peak traction, with median work-of-separation retentions of 0.72 and 0.65 and median peak-traction retentions of 0.47 and 0.43. In TiN, most decorated GBs preserve much of the clean response, with median work-of-separation retentions of 1.00 and 0.97 and median peak-traction retention of 0.85 for both fission products. Bonding analysis shows weak, occupied antibonding X-Fe replacement bonds in Fe, whereas the surrounding Ti-N network remains largely preserved after Nd/Ce segregation in TiN. These results provide atomistic support for the use of TiN and related nitride coatings to reduce FCCI damage by retaining GB strength if fission products segregate to coating internal interfaces. This retention of GB strength also provides a mechanistic descriptor for assessing coating susceptibility to FCCI-assisted corrosion cracking and environmentally assisted intergranular fracture.

Adaptive Crack-Tip Localization of Phase-Field Fracture Computations

Aarosh Dahal, Aditya Kumar

Phase-field models of fracture provide a unified framework for simulating crack nucleation and propagation, but their computational cost remains high because the governing equilibrium and phase-field equations are typically iteratively solved over the entire domain at every staggered iteration. We present an extension of the recently developed adaptive mesh refinement and coarsening framework that localizes not only spatial resolution but also repeated solution effort to a small active patch around the crack tip for linear elastic brittle materials. The inactive exterior is eliminated through a Schur complement and condensed onto the patch interface, and its factorization is reused as long as the patch remains fixed. The phase field is frozen in the exterior, introducing only a small approximation in the crack wake. The method is implemented in FEniCSx and evaluated for quasi-static crack propagation, dynamic crack branching, three-dimensional mixed-mode crack growth, and coalescence of multiple cracks in a unit cell. Across these problems, the condensed formulation reproduces the reference solutions with differences in global quantities below 0.5%, while substantially reducing the number of active degrees of freedom. In the quasi-static surfing problem, wall time speedups increase from 6.9 to 22.8 as the domain size is doubled, demonstrating increasing computational advantage for large-scale phase-field fracture simulations.
