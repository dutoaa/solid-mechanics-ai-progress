Synthetic Dataset Generation, Physical Consistency and Active Learning for Shape-Informed Surrogate Modeling of Hemodynamics

Francesco Romor, Richard Schussnig, Charles A. Taylor, Gerhard A. Holzapfel, Alfonso Caiazzo

Model order reduction has the potential to enable real-time patient-specific assessment of cardiovascular flows. However, it still faces significant challenges stemming from the geometric variability of vascular anatomies. Building upon the SynthAorta dataset (Bosnjak et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imag. 45(1), 2026) of physiological aortic geometries, we generate an extensive dataset of physiologically consistent computational fluid dynamics solutions through an automatic boundary-condition adjustment procedure. A common reference geometry allows all patient-specific anatomies to be represented by displacement fields, providing point-to-point correspondence without the need for geometric registration. Based on this dataset, we develop Graph U-Net and Transformer U-Net surrogate models to predict velocity and pressure fields at peak systole. We further investigate two strategies to enhance data efficiency and prediction accuracy: active learning and differentiable physical consistency constraints based on the finite element residual. The results show that active learning is strongly regime-dependent: while it offers little benefit over random sampling during early training, it improves performance during fine-tuning through gradient- and subspace-based acquisition strategies. Similarly, physical consistency constraints prove to be substantially more effective as a fine-tuning objective than as a regularizer applied throughout training. Finally, out-of-distribution studies demonstrate that the primary limitation of surrogate models lies in the geometric support of the underlying parametrization rather than in the neural network architectures themselves. The proposed framework combines synthetic dataset generation, geometry-aware surrogate modeling, physics-informed optimization, and active learning into a scalable workflow for inter-patient hemodynamic prediction.

Elastic theory of ideal liquid crystal elastomers

Zumrat Usmanova, Ruobing Bai

A liquid crystal elastomer (LCE) exhibits a two-way coupling between its mesogen order and network elasticity: the mesogen order sets the undeformed network conformation, and the network deformation reorients the mesogens. This coupling gives rise to large, reversible stimuli-responsive actuation, as well as to soft elasticity, a finite deformation at nearly zero stress. This article reviews the elastic theory of ideal LCEs, bridging single-chain statistics and continuum constitutive models. The "ideal" elastic behavior rests on the assumption of anisotropic Gaussian chains that retain no memory of the crosslinking state, such that rotating the director costs no free energy. The corresponding neo-classical free energy of a monodomain LCE is non-quasi-convex, with a continuous family of stress-free ground states, leading to thermally induced spontaneous deformation and soft elasticity accommodated by coexisting stripe domains. The quasi-convex relaxation of the free energy classifies the behavior of polydomain LCEs into three phases, each with a distinct macroscopic mechanical response depending on the stretch state. The ideal elastic theory can be further extended to incorporate additional behaviors and physical processes, including thermo- and photomechanical coupling, Frank elasticity from director distortion, semi-soft or non-soft behavior arising from network nonideality, and temperature- and rate-dependent viscoelasticity.

Can Language Models Get "Aphasia" From Neuronal Ablation?

Pratik Worah, Randall Bateman

Progressive neuronal loss is the hallmark of neurodegenerative disease, yet how loss of neurons shapes the pattern of cognitive decline remains poorly understood. Here we systematically ablate neurons (computational units) in language models spanning recurrent long short-term memory (LSTM) and Transformer architectures and apply limited fine-tuning as a simple proxy for compensatory plasticity. With whole-unit ablation, LSTMs degrade gradually with syntactic and semantic faculties declining in parallel across mild-to-moderate damage. By contrast, Transformers exhibit late threshold-like collapse under feedforward multi-layer perceptron (MLP) ablation and smoother sigmoid-like decline under attention-head ablation. Qualitatively, MLP-ablated models continue to make within-category semantic substitutions (semantic paraphasias) alongside rising non-responses, paralleling Wernicke-type aphasia, while attention-head ablation produces a trend toward loss of semantic specificity similar to the semantic variant of primary progressive aphasia (svPPA). These findings indicate that feedforward and attentional pathways have functionally distinct failure modes, suggesting that architecture-dependent failure profiles may offer new computational perspectives on aphasic syndromes and neurodegenerative diseases as an in silico model. Notably, early accuracy retention under Transformer attention-head ablation qualitatively parallels pre-symptomatic reserve before the 40--60% neuronal loss reported by Gomez-Isla et al. (1996) when clinical symptoms emerge in Alzheimer's disease, whereas the steadier LSTM decline parallels the symptomatic progression described by DeKosky and Scheff (1990) and Terry et al. (1991).

Concurrent two-scale topology optimization of printable bone scaffolds via rectified-flow generation and exact latent-space refinement

Reza T Batley, Sourav Saha

Bone scaffolds must reconcile a macroscale mechanical response with microscale geometric feasibility, yet conventional approaches to their design restrict attainable constitutive properties. This work presents a concurrent two-scale topology optimization framework; a conditional rectified flow generates periodic lattice microstructures, which are then refined in latent space by exact finite-element homogenization sensitivities. The resulting unit-cell geometries and their spatial assignments are jointly optimized under mechanical and feature-size constraints. In a distal rabbit femur benchmark, all eight flow-started refinements produce printable cells with maximum relative errors under 1\% across six prescribed engineering moduli. Constructing the implant from a single tiled cell reproduces intact whole-femur compliance within 0.1\% and regional strain energy within 0.35\% under four loading conditions. In human femoral reconstruction, multiscale concurrent optimization produces a cortical mid-shaft scaffold with porosity 63\% while satisfying the compliance constraint within 2\% under walking and stair-climbing loads. It achieves an 84\% porosity on a proximal cancellous scaffold with strain-energy deviations below 0.25\% under the same loads. Analysis of the learned representations reveals distinct architectural families in latent space. Further, the attention heads of the rectified-flow transformer specialize for spatial shifts -- learning the analogue of a finite-difference stencil -- and coordinate symmetries, with ablations showing their influence on generated topologies. These results establish a route to printable, mechanically constrained scaffold designs. The framework is general enough for extending to many other multiscale structural design applications including topology-microstructure co-design in metal additive manufacturing.

Inverse domain uncertainty quantification in hemodynamics: sampling virtual patient cohorts under constraints

Francesco Romor, Jose Rodrigo Rojo-Garcia, André-Alexander Zepernick, Ana Djurdjevac, Alfonso Caiazzo

We consider the following inverse domain uncertainty quantification problem: given a prescribed value or measurement of a hemodynamic biomarker, characterize the distribution of vascular geometries compatible with it. The problem is formulated as a Bayesian inverse problem in which a high-dimensional latent parameter encodes the cardiovascular shape and accounts for inter-patient variability. The parameter-to-observable map composes a conditional LDDMM stochastic interpolant generating the aortic surface with a high-performance numerical finite element solver for the unsteady incompressible Navier--Stokes equations, from which biomarkers of interest are evaluated. The map is nonlinear, high-dimensional and computationally expensive. To address the problem, we compare three ensemble strategies: ensemble adjusted Kalman inversion (EAKI), adaptive Bayesian updating with structural reliability (aBUS), and a Wasserstein--Fisher--Rao (WFR) sequential Monte Carlo scheme. The first two approaches are derivative-free. For the third method, we introduce a novel differentiable flow-matching surrogate that reduces the cost of a single forward map evaluation at the price of a distribution shift depending on the training distribution used for the surrogate. We apply the framework to the generation of virtual patient cohorts matching prescribed pressure drops along aortic geometries affected by coarctation, and we validate and compare the three methods on synthetic data.

CAST: Cast-Admissible Generative Design and Shape Optimization with Paired Depth Images

Jihoon Kim, Yongmin Kwon, Namwoo Kang

Deep generative design can produce complex geometries, but unconstrained representations may create undercuts that prevent release from a two-part mold. We present CAST, which reformulates three-dimensional shape generation under a selected mold-opening constraint as a two-dimensional image generation problem using paired top and bottom depth images. Each valid depth pair defines one material interval along a selected mold-opening direction, so reconstructions exclude undercuts in that direction by construction. Diffusion models generate candidate shapes in this representation, and a finite element analysis (FEA) guided optimizer refines the same two boundaries. We evaluate diffusion-based generation with limited training data comprising 211 SimJEB bracket designs. On SimJEB brackets, FEA-guided refinement with a volume penalty reduced compliance in 193 of 200 generated candidates. The median reduction across all candidates was 36.8 percent in the optimization FEA model. Reconstruction of existing brackets revealed a mass--stiffness trade-off: filling occluded regions increased material volume and reduced displacement under the evaluated loads. Paired depth images thus connect generative exploration and structural refinement in a shared design space that preserves the selected pull-direction restriction.

From Generic to Dedicated: A Novel Optimizer for Online Continual Learning

Yongyi Wu, Zheng Wang, Sen Lin

Online Continual Learning (OCL) requires models to learn from non-stationary data streams under a strict single-pass constraint, making them highly susceptible to catastrophic forgetting. While existing studies have explored various strategies from data, architecture, and optimization perspectives, the optimizer often directly uses generic approaches such as SGD and AdamW. In this work, we reveal a new link between optimization dynamics and OCL needs by recasting Polyak-Ruppert averaging as the engine of "plasticity" and Primal averaging as the anchor of "stability". This inspires our novel optimizer, SPIN (Stability-Plasticity INterpolation), which explicitly decouples plasticity and stability by interpolating between a fast-moving "plasticity" sequence and an adaptive "stability" sequence. We theoretically demonstrate that SPIN implicitly employs an inverse Hessian approximation, providing crucial Tikhonov regularization to damp noisy, single-sample Hessian estimates and mitigate forgetting. More importantly, SPIN can be seamlessly integrated into existing OCL methods by taking resulted gradients as input and replacing standard optimizers. Extensive experiments show that using SPIN instead of standard optimizers yields consistent and significant performance gains across multiple OCL approaches, including the challenging rehearsal-free setting and under strict memory constraints.

Trajectory Replay: Continuous-Time Mixture Transport for Lifelong Generative Models

Alessandro Palma, Charles Jones, Tristan Cinquin, Fabian J. Theis, Frederik Wenkel

Adapting pre-trained continuous-time generative models to sequentially incoming data without forgetting earlier tasks is a central challenge in lifelong learning. A leading strategy, generative replay, uses a generative model trained on historical tasks to augment its own continual update with synthetic samples from experience. However, it requires full trajectory simulations at every training step, which accumulate discretization errors and can degrade the replayed historical distribution. Here, we propose trajectory replay, which bypasses sample generation by training an updated velocity field to match both a frozen historical velocity along its trajectories and the incoming task's velocity field, weighted by a stability-plasticity coefficient. Through the lens of continuous-time transport, we show the resulting optimum averages the two task velocities by their respective densities and generates the mixture of their probability paths. Combined with mean velocity distillation, trajectory replay accesses historical trajectories using only a few network evaluations per continual update. Empirically, it outperforms generative replay and distillation baselines on class-incremental image generation. In two biological case studies, trajectory replay extends generative perturbation models to new contexts and experimental batches across two data modalities while preserving treatment-effect prediction.

Learning without Overwriting: Structural Knowledge Fusion for Continual Learning

Kanishk Bakshi, Amitansu Pattanaik, Kathiravan Srinivasan

Catastrophic forgetting in sequential deep learning arises from a fixed-capacity architectural constraint: parameter updates encoding new tasks perturb weight subspaces supporting prior representations. We introduce the Knowledge Fusion Network (KFN), which mitigates the stability-plasticity trade-off in class-incremental learning through novelty-gated structural expansion rather than parameter negotiation within a shared capacity budget. KFN decouples task acquisition from knowledge consolidation: each incoming task is learned in an isolated lightweight specialist network (2.8M parameters) and integrated into the global model via a Structural Fusion Module that allocates dedicated channel capacity conditioned on a quantitative novelty score. Dual structural isolation--channel-space separation with temporally phased gradient unfreezing--protects consolidated knowledge. On ten-task Split CIFAR-100, KFN achieves 11.94% minimum task accuracy and 71.16% task-10 accuracy, whereas shared-parameter baselines degrade severely: LwF to 9.07% average accuracy (0.00% min), Replay++ to 8.91% (85.08% forgetting), and Replay to 16.21% (73.44% forgetting); KFN's forgetting (57.92%) is 15.5 pp lower than Replay. Furthermore, KFN prevents logit-scale drift, maintaining near-symmetric task performance. Across CIFAR-100 and EuroSAT satellite imagery, KFN's class-incremental retention (72.7% and 42.5%) substantially exceeds the best baselines (29.6% and 0.94%). Crucially, KFN achieves parameter-efficient linear scaling, adding 25% of the base parameter count per step and requiring 25.1% of naive backbone duplication over ten tasks (28.1M vs 112.0M). With a 37.1% peak GPU memory reduction over full retraining, KFN establishes novelty-gated expansion as a practical path beyond fixed-capacity architectures.

Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners?

Xiaoyin Chen, Yizheng Huang, Yinglun Xu, Ziyun Zhao, Emmett McQuinn, Julie Liu

Large language model agents are often deployed in environments where new knowledge, tools, and workflows keep arriving. Such an agent is therefore expected to adapt continually, learning new tasks as they arrive without forgetting earlier ones. This resembles the continual learning problem, but at a pace that conventional gradient updates cannot sustain. Nonparametric self-evolving agents are a natural alternative: they adapt by rewriting their own harness, such as prompts, memory, and skills, from task feedback while the model weights stay frozen. We study whether such agents are effective continual learners across four drifting task streams that shift in topic, workflow, or domain (built from BIRD, BFCL, and ScienceWorld). Evaluating three representative methods spanning experience retrieval and self-modification (ReasoningBank, GEPA, and Meta-Harness), we find that although self-evolution improves aggregate performance on most streams, higher plasticity consistently comes at the cost of stability. Specifically, self-modifying methods apply global edits to a shared prompt or workspace, interfering with unseen tasks and overwriting earlier knowledge as the stream shifts. Conversely, similarity-based memory retrieval localizes updates across distant domains, but still retrieves foreign memories on unseen tasks and suffers from retrieval collision when similar queries require conflicting workflows. These findings show that current self-evolving agents remain incomplete continual learners.

A Novel Two-Level Domain Decomposition Method with Physically Motivated Coarse Space for Surface Acoustic Wave Device Simulations

Likun Qiu, Jianguo Huang, Xuejun Xu

The surface acoustic wave (SAW) equations are fundamental in describing the operating principle of SAW devices, which are characterized by high operating frequency, multiphysics coupling, and defined on an unbounded domain. Finite element (FE) simulation is one of the most accurate numerical methods for such devices. However, the method usually leads to ill-conditioned linear systems with a large number of degrees of freedom (DOFs), limiting its application. To address this issue, based on the finite element tearing and interconnecting (FETI) algorithm, this paper proposes a domain decomposition method with iteration numbers stable with respect to number of subdomains, frequency and mesh size, named FETI-SAW. The main novelty is that the Dirichlet preconditioner combined with a Rayleigh-wave-based coarse space is constructed to produce a preconditioned iterative algorithm for the Lagrange multiplier linear system. In some sense, this method reflects the physical essence of the SAW equations. In numerical experiments, we take interdigital transducers (IDT) and SAW resonators as examples to demonstrate the efficiency of the FETI-SAW algorithm. Additionally, the MATLAB and C++ source codes accompanying this paper are provided.

Abaqus implementation of the phase-field approach to fracture

Jiangwei Zhou, Victor Lefevre, Oscar Lopez-Pamies

In this paper, we introduce an Abaqus user-subroutine implementation of the phase-field model of fracture initiated by Kumar, Francfort, and Lopez-Pamies (\emph{J. Mech. Phys. Solids} 112, 523--551, 2018). The implementation is presented for the basic case of isotropic linear elastic brittle materials with a Drucker-Prager strength surface. The material inputs are therefore the Young's modulus $E$, the Poisson's ratio $ν$, the uniaxial tensile strength $\sts$, the uniaxial compressive strength $\scs$, and the fracture toughness $G_c$. The mathematical correspondence between the heat equation and the evolution equation for the phase field $v(\bfX,t)$ is exploited through a UMAT subroutine, wherein the driving force $c_{\texttt{e}}(\bfX,t)$ --- accounting for the material strength surface --- in the phase-field model plays the role of a heat source. To ensure that the equations for the displacement field $\bfu(\bfX,t)$ and the phase field $v(\bfX,t)$ are solved in a staggered manner --- a solution strategy that, as opposed to monolithic approaches, reliably yields the correct physical solution --- we make use of a combination of UEXTERNALDB, UAMP, MPC, and URDFIL subroutines. Within this approach, the MPC and URDFIL subroutines are additionally utilized to enforce the required bounding and irreversibility inequalities on $v(\bfX,t)$ via a primal-dual active set approach. The capabilities and robustness of the proposed implementation are demonstrated by simulating the ``Nine Circles of Elastic Brittle Fracture'' on a silicate glass --- a set of tests that span the entire range of well-settled experimental knowledge on fracture nucleation and propagation. These simulations also serve to illustrate the deployment of the code using several native Abaqus finite elements, including the simplicial elements CPS3, CAX3, and C3D4.

In this context, the Wave Finite Element Method (WFEM) combines Periodic Structure Theory (PST) with standard FE packages, enabling wave dispersion analysis of waveguides involving structurally realistic unit-cells. An application is provided for a bi-directionally stiffened panel and the influence of reduction parameters is discussed, as well as the robustness of the numerical results.

Vibro-acoustic optimisation of sandwich panels using the wave/finite element method

This paper investigates the use of a wave-based method in the framework of structural optimisation of composite panels involving advanced components. The wave/finite element method (WFEM) is used to evaluate the influence of a core's geometry on the transition frequency of a sandwich panel involving composite skins. This transition occurs is a sandwich panel when the transverse shear stiffness has a significant influence on the flexural motion, compared to the bending stiffness. It follows that the modal density and the acoustic radiation will considerably increase above this frequency.
