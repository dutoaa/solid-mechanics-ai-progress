Differentiable Schwarz-Coupled Solvers for Thin Boundary Layer Transport: Inverse Modeling via Agentic AI Optimization

Siva Viknesh, Amirhossein Arzani

High-Péclet-number transport problems give rise to thin boundary layers with steep near-wall gradients whose accurate solution requires high-resolution meshes and could challenge inverse modeling. To address this challenge, we develop an overlapping Schwarz domain-decomposition framework grounded in perturbation theory, in which an inner equation governs near-wall dynamics and an outer equation governs bulk transport, and both subproblems are discretized using the finite element method and assembled into a fully differentiable multidomain physics solver. The formulation permits coarser mesh resolutions, reducing computational cost relative to a single-domain formulation. Exploiting the differentiability of the coupled solver, we formulate inverse problems in which the governing equations and boundary conditions are imposed as hard constraints, with the Schwarz coupling enforcing interface continuity. To accelerate the resulting adjoint-based optimization, we integrate large language models (LLMs) into the inverse workflow in two distinct configurations: a standalone optimizer that proposes successive parameter updates from the adjoint-computed sensitivities and an agentic artificial intelligence (AI) approach with access to the complete solver workflow, enabling it to perform multistage optimization procedures. The agentic AI configuration is shown to be most advantageous when the spatially varying unknown is represented in a functional basis rather than a high-dimensional nodal field representation. In this case, the AI agent grows the library of candidate basis functions during optimization under a prescribed admission policy, admitting predefined families in stages and initializing their parameters. In short, our proposed framework integrates Schwarz-coupled differentiable physics with agentic AI optimization for the inverse modeling of multiscale transport problems.

Finite Element Modeling of Fracture in Brittle Foods with Physically Consistent Parameter Calibration: A Practical Case Study on Cooled Dark Chocolate

Patrick Kurzeja, Felix Rörentrop, Kai Langenfeld, Jörn Mosler

Fracture in food can be undesired during production and transportation but also intended during cutting, grinding or mastication. The associated peak load, the deformation at failure and the resulting fragment geometry are all product properties. This work condenses the modeling of such fracture into a physically rigorous recipe that considers the practical constraints imposed by finite element simulations where necessary and applies idealized theoretical assessments where applicable. Cooled dark chocolate serves as a reproducible model material. Two well-established modeling frameworks for Griffith-type brittle fracture are assessed at an affordable spatial discretization: a sharp-interface and a diffuse phase-field approach. The assessment covers the choice of numerical framework, its discretization and a staged identification of the model parameters. Each parameter is identified from the loading stage that governs it: elastic parameters from pre-crack tension and compression, critical energy release rate from three-point-bending peak force, and the phase-field width last. The phase-field width interferes with the structural response, crack curvature and stress fields, depending on its lower bound set by the affordable spatial resolution. Elastic slope, peak force and crack path therefore cannot be met practically by a single parameter set. The objective must be fixed for the calibration of the model parameters and reported together with the numerical parameters and the boundary value problem.

Retention-constrained fine-tuning of a pre-trained aluminium MACE potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity

Yue Wu, Yuan Tian, Deng Pan, Mingwei Chen

Adapting a pre-trained machine-learning interatomic potential for non-equilibrium defect dynamics and plasticity requires improving defect accuracy without degrading the near-equilibrium elastic and vibrational response. Here we achieve this balance for the aluminium MACE potential QP17 by integrating conditional readout uncertainty, iterative full-parameter updates and protocol-compatible relative-energy anchoring. Sensitivities over 144 linear readout weights guide acquisition, with within-batch covariance updates and evolving neural representations. After four active-learning rounds (32 acquired configurations and 400 gradient updates), force-uncertainty acquisition reduces mean development force error from 22.43 to 21.52 meV/Angstrom, outperforming random sampling (22.70), geometry-diversity selection (22.46) and uniform training across all 104 candidate labels (22.71) under the same update budget. All three force-uncertainty runs satisfy bulk-mechanical retention criteria: historical relative-energy error decreases from 0.375 to 0.207 meV/atom, while harmonic phonon root-mean-square drift remains below 0.05 THz. Matched objective ablations reveal that relative-energy anchoring suppresses harmonic drift. Out-of-sample evaluations across planar faults, twin boundaries and grain boundaries demonstrate transferable gains in defect energies and deviatoric stresses, even though virial stresses are never fitted. A closed-loop molecular dynamics and density functional theory (MD-DFT) pilot confirms that automated acceptance gating intercepts updates that violate historical elastic-group energy or vibrational tolerances. Together, these components establish a retention-constrained fine-tuning protocol for improving plastic-deformation observables while preserving the tested bulk mechanical response.

$H^2$-based equilibrated residual error estimates

Kexin Ding, Yuwen Li

We develop a framework for equilibrated residual a posteriori error analysis of finite element methods, based on decomposing vector- and tensor-valued Sobolev spaces into $H^2$ and $H^1$ spaces. To estimate the residual functional acting on $H^2$ component, the method reconstructs symmetric and piecewise polynomial tensors on vertex patches in broken $H(\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div})$ spaces. We apply the proposed framework to conforming finite element discretizations of biharmonic equation, Poisson's equation, and linear elasticity in mixed form. As a result, we obtain several novel equilibrated residual a posteriori error estimates, providing guaranteed error control for energy error of biharmonic equation, $L^2$ norm error of Poisson's equation, and stress error of mixed linear elasticity. They also serve as the first polynomial-degree-robust a posteriori lower bounds of discretization errors. Numerical experiments illustrate the effectiveness of the $H^2$-based error estimators.

Scaling of Interface Variability in Aerospace Composite Structures: A Multi-scale Stochastic Approach from Local Identification to Panel Response

Elton Donfack-Siewe, Sylvain Dubreuil, Christian Fagiano, Jérôme Morio, Jean-Philippe Navarro

Quantifying random spatial variations in composite interface properties is an essential prerequisite for the high-fidelity propagation of material properties variabilities through structural numerical models. This study proposes a multi-scale stochastic framework designed to bridge the gap between local interfacial characterization at the coupon scale and the global response of industrial aerospace panels, focusing specifically on the skin-stringer bond line. Building upon previous stochastic modeling and identification methodologies for Mode I fracture, we first extend the likelihood-free Bayesian calibration to Mode II configurations to characterize interfacial stochastic parameters using experimental coupon data. To address the scale-up challenge, we employ a simulation-based Stochastic Finite Element Method (SFEM) to contrast two distinct probabilistic strategies -- spatially uniform variations (Paradigm 2) and spatially correlated random fields (Paradigm 3) -- against the traditional industrial B-basis standard (Paradigm 1). The numerical results reveal a dual mechanism in uncertainty propagation depending on the targeted airframe design criterion. For global performance, such as the ultimate load capacity, a significant scale effect leads to an uncertainty attenuation phenomenon; statistical averaging smooths out local heterogeneities, demonstrating that a computationally efficient spatially uniform approach is sufficient. Conversely, for local integrity, such as the threshold and spatial localization of early damage initiation, the inclusion of spatially correlated random fields becomes indispensable to capture premature degradation driven by local material weak spots. This balanced probabilistic treatment offers a route toward test derisking and predictive uncertainty management in the design of damage-tolerant aerospace composite airframes.

Higher-Order QMC Galerkin FEM for Singular Perturbation: Reaction-Diffusion with Affine-Parametric Reaction

Fabian Rohner, Christoph Schwab

We analyze higher-order quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) Galerkin discretizations for a singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion problem with an affine-parametric reaction coefficient. We derive error estimates that are robust with respect to the perturbation parameter $\varepsilon$. To this end, we establish $\varepsilon$-uniform stability and mixed-parametric regularity estimates in an $\varepsilon$-weighted energy norm, with constants independent of the parameter dimension and of $\varepsilon$. These estimates imply robust dimension truncation estimates and dimension-independent higher-order QMC error bounds for linear quantities of interest. Assuming an abstract, $\varepsilon$-robust finite element approximation property, we combine the parametric estimates with the spatial discretization analysis to derive a general fully discrete QMC-FE error estimate with constants independent of both the parameter dimension and $\varepsilon$. We verify this approximation property for a one-dimensional model problem using continuous piecewise affine finite elements on Shishkin meshes. Numerical experiments illustrate the predicted robustness and convergence rates.

Large deformations, near-incompressibility, and evolving contact make these simulations computationally demanding and can challenge nonlinear convergence. Differentiable, GPU-accelerated methods offer efficient derivative evaluation and parallel computation, but their matrix-free application to soft-tissue shells with contact remains less established. We developed a forward solver using tatva's energy-based finite-element framework in JAX. The solver combines finite-strain extensible-director shells, hyperelastic constitutive models, and a finite-range repulsive contact energy. Nonlinear equilibrium is solved using a regularized, matrix-free Newton-Krylov method with two-level preconditioning. Automatic differentiation evaluates tangent-vector products without forming or storing the primary tangent matrix, while GPU execution parallelizes element and quadrature-point computations. Constitutive, shell, contact, and self-contact responses were assessed against analytical, semi-analytical, and published benchmarks. At a contact-free plate equilibrium, implicit sensitivities of the converged apex displacement to constitutive parameters, thickness, and pressure agreed with finite differences from independent nonlinear simulations. Simulations of three patient-specific aortic-valve models (two tricuspid and one bicuspid) achieved final relative equilibrium residuals below 10^-4. Median GPU runtimes were approximately 15-25 minutes, with 5.3-6.5-fold speedups relative to corresponding CPU runs on the tested hardware. These results provide component-level verification and demonstrate GPU-accelerated forward valve simulation in the tested cases, supporting future gradient-based inverse studies.

Total Deformation-based MPM for Perfectly Elastic Solid Simulation with Strong Deformations

Timna Böttcher, Rick Ridder, Jan Bender

We present a new MPM-based solver for perfectly elastic solids, which can recover from strong deformations and naturally allows contact handling. Typical MPM solvers suffer from numerical fracture, where objects break apart even when no plasticity is included in the elasticity model. Previous approaches for perfectly elastic solids solve this by computing forces on a fixed grid in the reference configuration. However, this makes them incompatible with standard collision handling methods. We instead propose a method for deformable solid simulation without numerical fracture, that still computes elastic forces on the current grid. Our method can easily include grid-based collision handling, as commonly used in MPM frameworks. Additionally, we compute the deformation directly from the particle positions and a fixed reference grid, without any updates based on velocity gradients. This facilitates the inclusion of particle-level collision handling and zero-energy mode suppression, which counteracts artifacts from non-linear deformations. As a result, we can efficiently simulate complex scenarios of elastic objects undergoing large deformations, including contact.

A minimizing-movement framework for parametric finite element approximation of anisotropic curve shortening flows

Wei Jiang, Chunmei Su, Ganghui Zhang

We propose a minimizing-movement framework for constructing parametric finite element methods (PFEMs) for anisotropic curve shortening flows. The cornerstone is an admissible modified energy: a smooth, convex, and iteration-dependent surrogate that majorizes the original anisotropic energy and agrees with it at the current iterate. For any fixed finite element degree $n\geq1$ and any positive surface energy density $γ\in C^2(\mathbb{S}^1)$, the unconstrained one-step problem has a unique solution for any time-step size whenever the parametric derivative of the current discrete curve does not vanish on any mesh element. It also satisfies a one-step discrete energy dissipation inequality. It therefore preserves the variational structure of the underlying $L^2$-gradient flow. The framework recovers a variational interpretation of Dziuk's isotropic scheme and applies directly to the weak anisotropies. For strong anisotropies, a convex splitting removes the weak-anisotropy restriction on the original density. A scalar constraint further extends the framework to area-preserving flows. Numerical experiments exhibit approximately the expected convergence for smooth isotropic and weakly anisotropic examples. They also confirm energy dissipation and area preservation. In the strongly anisotropic regime, the computations produce sharp facet-like profiles but reveal mesh-scale oscillations under the grid refinement.

Convergence analysis of an augmented dual-mixed fnite element method with skew-symmetric Nitsche stabilization for the generalized Oseen equations with mixed boundary conditions

Cristian Cárcamo, Abner Haguit Poza, Ramiro James Rebolledo

In this paper, we propose and analyze a new skew-symmetric Nitsche-augmented mixed scheme for the generalized Oseen equations with non-solenoidal convective fields, subject to mixed boundary conditions. The scheme is based on a continuous pseudostress-velocity augmented dual-mixed finite element formulation, in which the Dirichlet datum is imposed naturally, whereas the prescribed normal trace of the pseudostress is incorporated into the $H(div,)$ stress space. We establish the well-posedness of the continuous formulation by means of a classical lifting argument and its corresponding homogeneous problem. The essential boundary condition is then weakly enforced in the discrete scheme through suitable stabilizing Nitsche terms. We establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the discrete scheme, allowing the use of general conforming approximation spaces for both the pseudostress and the velocity. Moreover, in the case of homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and general Neumann data, we establish the stability of the Nitsche scheme. Under mild regularity assumptions on the exact solution, we prove optimal convergence rates in an energy norm. Finally, numerical experiments in two and three dimensions confirm the theoretical convergence rates and assess the performance of the proposed scheme for non-solenoidal convective fields and solutions exhibiting boundary layers.

Constrained neural softening functions for the Mullins effect in parameter-dependent materials

Dominik K. Klein, Jonas Fey, Maximilian Kannapinn, Oliver Weeger

Accurately capturing the Mullins effect across varying material compositions remains challenging, as constitutive models must unify flexible representations of stress softening with physical consistency and robust parameter calibration. In this work, we investigate parameter-dependent physics-augmented neural network constitutive models within the pseudo-elastic modeling framework, focusing on structural constraints that ensure physically admissible neural softening functions. This paper has three key contributions: (1) We formulate different neural softening functions within the pseudo-elastic framework, including a multiplicative decomposition of the softening function that enables parameter-dependent ansatz functions satisfying common mechanical constitutive conditions by construction. (3) We systematically investigate how flexibility and structural constraints influence the performance of neural softening functions in terms of prediction accuracy and calibration robustness. For this, we consider multiaxial experimental data of carbon black-filled EPDM rubber synthesized with varying amounts of curatives. The findings of our work suggest that neural softening functions are a promising extension of the classical pseudo-elastic framework and, when subject to suitable constraints, can improve predictive accuracy for highly nonlinear, parameter-dependent materials.
