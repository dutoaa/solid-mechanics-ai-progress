Element-Wise Femoral Stress Prediction: A Hybrid Graph Neural Network Surrogate for Finite Element Analysis

Sikha O K, Abdollah Moslemi, Elham Alizadeh, Ludovic Humbert, Miguel A. Gonzalez Ballester, Jerome Noailly

Hip fractures represent a significant clinical challenge because they cause excess disability and mortality, and have a growing incidence in aging populations, as bone fragility increases. Although finite element analysis (FEA) provides biomechanical assessment of femoral strength, its clinical applicability is mainly limited by high computational cost. Conventional machine learning (ML) approaches have been explored as surrogate models for FEA; predictions are fast but fail to capture the spatial and topological structure of finite element meshes. To address these limitations, this study proposes a hybrid Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based framework for femoral stress prediction using graph-structured finite element modeling (FEM) data. The proposed GCN-SAGE-Residual architecture combines graph convolution, neighborhood aggregation, and residual learning to model spatial dependencies and stress propagation across mesh elements. We systematically evaluated 14 models in a primary comparison, spanning classical ML methods and baseline GNN architectures, complemented by a targeted architectural ablation that isolates the contribution of each component of the proposed design. Evaluation results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid architecture achieves the strongest predictive performance among all evaluated models. The proposed hybrid model achieves the best performance with an R^2 score of 0.8022 and mean absolute error of 2.879 MPa, highlighting the importance of incorporating mesh topology in biomechanical prediction tasks. The proposed framework provides a scalable and computationally efficient alternative for FEM-based orthopedic analyses and fracture risk assessment. The code is publicly available on GitHub.

Constitutive Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks in Finite Element Model Updating: Closed-Form Hyperelastic Strain Energies from Heterogeneous Experiments

Kian P. Abdolazizi, Kevin Linka, Christian J. Cyron

Calibrating a hyperelastic constitutive model for a soft material traditionally poses two problems: an expert must choose its functional form, and several homogeneous tests must determine its parameters. Neural constitutive models solve the first, but either remain black boxes or draw on a library of candidate terms fixed in advance. Full-field measurements, such as those from digital image correlation (DIC), promise to solve the second, replacing those tests by a single heterogeneous one. Two gaps remain: flexibility during training with interpretability afterwards, and a way to train such a model on one heterogeneous test. Here we close both. We embed a constitutive Kolmogorov-Arnold network (CKAN) in finite element model updating (FEMU) and train it by minimizing the mismatch between the predicted and the measured kinematic fields and reaction forces. Pruning and symbolic regression then reduce the trained network to a compact closed-form strain energy that satisfies every constitutive requirement. On perforated natural-rubber membranes, the CKANs reproduce the DIC displacement fields with median errors between one and two percent, and transfer to a hole pattern withheld from training. On a perforated silicone plate, a CKAN trained on one stretch profile at a single load reproduces that profile and the measured reaction force to within a few percent. On an aortic specimen inflated under pressure, an anisotropic CKAN predicts the inflated three-dimensional shape. The discovered closed-form energies match the CKANs they replace on the training data and can be compared directly with established models.

AI agents can redesign engineering parts with more than a thousand CAD parameters and nonlinear simulations

Alexandra Starostina, Alexander Hermann, Mahish Guru, Kartik Bali, Roland Aydin, Christian C. Cyron

Simulation-driven design of real engineering components has to meet three conditions at once. It runs on few solves, since a nonlinear finite-element simulation of a 3D part takes hours; its design space has many values, the hundreds to thousands of values of the part's CAD model; and a better design often needs a redesign, coordinated changes of the part's proportions rather than local polishing of its surface. Automated optimisers meet at most two of these conditions. Classical optimisers, Bayesian or derivative-free, need a person to cut the design down to a few parameters, and on all the values they need far more solves; industrial shape optimisers move every surface node of the mesh in one solve per cycle, but only in small steps that polish rather than redesign. We ask whether a general-purpose large language model (LLM) can meet all three by joining what it knows with what the simulation shows. GPT-6 Astra edits a magnesium stent through a CAD geometry parametrisation of about 1,650 values, with a budget of 15 simulations; every proposal is built in CAD, meshed and simulated with large-deformation plasticity and contact, and the LLM reads the full results of every solve. Within that budget it lowered the objective, a high percentile of the strains in the stent's bends, by 32%; on the same budget, the industrial shape optimiser Tosca improved the original design by a tenth and then stopped improving. An earlier GPT-6 Astra design, 25% lower in the same objective, was checked in an independent, higher-fidelity model of the full deployment with crimping and damage: it was 14% less strained than the original after the full chain, and its most damaged spot began to fail later and carried 63% less damage at full expansion. LLMs can thus join their learned knowledge with simulation data to adjust more than a thousand design values and reach valid designs with real improvements.

Emergent Fatigue Dynamics from Foundational Interatomic Potentials

Jingye Tan, Benjamin A. Jasperson, Eric Rothchild, Anders Johansson, Rèmi Dingreville, Krishna Garikipati

Foundational machine-learned interatomic potentials are trained on large, diverse DFT datasets, but their validation rests on equilibrium states, which does not guarantee correct emergent behavior in large-scale molecular dynamics. Where no direct experimental validation is available, comparisons to physically motivated potentials are valuable for drawing insight. We distinguish static properties, which are thermally accessible, from emergent properties, which require work from an external fatigue stress to drive rare events, and compare the potentials on both. An EAM potential and the NequIP foundational potential both agree closely with reference data for many near-equilibrium properties, but NequIP predicts an anomalously low intrinsic stacking fault energy ($\sim7$ times lower than EAM) and thus predicts far more plastic deformation under fatigue cycling of a nanoscale flaw modeled after the classical Griffith crack problem. Per-atom energy distributions after loading show that the two potentials differ most at energies far above what is thermally accessible. We also document memory-related crashes and silent extrapolation as practical concerns for practitioners of these foundational model forms.

Frame Based Inelasticity for Strength and Fracture in Anisotropic Media

Robert Lipton, Prakash K. C.

A field theory is presented for predicting damage and fracture in brittle materials. Four concepts are used: a new nonlocal deformation gradient for dynamic stress analysis in anisotropic media, a new type of phase field coming from a constitutive law, the evolution of material displacement from Newton's second law, and a new incorporation of scaled strength surfaces into constitutive laws for predicting dynamic and quasi-static fracture patterns. The elastic softening is governed by the strain dynamics through the constitutive law and in this way we provide the two way coupling between strain and fracture. Simulations validating the approach for both dynamic and quasi-static fracture analysis are compared to experimental results and other simulation methods. The model is capable of predicting material strength in titania and the competition between strength and energy in soda lime glass and titana and captures dynamic crack branching in soda lime glass and concrete.

Unusual increase in electrical and thermal conductivity of severely deformed CrMnFeCoNi high-entropy alloy: Roles of disordering and nanostructuring in electron and phonon transport

Jacqueline Hidalgo-Jiménez, Payam Edalati, Md Amirul Islam, Makoto Arita, Dmitry Chernyshov, Bidyut Baran Saha

This work investigates how defect engineering and nanostructuring via severe plastic deformation influence electrical and thermal transport in the high-entropy alloy CrMnFeCoNi. High-pressure torsion (HPT) for up to N = 4 turns produces a hierarchical defect structure, including deformation twins at low strains, dislocations and Lomer-Cottrell locks at intermediate strains, and a high fraction of high-angle grain boundaries at large strains. While hardness naturally increases and saturates at ~520 Hv, both electrical and thermal conductivity exhibit an unusual increase upon HPT processing (from 0.953 MS m-1 and 10.3 W m-1 K-1 after homogenization to 1.003 +- 0.005 MS m-1 and 14.4 W m-1 K-1 after N = 4). Analysis based on the Wiedemann-Franz law indicates a modest increase in electron transport (~5%) and a significant increase in phonon transport (~100%) after HPT processing. The phonon mean free path remains small on the order of 0.6-1.1 nm, and comparison with the Cahill-Pohl model shows that heat transport occurs near the minimum thermal conductivity limit, where vibrational modes are strongly scattered. This suggests that the enhancement in thermal transport is not governed by conventional defect scattering, but it is hypothesized that modifications in atomic-scale disorder, such as deformation-induced short-range ordering, are responsible for this unusual behavior. Specific heat capacity increases after HPT, consistent with stored defect energy. The linear thermal expansion exhibits a non-monotonic behavior, decreasing initially due to internal stresses and relaxation effects, and increasing at higher strains due to grain boundary-induced anharmonicity. These findings highlight the complex interplay between defects, atomic-scale disorder, and nanostructure in governing the electrical and thermal properties of severely deformed high-entropy alloys via electron and phonon scattering.

Ising-Encoded Computational Homogenization: Stress as a Quantum Observable

Mourad Halla, Suvranu De

Computational homogenization requires repeated solutions of representative volume element (RVE) problems to determine the effective response transferred to the macroscale, including the homogenized stress. Here, we develop a basis-encoded, energy-based variational quantum formulation for linear-elastic computational homogenization. The reduced finite-element energy of the RVE is expressed as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem and mapped to an Ising Hamiltonian, whose ground state represents the equilibrium fluctuation field on the encoding grid. Using the Hellmann--Feynman theorem, differentiation of the strain-dependent Hamiltonian with respect to the prescribed macroscopic strain yields stress observables whose expectation values recover the conventional volume-averaged homogenized stress. A key structural result is that, although the equilibrium Hamiltonian is two-local, the stress observables are one-local. In linear elasticity, the two-qubit coupling coefficients are independent of macroscopic strain, hence, differentiation eliminates all two-qubit coupling terms, leaving only identity and single-qubit Pauli-$Z$ operators. These observables are diagonal in the same computational basis as the Ising Hamiltonian and can therefore be evaluated from the same computational-basis measurements used for the energy. Consequently, the homogenized stress can be obtained directly from the optimized quantum state using the same computational basis measurements as the Ising Hamiltonian, without requiring reconstruction of the microscopic displacement field or an additional measurement basis. Ground states are approximated using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE), and the formulation is demonstrated on a two-dimensional periodic RVE.

A locking-free enriched linearized neural network for linear elasticity

Bonyong Kim, Young Ju Lee

Volumetric locking remains a fundamental challenge for neural-network Galerkin methods in nearly incompressible elasticity. We introduce the \emph{locking-free enriched linearized neural network} (LF-ENN), to the best of our knowledge, the first neural-network Galerkin method with a rigorous locking-free guarantee. Building on the enriched Galerkin framework, the method combines neural approximation with a structure-preserving enrichment that reduces the discrete incompressibility constraint to a graph-Laplacian problem. This construction enables divergence-free corrections and error estimates uniform in the Lamé parameter $λ\in(0,\infty]$. Nitsche boundary enforcement and tailored stabilization overcome the lack of conventional finite element trace inequalities for neural trial spaces. Under suitable approximation and mesh-resolution assumptions, we establish stability and error estimates independent of network width, mesh size, and $λ$, including an $O(h^s)$ estimate for reduced-regularity solutions with $s>1/2$. Numerical experiments in two and three dimensions confirm locking-free behavior and demonstrate substantial gains in displacement accuracy. In particular, LF-ENN achieves approximately twelve times smaller displacement error than a higher-order locking-free enriched Galerkin method while using fewer than one-third as many unknowns. These results establish a rigorous neural Galerkin framework that combines incompressibility robustness with markedly improved approximation accuracy.

Helices at Twisted Ferroelectric Interfaces

Kristina Holsgrove, Ronan Lynch, Conor McCluskey, Eoin Moynihan, Andrew Rogers, Xinxin Hu

We report that twisted interfaces, formed between two ferroelectric lithium niobate single crystals, contain arrays of coherently bonded and elastically deformed nanoscale structural helices, separated from one another by screw dislocations. The helices generate remarkable contrast in four dimensional scanning transmission electron microscopy (4D STEM) and through related differential phase contrast (DPC). Such images have been successfully rationalised by modelling the trajectory of electrons that channel along helical pathways as they traverse the interface, but the structural helices implied have also been directly imaged using electron ptychography. Importantly, as lithium niobate is a uniaxial ferroelectric, the torsional strain in these structural helices necessarily also causes dipolar helices to form, inferred through consideration of pertinent piezoelectric tensors and flexoelectric coefficients. Helices are topologically distinct from the more commonly reported vortices and, importantly, they are chiral. Helical (and hence chiral) character was confirmed by observing that DPC images were identical before and after the sample was inverted. Chirality was also established through second harmonic optical circularly-polarised dichroism measurements, in which unusually large interfacial signals were seen. It must be stressed that the arrays of helices observed are distinct from moiré patterns; they facilitate lattice rotation combining plastic and elastic deformation and are not simply a superposition of lattices, twisted with respect to one another. Thus, intimately bonded twisted oxide interfaces will have distinctly different characteristics from those of twisted stacks of two-dimensional van der Waals materials, opening up a new and distinct kind of twistronics.

Variationally correct physics-informed operator learning for nonlinear partial differential equations

Yuan Qiu, Wolfgang Dahmen, Peng Chen

Physics-informed operator learning trains neural operators on the residual of a parametric partial differential equation (PDE) instead of labeled solution data, but its reliability depends on a variationally correct residual: one that is equivalent to the error in a norm dictated by the equation, the PDE-compliant norm. For nonlinear equations written as first-order systems, we prove that the first-order system least-squares residual is equivalent to the error in this norm near a solution branch if the linearized residual is stable and the residual derivative is Lipschitz continuous, possibly in a norm stronger than the error norm. We verify these conditions for monotone reaction, solution- and gradient-dependent diffusion, and compressible Neo-Hookean hyperelasticity. The stability mechanisms vary across these classes, as do the regions where the equivalence holds, from arbitrary bounded sets to a neighborhood of the branch. In a conforming finite element space, we show that the loss excess of a state near the finite element solution is equivalent to its squared distance from that solution. We use this loss to train a reduced basis neural operator with values in this space, with solution snapshots needed only for the basis and the loss and its symbolically derived gradient assembled by the finite element library. At inference, this loss estimates the error. Numerically, the square root of the loss excess tracks the error within factors comparable to those of the linear case, and the operator achieves higher accuracy in the PDE-compliant norm than grid-based baselines evaluated in the same finite element space.

A GPU Interior-Point Solver with Macro-Element Condensation for Equilibrium-Based Plasticity

Youssef Essousy, Mohammed-Khalil Ferradi

Equilibrium-based conic formulations of elastoplasticity and limit analysis provide statically admissible stress fields but generate large Newton systems whose sparse factorization limits three-dimensional computations. This work develops and analyzes a macro-element condensation tailored to the Newton systems arising from primal--dual interior-point methods. Exploiting four-tetrahedron macro-elements, the method eliminates stress directions and macro-internal traction-continuity multipliers, yielding reduced systems that can be solved using positive-definite factorizations, with estimated reductions of approximately \(4\times\) in dimension and structural nonzeros relative to stress-only elimination. The validity of this elimination is established through global and macro-local nonsingularity conditions. Local nonsingularity is independent of the yield criterion in elastoplasticity, whereas in limit analysis it holds directly for Rankine and is ensured for von Mises through trace-bound augmentation. Numerical results show that, relative to stress-only elimination, the proposed condensation achieves CPU speedups of up to approximately \(2\times\) and reduces peak linear-solver memory by up to \(66.5\%\). We further develop a CUDA Fortran implementation that exploits uniform macro-local blocks through batched routines and uses cuDSS for the reduced sparse solve. This implementation achieves speedups of up to \(13.5\times\) over its CPU counterpart and up to \(87.5\times\) over MOSEK, while successfully solving the largest tested 3D problems that exceed the device-memory capacity of the GPU solver CuClarabel. For elastoplasticity, we also propose a preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver that reduces device-memory requirements relative to the direct GPU solver, at the expense of increased solution time.

High-Order Area-Preserving Runge--Kutta Methods for Surface Diffusion Flow of Curves

Lei Cui, Gengen Zhang

We propose a class of high-order area-preserving Runge--Kutta (RK) methods for the surface diffusion flow of curves, a curvature-driven geometric flow that decreases the perimeter while conserving the enclosed area. By coupling the parametric finite element approximation of the classical BGN formulation with symplectic RK time discretisation, we rigorously prove that the fully discrete scheme unconditionally conserves the enclosed area of curves. This conservation arises solely from the intrinsic symplectic structure of the RK methods, without introducing any additional constraints, auxiliary variables, or Lagrange multipliers to enforce structure preservation. To attain high temporal accuracy, we adopt $q$-stage Gauss RK methods, which are symplectic and yield order $2q$ (e.g., $q=1,2,3$). The resulting fully implicit, strongly nonlinear system is solved efficiently via an equivalent discrete formulation combined with a monolithic damped Newton iteration. Extensive numerical tests on various evolving curves validate the theoretical findings, confirming exact area preservation, high-order temporal convergence, and favorable mesh quality, while the Newton iteration count rapidly decreases to fewer than 8 per step, demonstrating the robustness of the tailored solver.

Crash simulations of glass-fiber-reinforced plastic parts are highly sensitive to the fiber-orientation field supplied to the material model. In conventional workflows, that field comes from resin-flow analysis, which requires molding-specialist support and takes about 25 hours of work per part. We previously proposed a surrogate model that addresses this bottleneck by predicting the orientation field in about 3 minutes. This paper reports the implementation, validation, and operational design that turned the model into an engineering tool for Honda design workflows. We present staged validation from 2D plates to production parts, evaluation against a predefined engineering angular-error criterion, and the expert review that approved design use in March 2025; the system has since been transferred to the crash-safety group and is in initial, limited use in directed fracture-prediction scenarios. The complete orientation-preparation workflow takes about 0.1 hours, a 99.6% reduction from the conventional 25-hour flow-analysis route, and can be run directly by crash CAE engineers without molding-specialist support. As evidence of practical impact, component-level crash analysis using the predicted orientation field reduced the absolute peak-force error from 19.0% with an isotropic baseline to 11.4%, while confining predicted fracture to the test-observed region. The deployment lesson is that a surrogate model alone is not enough for practical adoption: gate-setting support, automatic data conversion, and shareable reporting are what make the model usable as a design tool.

Alloying effects on the interface cohesion of grain boundaries in the Ni--Fe--Cr system

Aljoscha Felix Baumann, Reyhaneh Ghassemizadeh, Daniel F. Urban

Grain boundaries (GBs) in polycrystalline Ni-based alloys strongly influence mechanical strength and fracture behavior, yet the effect of alloying elements such as Fe and Cr on GB cohesion remains difficult to quantify systematically. Using density functional theory (DFT), we compute rigid-body separation (RBS) curves for six symmetric-tilt Ni GBs, extracting the work of separation $W_{\mathrm{sep}}$ and maximum traction $σ_{\max}$ via the universal binding energy relation (UBER). We find that substitution of Cr or Fe into the GB plane increases both quantities relative to pure Ni GBs. To overcome the size limitations of DFT, we compare several machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP) foundation models against the DFT dataset and identify a model achieving quantitative agreement with DFT for $W_{\mathrm{sep}}$ and $σ_{\max}$. This MLIP enables systematic sampling of alloying configurations at finer concentration steps. Extending the analysis to a ternary $\mathrm{Ni}_{56}\mathrm{Fe}_{20}\mathrm{Cr}_{24}$ composition, inspired by the principal constituents of the widely used commercial Alloy 718, shows that the binary trends provide a useful basis for interpreting the combined effect of both substitutes, while explicit ternary calculations are required to capture their interplay quantitatively. Together, these results provide a validated workflow for quantifying the effect of alloying elements on GB cohesion in multicomponent alloys. The resulting values may serve as input for continuum-scale fracture models, paving the way toward atomistically informed multiscale modeling.

Axion: A differentiable, GPU-native finite element framework,demonstrated on phase-field fracture

Wei Wang, Meenu Krishnan U., Somdatta Goswami

We introduce Axion (Autodifferentiable, XLA-compiled, Implicit, On-device Numerics), a differentiable, GPU-native finite element framework written in JAX. The element kernels, Newton and Krylov iterations, bound-constrained updates, staggered coupling of fields, and the loop over load increments are expressed as a single program that is just-in-time compiled and executed on the GPU without host intervention. Consistent tangent operators are obtained by forward-mode automatic differentiation of the element residual, and derivatives of the discrete solution with respect to model parameters are obtained by differentiating the same program. Global operators are applied element by element without assembling a sparse matrix, octree mesh refinement is performed within fixed-size arrays, and a cell-based domain decomposition runs the same program on several GPUs. Phase-field fracture is implemented as the first module because it combines fine mesh resolution, bound-constrained damage, and repeated nonlinear solves over a loading history, which together make it computationally expensive. On two- and three-dimensional single-edge-notched tension benchmarks, a uniformly refined Axion computation completes faster than the corresponding adaptive FEniCSx computation. At identical discretization, the two-dimensional benchmark runs 37.9 times faster on one GPU than a FEniCSx implementation on one CPU. Strong and weak scaling efficiencies on four GPUs are 92% and 96%, respectively. Large-scale demonstrations include hydraulic fracture in a poroelastic medium (0.38M degrees of freedom), fracture of a multi-material premolar under contact loading (0.69M degrees of freedom), and a concrete gravity dam (96M degrees of freedom) on a single GPU. The implementation is available as open-source software.

The initial slope preserves the averaged tensile strain-energy density to leading order. The first variation yields a constitutive stress in which the tensile contribution is multiplied by the adjoint average of the derivative of the saturation potential. The resulting degradation is therefore determined by the stored-energy functional, without introducing an independent fracture field or prescribing a separate degradation law. For fixed smooth displacement fields with bounded strain, the energy density converges to the classical elastic strain-energy density as the averaging radius tends to zero. The Griffith surface-energy coefficient is recovered for planar opening transitions. A structure-preserving scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) scheme satisfies an exact discrete balance between the modified-energy increment and external work. We compare SAV and velocity-Verlet solutions for dynamic crack branching and Kalthoff--Winkler impact.

The heterogeneous AFEMAG framework thus provides an efficient and practical platform for micromagnetic simulations in the design of magnetic storage materials.
