Thermodynamically consistent neural constitutive models for elastoplasticity via eigen-representations

Harikrishnan Vijayakumaran, Shunyu Yin, Martin Abendroth, Miguel A. Bessa, Karl A. Kalina

Neural network material models can represent yield surfaces and hardening laws with far more flexibility than classical closed-form plasticity formulations, but training such a model end-to-end through an implicit return map introduces non-trivial challenges. This work presents a thermodynamically consistent neural constitutive model for rate-independent, isotropic elastoplasticity that jointly learns a flexible yield function together with its evolution laws. For non-associative flow, the yield function extends the convex flow potential by a jointly trained additive term, which renders the model thermodynamically consistent by construction. Isotropy and convexity are enforced simultaneously across all learnable components through a permutation-invariant, spectral (eigenvalue-based) representation. Training proceeds through a backward-Euler return map, with gradients propagated by the implicit function theorem together with an eigenvalue-degeneracy-aware scheme for safe differentiation. The robustness and generality of the approach are demonstrated by reproducing known constitutive models, including non-associative flow, learning material behavior from stress--strain data extracted from a full-field finite element simulation, and capturing unknown non-associative behavior from homogenized representative volume elements of a foam. Finally, the proposed neural plasticity model is evaluated within finite element simulations to solve boundary-value-problem under plane stress.

Dynamic Prandtl-Reuss Thermoplasticity with Degenerating Yield Constraints

Sebastian Owczarek

We study a three-dimensional dynamic thermoplastic system with a temperature-dependent convex yield set whose deviatoric section may collapse completely at a finite temperature. The underlying constitutive model is rate-independent Prandtl-Reuss perfect plasticity, without Kelvin-Voigt viscosity, hardening, or a safe-load condition. We construct global approximation-generated relaxed entropy-energy states by a one-parameter Temam-Norton-Hoff-Galerkin scheme with an undiscretized heat equation and untruncated plastic heat production. The logarithmic temperature is strongly compact in $L^2(0,T;L^q(Ω))$ for every $q<6$ and in $L^p((0,T)\timesΩ)$ for every $p<8/3$. An entropy-weighted work estimate yields convergence of the overstress at rate $O(\sqrt{\log n/n})$ in every fixed finite Lebesgue space and identifies stress admissibility. The constitutive limit comprises a distributional plastic balance, finite support-weighted measures, and compact-fibre lifts carrying exact normality and maximum plastic work. A locally finite lift on the positive-radius fibres unifies the level-restricted limits at every continuity level. At the macroscopic level, however, the available compactness does not identify a canonical stress-plastic-flow pairing. Moreover, an explicit smooth state satisfying the intrinsic relaxed balance conditions shows that these conditions do not imply macroscopic Prandtl-Reuss normality. In the fully collapsed regime, an energy-entropy rigidity argument determines the evolution and yields approximation-generated examples in which the plastic flow need not be a finite measure or the displacement need not retain its full boundary trace. Thermal concentration and plastic entropy production remain explicit throughout.

A scaling law for numerical computation of fracture stress intensity factors

Ronghao Cui, Jan Martin Nordbotten, Ivar Stefansson, Inga Berre

Numerical simulations of fracture propagation require accurate computation of stress intensity factors (SIFs) to determine whether a pre-existing fracture will propagate. Even though displacement solutions from well-established numerical methods generally converge with mesh refinement, the convergence behavior of SIF estimates remains less understood. In this work, we propose a scaling law to improve the performance of SIF extraction using two-point displacement correlation methods. This scaling law relates sampling distance from the fracture tip to mesh resolution. It is developed by balancing the asymptotic truncation error against the numerical discretization error. Three numerical discretization methods--standard quadratic finite elements, quadratic finite elements with quarter-point elements, and a multi-point stress approximation finite volume method--are employed to validate the proposed scaling law on fracture benchmark problems under pure-mode and mixed-mode loading. We find that the conventional nearest-tip sampling strategy fails to compute convergent SIF estimates. In contrast, the proposed scaling-based approach yields decreasing errors in the SIF estimates as the mesh is refined. Across all three discretization methods, the proposed scaling law captures the sampling locations required for convergent SIF extraction, suggesting a general numerical characteristic of near-tip SIF extraction.

Operator Learning of Microstructure-Dependent Internal-Variable Models for Inelastic Homogenization: Approximation and Complexity

Lianghao Cao, George Stepaniants, Andrew M. Stuart

We study the learning of homogenized constitutive laws for inelastic heterogeneous materials, aiming to replace costly concurrent unit-cell simulations with surrogates that generalize across loading histories and microstructures. We focus on the recurrent neural operator framework, motivated by the explicit form of homogenized Kelvin--Voigt linear viscoelasticity. In this framework, internal variables enable a Markovian approximation of loading-history dependence, and neural operators represent the microstructure-dependent constitutive response. For periodic Kelvin--Voigt viscoelasticity, we prove a universal approximation theorem showing that the model can approximate the homogenized constitutive law to any prescribed accuracy, uniformly in time and over a wide set of admissible strain trajectories and microstructural property fields, in two and three dimensions. This comprises a significant extension of the existing, somewhat limited, one-dimensional theory that uses explicit solutions for piecewise constant materials. Instead, in two and three dimensions, we adopt a more versatile approximation based on the spectral representation of the memory kernel that arises from homogenization. This also enables us to give an explicit bound on the internal-variable dimension, sufficient for approximation, exhibiting logarithmic growth in the inverse relative stress error and in material contrast. Numerical experiments on viscoelasticity and crystal plasticity complement the theory, illustrating the roles of internal-variable dimension and sample complexity in learning homogenized responses from unit-cell data. Theoretical results are formally verified in Lean 4.

Marius Nevland, Kundan Kumar, Inga Berre, Jakub Wiktor Both, Eirik Keilegavlen

This paper studies the numerical approximation of a mathematical model of coupled flow and viscoelastic deformation in a fractured porous medium including fracture contact mechanics. We consider a mixed-dimensional model, where the fracture is modeled as a co-dimension one object within the porous medium. This model couples Darcy flow in the matrix and fracture, linear viscoelasticity of the matrix and frictional contact mechanics of the fracture surfaces; the contact conditions include normal compliance and a Coulomb friction law. A numerical experiment is performed in which numerical error estimates are computed and compared to the theoretical results.

SiC-BN composites with extreme thermal and environmental stability

Abhijit Biswas, Eugenio Garcia-Granados, Saman Zare, Mozhdeh Mirakhory, JungHun Park, Shuvodeep De

Ceramic materials that maintain structural integrity and resist thermal-shock under extreme conditions are critical for many applications. Here we show that spark plasma sintered composites containing equal weight fractions of hexagonal silicon carbide (6H-SiC) and boron nitride (h-BN) exhibit exceptional stability under extreme high-temperature oxidizing conditions and unusually stable temperature-independent thermal conductivity across a broad temperature range. Under high-velocity oxygen-fuel torch exposure in air, it withstands prolonged heating and thermal-cycling at surface temperatures reaching >1500 °C, whereas pure 6H-SiC and h-BN rapidly fractures or erodes. This lightweight, easily machinable ceramic composite will be a promising structural material in extreme environment applications.

Characterization of tissue mechanical properties is achieved by imaging the response of the tissue to an induced stress or excitation. A plethora of approaches have been used in the scientific community to induce excitation in tissue, which usually require additional tools or devices than are costly. Physics-constrained differentiable causal discovery of constitutive mechanisms through boundary value problems

Michael A. Kraus

Data-driven constitutive model discovery is recast as causal discovery of mechanism parent sets using time-unrolled structural causal models. Hard-coding balance laws and constitutive skeletons, candidate graphs score environment--mechanism and state--state edges via weak-form equilibrium gaps evaluated from full-field displacements, avoiding the need to measure stresses or internal variables. An adaptive lasso and a NOTEARS-type constraint ensure graph acyclicity and an explicit evaluation order, treating climate-chamber protocols as interventions. Automatic differentiation yields the score's Gauss--Newton matrix as a local identifiability certificate, driving greedy D-optimal test design. In a synthetic study on a hygro-thermo-viscoelastic damaging plate (8 true edges among 13 candidates), interventional data recover the exact true graph in 7 of 10 runs (0.01px displacement noise) with zero attribution error. Conversely, observational data with a temperature--moisture correlation of $-0.998$ fail entirely because confounded scores are 250--380 times flatter. Observationally discovered models reproduce training climates (3.6% error) but mispredict hot and humid climates by up to 41.5% (16.9% average), whereas interventionally discovered models stay within 3.2%. A correlation sweep shows the certificate successfully predicts attribution errors, though weak-form score noise bias remains a primary limitation. Computed without knowledge of the truth, the D-optimal design selects test sets ranked second or third out of 126 candidates.

Mechanical behaviour of the intact brain-skull interface under tension, compression and shear

Sajjad Arzemanzadeh, Karol Miller, Ron Kikinis, Adam Wittek

The brain-skull interface governs the boundary/contact conditions of the brain in computational head models, yet its mechanical behaviour remains poorly characterised experimentally. We characterised the brain-skull interface by testing brain-skull complex samples (skull bone, meninges and brain tissue in their native arrangement) excised from 19 sheep cadaver heads under uniaxial tension and compression at strain rates of 0.002, 0.02 and 0.2 s$^{-1}$ and simple shear at 0.02 s$^{-1}$. Histology confirmed that the meningeal layers remained intact and attached to the brain and skull after excision. We acquired magnetic resonance images of every sample, reconstructed their geometry and developed a sample-specific finite element model of each experiment. We represented the brain tissue using a second-order Ogden hyperelastic model and the brain-skull interface using a cohesive zone model. Using inverse finite element analysis, we first calibrated the brain tissue parameters for each subject. Keeping these parameters fixed, we then identified the interface parameters from the corresponding brain-skull complex specimen. The brain tissue shear modulus increased with strain rate from 0.94 $\pm$ 0.16 kPa at 0.002 s$^{-1}$ to 1.60 $\pm$ 0.30 kPa at 0.2 s$^{-1}$. In contrast, the interface properties (maximum normal traction 2.35 $\pm$ 0.81 kPa, maximum tangential traction 4.14 $\pm$ 0.97 kPa, and fracture energy 0.35 $\pm$ 0.14 N m$^{-1}$) showed no significant dependence on strain rate or loading mode. In all samples, failure occurred within the subarachnoid space. Neither a rigid brain-skull connection nor frictionless sliding contact with separation reproduced the measured mechanical response or failure mode of the brain-skull interface. Our experimentally derived cohesive zone model provides a realistic representation of the brain-skull interface for computational head models.

When Does the Real Wiring Win? Testing connectome structure-function claims against unmeasured physiology

Yeyuan Liu

Connectome-constrained models are often used to argue that a measured wiring diagram outperforms shuffled or rewired wiring, and hence that structure shapes function. Such comparisons can confound wiring with how the models are tuned: a connectome fixes who connects to whom, not the excitability, gains or plasticity rates the model also needs, and modellers often tune these until the real-wiring model works. We separate three estimands: physiology tuned on the real wiring and transferred to nulls (P1), each wiring tuned on its own (P2), and P2 restricted to physiology consistent with independent data (P3). The biases involved are known in machine learning (under-tuned baselines, inherited weights, winner's curse); we measure what they do to biological conclusions, using synthetic ground truth, placebo nulls and three Drosophila circuit models. Some claims survive every protocol we test: segregated reward and punishment dopamine improves learning, and head-direction topography is required for a persistent bump. Others do not. In the mushroom body, the real Kenyon-cell output wiring beats a cross-subtype identity shuffle by 0.09 under P1 and P2 but loses to it by 0.09 under P3 (95% CIs exclude zero; held-out runs confirm). The verdict is set by the share of alpha'beta' cells among active Kenyon cells, which P1 selects low; at the share implied by imaging (about 44%) the real-wiring model fails to learn, exposing a model inadequacy rather than a deficit of the fly's wiring. In flyvis, P1 penalises a mirrored connectome, which has a constructed solution within 0.07 (median) of the real error, more than a spatially shuffled one in 50 of 50 members; the ranking survives ten-fold longer retraining but depends on the checkpoint at fifty-fold. A credible structural claim needs matched optimisation, data-constrained physiology and a model that still works there.

A pseudostress-based fully mixed finite element method for the stationary Biot--Poisson--Nernst--Planck system

Cristian Inzunza

We develop a pseudostress-based fully mixed finite element method for the stationary Biot-Poisson-Nernst-Planck system describing ionic transport in a charged, saturated, and deformable porous medium. The mechanical equations are reformulated in terms of a non-symmetric pseudostress and the solid displacement, while mixed formulations are retained for the Darcy, electrostatic, and ionic equations. The nonlinear electrostatic and advective couplings are treated in a Banach space setting. The formulation provides direct approximations of the pseudostress, fluxes, and concentration gradients, and permits algebraic reconstruction of the displacement gradient and Cauchy stress. Under block-stability and small-data assumptions, we establish continuous and discrete well-posedness, with poroelastic stability bounds uniform with respect to the first Lame parameter. A conforming Raviart-Thomas discretization, completed by discontinuous polynomials, yields a priori error estimates and convergence rates. For the coupled scheme in two dimensions, we construct a residual-based a posteriori error estimator and establish its reliability under an additional smallness condition on the prescribed data. Local efficiency is proved for polynomial boundary data, with boundary-data oscillation included for general traces. We also describe the estimator modifications for nonhomogeneous displacement and normal-flux data under corresponding stability and solvability hypotheses. Numerical experiments examine convergence of the mixed and reconstructed fields, robustness in the nearly incompressible regime, adaptive refinement, and ionic polarization in a charged porous medium.

This ensures that the basis functions are fully reusable across different global magnetic configurations, significantly reducing the online computational overhead for large-scale plasma simulations.

Accelerating matrix-free fast diagonalization and preconditioned linear solvers for high-order finite element discretized elliptic problems on distributed GPUs

The time to solution of an iterative solver depends on the number of iterations and the cost per iteration. This paper develops algorithms that reduce both for elliptic problems discretized with high-order continuous finite elements on distributed GPUs, implemented in a unified matrix-free code supporting CUDA, HIP and SYCL. A distributed direct fast-diagonalization (FD) Poisson solver provides exact solutions on tensor-product meshes with direction-wise grading and coefficients varying along one coordinate direction. Its tensor data remain distributed, moved between slab layouts using collective transposes or kept in place by a Schur-complement approach.
