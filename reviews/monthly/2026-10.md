## Major Themes

- Physical admissibility by construction: convexity, symmetric polyconvexity, frame-indifference, and constrained softening functions increasingly shape neural constitutive architectures.
- Mechanics-aware learning for fracture and plasticity: deep energy formulations incorporate incremental plasticity, evolving toughness, thermal coupling, and crack-specific representations.
- Full-field surrogates for heterogeneous solids: neural networks predict spatially and temporally resolved responses rather than only scalar effective properties.
- Generative design with deterministic refinement: diffusion and rectified-flow models propose geometries that are subsequently optimized using finite-element analysis or homogenization sensitivities.
- Reduced computational cost without discarding mechanics: adaptive hyperreduction, localized approximation, and hybrid finite-element–random-feature methods complement neural surrogates.
- Data efficiency and retained fidelity: active learning, limited experimental validation, and retention-constrained fine-tuning target expensive labels and out-of-distribution material states.
- Uncertainty across scales: admissible constitutive sets, stochastic interfaces, random-field reliability, and generated microstructure ensembles broaden attention beyond single deterministic predictions.

## Field Progress

- Neural constitutive modeling is increasingly concerned with admissibility and expressivity together. The symmetric-polyconvex and constrained-Mullins studies address restrictions needed for mechanical consistency rather than relying solely on training-data fit.
- AI-based mechanics solvers are incorporating more demanding physics, including history-dependent plasticity and coupled fracture processes. The supplied work supports richer formulations, but does not yet establish general replacement of mature finite-element workflows.
- Generative design is becoming more tightly integrated with verification and refinement. Scaffold optimization and casting-aware shape generation demonstrate complementary ways to connect learned proposals to mechanical targets and geometric feasibility.
- Computational acceleration spans learned surrogates and structure-preserving reduction. Adaptive hyperreduction is particularly relevant when constitutive parameters change during identification, making a fixed reduced model potentially unreliable.
- Evaluation is beginning to separate global quantities from local mechanisms. The slope study's accurate safety factors alongside under-resolved localization show why design-level agreement alone is insufficient evidence of field-level fidelity.

## Open Questions

- How can neural constitutive models combine sufficient expressivity with admissibility guarantees under finite deformation, cyclic loading, anisotropy, and irreversible damage?
- Which verification procedures can detect inaccurate localization, crack paths, or peak stresses when aggregate quantities such as compliance or factor of safety appear accurate?
- How reliably do full-field surrogates transfer across geometries, meshes, material distributions, and loading regimes beyond those represented in simulation-generated training data?
- Can generative microstructure ensembles reproduce experimentally observed variability and rare failure-relevant features, rather than only plausible appearance and selected summary statistics?
- When do adaptive hyperreduction, physics-informed learning, and direct finite-element solves offer the best total cost once training, model updates, uncertainty estimation, and verification are included?
- How can active learning improve defect and failure predictions while demonstrably retaining equilibrium properties and avoiding new errors elsewhere in configuration space?

## Outlook

The most promising near-term direction is hybrid: use learning to represent admissible material behavior, propose feasible designs, or accelerate repeated predictions, while retaining variational mechanics, finite-element refinement, and independent validation where they are strongest. Future assessments should emphasize end-to-end computational cost, out-of-distribution tests, localization accuracy, and uncertainty calibration rather than isolated prediction errors or inference speed. The supplied papers suggest useful progress toward this integration, but truncated abstracts and application-specific evidence prevent firm conclusions about broad robustness or industrial readiness.
